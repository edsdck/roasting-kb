# Findings from my roasts

*Your own roasts, grade **P**: the best evidence for this machine, but one roaster, uncontrolled, and only five roasts cupped so far. 45 roasts, Jan–Nov 2026. Updated 2026-11-20 (Build 13). The full evidence and sources are in [Findings — details](findings-detail.md). Terms (BT, RoR, DTR, 1C): [Glossary](../kb/glossary.md).*

## Where things stand

!!! abstract "In short"
    - **Bitterness and an unnamed "infamous" flavour show up in every cup** (five roasts, two coffees, 1–41 days of rest). It is mildest in id43.
    - **You have ruled out** your brewing (store-bought coffee tastes right on the same setup; a coarser grind only added acidity), storage, cooling and charred beans. **Still open:** the roaster itself (never cleaned), the profile, and the green coffee.
    - **The longer roast tasted better both times so far** (id41 → id42, id39 → id43). That is weak evidence: the tastings were not blind and other things changed too.
    - **Test in progress:** id44 (1:30) vs id45 (2:14), same coffee, roasted 2026-11-20. Taste both at 10–14 days (about 2026-11-30 to 12-04).
    - **Your plan is repeatable:** id44 reproduced id42's roast (drop +12 °C above first crack in both, RoR 10.4 vs 10.1 °C/min at first crack).

## Cupped roasts

*Rest = days from roast to the tasting. "Above 1C" = drop temperature minus the first-crack reading. Your words, shortened.*

| Roast | Coffee | Development · above 1C | Tasted | What you wrote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| id39 | Costa Rica natural | 0:41 · +6.2 °C | day 5, 41 | bitter; very acidic when cooled; no clear flavour |
| id40 | Costa Rica natural (same) | 1:12 · +12.0 °C | day 34–35 | bitter on 11-19, little on 11-20; a coarser grind only made it more acidic |
| id41 | Colombia (same lot as id42, 44, 45) | 1:01 · +9.8 °C | day 13 | flat or baked; a bit of smoke; bitter deep in the mouth |
| id42 | Colombia | 1:30 · +12.0 °C | day 1–9 | tea-like body, sweet, weaker bitterness; by day 9 almost no acidity and "smoky or ashy" |
| id43 | Costa Rica Sarchimor natural | 1:30 · +10.4 °C | day 3–11 | fruity, balanced acidity, tastier on a V60 on day 10; "infamous" weak and fading |

**What the cups say (n = 5, uncontrolled):**

- **Bitterness is not about development length.** It appears at 0:41 and at 1:30, at +6 and +12 °C, in a natural and a washed coffee.
- **The shorter roast lost both times,** with Colombia at 1:01 vs 1:30 and Costa Rica at 0:41 vs 1:30. It is a lead; the test below checks it.
- **"Smoky" and "ashy" keep coming back** (id41, id42). That points at char or smoke more than at under- or over-development. **Inference — test it.**
- **Rest changes the cup,** in different directions: id42 calmed down over days 1–4 and lost acidity by day 9, while id43 got fruitier between day 3 and day 10.

## Test in progress: id44 vs id45

Same coffee, same day, same temperature-triggered plan until first crack. Not cupped yet.

| | id44 | id45 |
|---|---|---|
| Development (DTR) | 1:30 (15.1 %) | 2:14 (20.0 %) |
| Drop · above first crack | 193.8 °C · +12.1 °C | 200.5 °C · +17.4 °C |
| Weight loss | 13.7 % | 15.1 % |
| First crack | 8:25 | 8:54 |
| RoR at first crack / at drop | 10.4 / 5.3 °C/min | 10.0 / 5.7 °C/min |

- **Caveat:** the pair differs in roast degree as well as development (1.4 points of weight loss, 6.7 °C of drop), and id45 reached first crack 29 s later. A darker cup is not proof that development alone did it.
- **Taste for:** sweetness, bitterness, body, how each cup behaves as it cools, and whether the "infamous" flavour is stronger in either.

## What would make these findings stronger

- Cup notes for every roast, with the rest day and the brew written down.
- The roasted weight every time (14 roasts have none), and the coffee labelled in Artisan.
- Probe depth and how you marked first crack (first pop or regular pops), since both shift readings [S0045](../sources/S0045-home-barista-quest-m6-first-crack-temperature-probe-depth.md).
- Green moisture or density, so weight loss can be read as a degree [S0030](../sources/S0030-royal-coffee-understanding-roast-yield-and-loss.md).
