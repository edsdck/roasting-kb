# S0036 — Hacea Roast Approach tasting kit (Home-Barista thread)

**Author:** many members · **Type:** forum thread (70 posts over 7 pages, 2025-12-12 to 2026-02-12; all pages read) · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/do-it-now-timing-hacea-roast-roast-approach-experience-with-three-ethiopian-coffees-t102133.html)

## Summary
Hacea Coffee sold a kit of 9 × 30 g roasted samples: three roast profiles of each of three Ethiopian coffees (washed, natural, black honey) from the same washing station. The profiles differ in how many seconds are spent in drying, Maillard, caramelization and post-crack development, with first crack marked at a fixed temperature. Members cupped the samples, then tried to reproduce the profiles on other machines. The thread gives cup preferences across profiles (washed best with the quicker profiles, natural best with the longest), a method for translating the profiles to another roaster, and practical views on rest time. Opinions are not unanimous and the panel is informal (a handful of home roasters).

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The kit's profiles are defined by seconds spent between four bean-temperature marks, and first crack is not taken from the sound but fixed at one temperature to make roasts comparable.
   > "charge - 305 degrees (drying), 305-350 degrees (Maillard), 350-385 degrees (caramelization), and 385 - End (Post crack development)" — *post #20, emilymakescoffee*
   > "I have marked all first cracks at 385 to create a standard that is more objective." — *post #20*
2. **C2** — Emily's reason for ignoring the audible first crack: it varies with bean irregularity and with how well the roaster lets you hear it.
   > "I have always been a bit irked by using the audible first crack as a major indicator on roasts" — *post #20*
3. **C3** — The kit is 9 samples of 30 g: three profiles of each of washed, natural and black honey; the profiles were developed on a 1.5 kg drum.
   > "9 x 30g roasted samples (3 unique roast profiles of each washed, natural and black honey processed coffees from the Adorsi washing station in Ethiopia)." — *post #1*
   > "I developed the profiles on the 1.5kg, so that's what I'll be roasting them all on." — *post #7*
4. **C4** — EddyQ's cup result (pour-over/cupping): washed coffee best with the quicker profiles 1 and 2; the natural gained nothing from profile 1's higher acidity and was best with profile 3 (stone fruit dominant).
   > "The washed beans tasted best (to me) with the quicker profiles 1 and 2." — *post #53, EddyQ*
   > "actually tasted best (to me) with profile 3 where stone fruits were dominant" — *post #53*
5. **C5** — The same taster's espresso: after dialling in, he preferred the higher peak acidity of roasts 1 and 2 for the washed coffee, having first found all three too sour.
   > "I preferred the higher peak acidity in roasts 1 & 2 with this coffee." — *post #61, EddyQ*
   > "1 & 2 were far too acidic/sour and 3 was still a bit sour, but much more rounded." — *post #58, EddyQ (before dialling in)*
6. **C6** — No consensus across tasters: SAS liked washed #1 and black honey #3 and later preferred his own first roasts; GDM528 reports roast 3 as the group favourite; CoffeeBeGoode (new to cupping) saw no pattern in acidity.
   > "I liked Hacea #1 Washed, Lemony and balanced. Hacea #3 Black Honey, sweet and complex flavorful." — *post #40, SAS*
   > "Roast 3 was the favorite among the tasters here" — *post #66, GDM528*
   > "I found no correlation in acidity between the roast profiles, or between the coffee processing profiles." — *post #55, CoffeeBeGoode*
7. **C7** — Each profile has a purpose, according to the designer: she uses all three in production for different reasons.
   > "each roast profile was developed for a specific purpose." — *post #24, emilymakescoffee*
8. **C8** — To compare your own roast, line up seconds in each phase; if your machine's first crack is at another temperature, rescale the phase boundaries (one member shows the proportional method).
   > "If you don't have FCs on your machine at 385 folks (mine is 355-365) then adjust as needed." — *post #21, CarefreeBuzzBuzz*
   > "my FCs was 360-361" — *post #48, CarefreeBuzzBuzz (after test roasts of these three coffees)*
9. **C9** — EddyQ reproduced the profiles on a Quest M3s with 91 g batches; his first crack read 10 degrees higher than Emily's, so his roasts were slightly less developed; speed needed high heat a minute before charging.
   > "my first crack temperature is 10deg higher than Emily's" — *post #63, EddyQ*
   > "you must apply high heat a minute before dosing beans" — *post #69, EddyQ*
10. **C10** — GDM528 matched Roast 3 on an IKAWA Home; the inlet-temperature set-points sit about 50 °C (90 °F) above Hacea's bean readings. Natural-processed greens release their moisture-rate peak about 30 s later than washed; honey behaves like washed.
    > "The temperature offset between Hacea's BT readings and the IKAWA setpoints is around 50C/90F" — *post #62, GDM528*
    > "natural-processed greens show a less pronounced spike in the moisture rate of release and tend to peak about 30 seconds later than washed" — *post #60, GDM528*
11. **C11** — Rest-time views in the thread: week-plus for these samples, up to a month or more for ultra-light roasts; Emily does not think the difference matters much for this exercise.
    > "I recommend that you give it at least a week, for the beans to degas." — *post #33, flamenkito*
    > "My very light roasts tend to be best 4-6 weeks in." — *post #36, SutterMill*
    > "need a LONG time to rest. Like a month or more." — *post #35, flamenkito (about Nordic roasts dropped less than 30 s into first crack)*
    > "I honestly wouldn't worry too much about the rest time." — *post #20, emilymakescoffee*
12. **C12** — One member prefers a much darker roast for milk drinks: 25.3 degrees past first crack was liked more than lighter versions.
    > "at 25.3° past FCs. The lighter roasts just weren't doing it for us." — *post #65, CarefreeBuzzBuzz*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Useful as a worked example of cupping the same coffee across profiles that differ in time per phase, and of translating profiles between very different machines (drum, air-fluid-bed, small electric drum). The profile times were made on a 1.5 kg gas drum and numbers do not transfer directly to a 350 g IR roaster; the fixed-temperature first-crack convention and the rescale method may. The thread does not give cup results below about a minute of post-crack development.

## Gatherer notes
- Profile charts and the Hacea roast-comparison card are images; per-phase seconds were not extractable. Only text-stated numbers are recorded.
- Temperature units are mostly not written in the posts; the 385 mark is consistent with °F (it equals about 196 °C), which is a Gatherer inference.
- The cup comparisons are small, informal and not blind; several posters say they would retaste after more rest.
- Companion thread: S0038 (Hacea's roast philosophy), where Emily explains the 385 convention.
