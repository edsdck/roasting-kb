# S0035 — Stretchin' Out the Roast pt. 3 (Sweet Maria's Coffee Library)

**Author:** Sweet Maria's staff (unnamed) · **Type:** article with roast logs and panel cupping · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** electric drum sample roaster · [Original](https://library.sweetmarias.com/stretchin-out-the-roast-pt-3/)

## Summary
Third experiment of the series, on a Rwanda Bourbon. The initial drying stage (before any yellowing) is stretched or shortened so the total roast differs by about 30 seconds either way, while first-crack length and the 30-second finish after the end of crack stay about the same. The control roast was the most common panel favourite, the long roast had a muted but balanced acidity and long, syrupy, candy-like sweetness, and the short roast was tart with a short finish. Pour-over drinkers preferred the shorter roasts across the whole series. The author reports the long result was similar to Part 1 except that this time the long roast kept a pleasant sweetness instead of turning flat and cocoa-bitter.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The variable here is the early drying time before yellowing; Maillard browning has not started yet.
   > "I stretched the initial drying time, before any yellowing had occurred." — *experiment description*
2. **C2** — The three roasts differ in total time by about 30 s each way; first-crack length and the finish after crack are about the same.
   > "the variance between roasts is approximately 30 seconds" — *after the roast logs*
3. **C3** — The long roast: longer finish, muted but balanced acidity, candy-like, syrupy sweetness through the finish, muted aroma.
   > "more candy sweet, long sweetness through palate and finish, syrupy" — *cupping notes, Roast 2*
4. **C4** — The short roast: forward, tart acidity and a short finish.
   > "tart, more forward acidity" — *cupping notes, Roast 3*
5. **C5** — The control (middle) roast was the most common favourite; most tasters noticed the long roast's longer finish and sweetness.
   > "Roast 1 was the most common favorite" — *panel paragraph*
6. **C6** — Across the series, people who brew by manual pour-over preferred the shorter roasts; in auto-drip and press pot the short roasts' dry finish and aggressive acidity became too much.
   > "the people who prefered manual pour-over brewing methods prefered the shorter roasts in each experiment." — *panel paragraph*
7. **C7** — Longer roasts reduced acidity, as in Part 1, "in a way similar to cocoa roasting".
   > "the longer roasts reduced acid, in a way similar to cocoa roasting." — *conclusions*
8. **C8** — Difference from Part 1 (as summarized here): stretching the drying stage let acidity break down without prolonged caramelization, so a pleasant sweetness remained; in Part 1 the long roast had gone flat with cocoa bitterness.
   > "by stretching out the drying stage, acidity breaks down with no prolonged caramelization" — *conclusions*
   > "the long roast was pretty flat and the sweetness had begun to take on some cocoa bitterness due to prolonged caramelization." — *about Part 1, second-hand*
9. **C9** — Series summary by the author: time after first crack alters perceived acidity (Part 1), the first crack itself alters body and mouthfeel (Part 2), drying alters acidity and sweetness (Part 3); whether stretching early browning differs from stretching drying is left open.
   > "alter the perceived acidity as well as sweetness by stretching out the drying stage of the roast (Part 3)" — *The Upshot*
