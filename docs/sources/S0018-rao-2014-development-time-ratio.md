# S0018 — Rao (2014): Development time ratio as a prerequisite

**Author:** Scott Rao · **Type:** article (Daily Coffee News) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2014/08/12/roast-development-time-and-the-prerequisite-for-any-successful-roast)

## Summary
The short column that introduced the "development time ratio" (DTR). From a decade of consulting
and cupping thousands of batches from more than two dozen machines, Rao reports that in all the
best batches first crack began at 75–80 % of total roast time, i.e. development was 20–25 % of
the roast. He argues development time only makes sense relative to total roast time, that a DTR of
20–25 % is a prerequisite (not a guarantee) for a successful roast, and that changing development
time without regard to total time tends to create baked flavours and lose sweetness. Evidence is
his own experience; no data are shown.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — In all of the most memorable batches Rao cupped, first crack began at 75–80 % of total roast time, i.e. development was 20–25 % of the roast.
   > "One was that first crack* began at between 75%–80% in all the great batches. Put another way, “development time” was between 20%–25% of total roast time." — *para. 1*
2. **C2** — He had not, in six years, tasted a sweet, well-developed coffee whose first crack began outside that range, across all roast degrees and roast times he had experienced — but he rarely cups roasts dropped well before the end of first crack or after second crack starts, so he does not claim the ratio for those.
   > "This ideal “development-time ratio” (DTR) has been valid for all roast degrees and roast times I’ve experienced." — *para. 2*
   > "I don’t often cup roasts dropped well before the end of first crack or well after the onset of second crack, so I won’t assume the ratio is valid for those roast levels." — *para. 2*
3. **C3** — Development time quoted on its own (e.g. "three minutes") is close to meaningless without the total roast time; track DTR instead.
   > "To me, that piece of roast data is relatively meaningless out of the context of the total roast time." — *para. 3*
   > "I recommend roasters focus on the DTR rather than nominal development time." — *para. 4*
4. **C4** — Manipulating development time without regard to total roast time often produces baked flavours and destroys sweetness.
   > "When roasters manipulate development time without considering it in the context of the total roast time, they often create baked flavors and destroy sweetness." — *para. 4*
5. **C5** — A 20–25 % DTR is a prerequisite rather than a guarantee; it is a marker of balance (a roast that starts very fast and ends slowly, or vice versa, falls outside the range).
   > "While achieving a 20%–25% DTR doesn’t guarantee a brilliant roast, it seems to be a prerequisite for a successful roast." — *para. 5*
   > "if a roast begins very fast and ends slowly or vice versa, the DTR will be outside of the 20%–25% range." — *para. 5*
6. **C6** — Rao says roasters who claimed success outside the range had not verified development by taste and objectively (e.g. refractometer); in his own sample, checked by smell, taste and refractometer, every successful roast was within it.
   > "in my sample of thousands of batches tested for development by smell, taste, and refractometer, every successful roast’s DTR was within that range." — *para. 5*
7. **C7** — Rao marks the start of first crack when he hears more than one or two isolated cracks; other definitions may give different results.
   > "I mark the beginning of first crack as the moment I hear more than one or two isolated cracks. If you measure the beginning of first crack differently, your results may differ." — *footnote*

## Numbers & parameters
| Parameter | Value | Context (machine, batch, unit) | Claim |

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* This is the main practitioner rule the owner's roasts break: his DTR of
~7–12 % (~1:00 development) is well below Rao's 20–25 %. It is opinion from commercial gas/drum
roasting, verified by Rao's own cupping and refractometry, not a published experiment; the Alstrup
study (S0006) and Münchow et al. (S0012) are the controlled counterpoints. On a radiant machine
with FC around 180 °C and steady temperature after heater cut (S0015 C8, S0017 C12) the balance
Rao describes may differ — worth testing rather than assuming.
