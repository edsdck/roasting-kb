# S0059 — Hoos: weight-loss % as a roast-degree and consistency check

**Author:** Rob Hoos · **Type:** consultant blog article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://hoos.coffee/blog/why-take-weight-loss-percentage)

## Summary
Hoos argues that weighing the roasted batch every time is a free, useful measure. He gives personal bands for weight loss (under 11 % suspect under-development, 11-13 % light, 14-16 % medium, 17-18 % dark, 19-21 % extremely dark, over 22 % avoid), says a lower-moisture lot will lose less for the same roast, treats a repeat deviation of 0.5 % or more as a sign something is wrong, and shows how to back-calculate the green needed for an order. The moisture/CO2 background is quoted from Schenker's ETH Zurich thesis. The bands are one practitioner's opinion, and he says to take them "with a grain of salt".

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Formula: loss % = (beginning weight - final weight) / beginning weight x 100.
2. **C2** — Most of the weight lost is water, then carbon dioxide; specialty coffee starts at 8-12 % moisture and roasted coffee is under 3 %.
   > "the majority of the weight lost is water, followed by carbon dioxide, and then everything else." — *What is weight loss, and why does it matter?*
3. **C3** — Quoted from Schenker (citing Illy and Viani 1995): the degradation products are about 70 % water and 30 % carbon dioxide.
   > "70 % of the degradation products are water and 30 % carbon dioxide" — *quoted passage from Schenker*
4. **C4** — Under 11 % loss he suspects under-roasted / under-developed.
   > "I am generally skeptical of a roast when I am less than 11%." — *No. 1 Roast Degree*
5. **C5** — 11-13 % is light; 14-16 % medium; 17-18 % dark; 19-21 % extremely dark; over 22 % to be avoided.
   > "This is, almost without exception, light roast territory." — *No. 1 Roast Degree (11-13 %)*
6. **C6** — Lower green moisture gives lower loss for a matched roast: 10 % moisture and 12 % loss implies about 11.5 % loss at 9.5 % moisture.
7. **C7** — A light profile with a medium-or-darker weight loss suggests something is out of balance.
   > "there is likely something out-of-balance" — *No. 1 Roast Degree*
8. **C8** — Consistency: a loss 0.5 % or more away from the baseline for the same coffee and method means something is wrong, and such batches were also out of spec on colour in his QC.
   > "if a roast is ≧ 0.5% different from the baseline, then something is seriously wrong." — *No. 2 Consistency*
9. **C9** — Green needed = roasted needed / (1 - loss as a decimal).
   > "roasted needs / (1 - the weight loss % as a decimal) = green coffee needed to fulfill orders." — *No. 3 Calculating coffee needs*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* This corroborates the loss bands already in the base (S0030, S0032) and adds a consistency rule. On 350 g, 0.5 % is 1.75 g (derived), so the repeatability rule needs a scale accurate to about 1 g and a consistent cooling and weighing routine; whether it is realistic for a home roaster is an inference, not something the page tests. The bands are one consultant's view and are not tied to colour or cup data here.
