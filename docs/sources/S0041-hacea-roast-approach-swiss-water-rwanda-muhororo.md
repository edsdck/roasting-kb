# S0041 — Hacea Roast Approach: Swiss Water decaf Rwanda Muhororo (2026)

**Author:** Hacea Coffee Source · **Type:** blog article · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://haceacoffee.com/blogs/roasting/roast-approach-swiss-water-process-rwanda-muhororo-natural)

## Summary
The decaffeinated lot of the same Rwandan natural came out astringent and sweet-less on every one of four standard profiles (and one profile run at three charge temperatures). The writer suspected the denser decaf seed was not developing evenly inside, and that it dried too early to leave water for caramelization. Three new roasts combined a "soak" (lower early heat) with restricted airflow; all three removed the astringency, and the roast with the least soak (50 % starting gas, sharp gas drop in the second half) was clearly best. One coffee, three changes at once, no replicates; charts and phase times are in images and not in the text.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Decaffeination raised density (1.15 to 1.20 g/ml) and left moisture almost unchanged (11.8 to 11.6 %).
   > "After decaffeination, the coffee had a density of 1.20 g/ml and humidity of 11.6%." — *Arrival QC*
2. **C2** — Standard profiles gave astringency, lemongrass acidity and weak sweetness, and changing heat application made little difference; this astringency was new to the writer on these profiles.
   > "It was an astringency that I had never encountered before when using these same roast profiles." — *The Goal*
   > "with this coffee the differences were very minimal." — *The Approach*
3. **C3** — The writer's reading of astringency: an under-developed seed interior or too much airflow.
   > "Astringency in a roast can be a result of underdeveloped seed interior or possibly too much airflow in the roaster." — *The Approach*
4. **C4** — The "soak method": reduce early heat so the seed warms more slowly and evenly; the drawback is lost momentum into Maillard and a muted acidity.
   > "The "soak method" involves reduced heat at the beginning of the roast, allowing the coffee seed to come up to temperature more slowly and create more even heating through the seed." — *The Approach*
   > "you can greatly reduce your thermal energy momentum going in to the Maillard reaction, leaving you with a muted acidity." — *The Approach*
5. **C5** — Restricting airflow keeps the drum more humid during early evaporation; the writer normally opens airflow to 100 % just before first crack.
   > "Reducing the airflow in the machine will keep the humidity inside the drum higher as water evaporation is happening at the beginning of the roast." — *The Approach*
6. **C6** — Roast design: R1 charge 380 °F and 25 % gas, gradual steps, airflow restricted longer; R2 even lower charge and slower ramp (longest soak); R3 charge 380 °F but 50 % starting gas with faster steps, then a sharper gas cut in the second half to lengthen caramelization.
   > "I used a low charge temperature (380 F) with a starting gas application of 25%." — *The Approach*
   > "my initial gas application was 50% (where the first two were 25%)." — *The Approach*
   > "I dropped my heat application more drastically for the second half of the roast to stretch out time spent in this phase." — *The Approach*
7. **C7** — Cup results: all three removed the astringency and improved sweetness; R3 was the standout, R2 (longest soak) was "flat" but more chocolatey.
   > "On the cupping table, Roast 3 was the real standout." — *The Approach*
   > "this batch all had better sweetness, more pleasant acidity, and the astringency was gone." — *The Approach*
   > "The roast with the longest soak (Roast 2) was "flat" compared to the others" — *The Approach*
8. **C8** — The writer's reading of why R3 won: more momentum into Maillard gave more intense and complex acidity.
   > "Roast 3 was able to gather more momentum into Maillard" — *The Approach*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The closest published "fix" story for astringency plus low sweetness on a dense coffee: lower early energy, less airflow, then keep momentum. Radiant IR roasters heat the bean surface fast, and renatoa reports fast Skywalker roasts that look dark outside but are under-developed inside (S0015). The writer's explanation (the inside lagging behind) points the same way. The "soak" is a lower-early-heat idea; the one IR astringency recipe so far (S0026, Rytopa) also uses lower power percentages and small steps, but with a higher charge. Untested here, and a gas drum differs from a lamp roaster.

## Gatherer notes
- Three variables moved together (charge/gas, soak, airflow) so the article cannot isolate the cause; the best roast had the shortest soak but also the sharpest second-half gas cut.
- Related: S0040 (same lot, no decaf), where airflow restriction was used for a different suspected cause (too much water lost).
- A reader comment (2026-07-11) says the decaf was "delicate" and "tricksy"; no data.
