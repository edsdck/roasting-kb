# S0031 — Add moisture during a roast? (Home-Barista thread)

**Author:** EddyQ and others · **Type:** forum thread (47 posts over 5 pages, 2025-09-25 to 2025-10-29; all pages read) · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** multiple (electric Quest M3s, fluid-bed IKAWA) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/add-moisture-during-roast-t101224.html)

## Summary
A thread that grew out of a discussion of Rob Hoos's *Cultivar* / *Field Notes*. GDM528 shares humidity-sensor curves from an IKAWA showing that beans release moisture all the way through first crack and that the release can be steered by the heating rate. EddyQ then tests injecting steam (a La Pavoni wand, about 1000 W) into a Quest M3s intake: he measures weight loss at different drop points, runs a charge-temperature comparison, oven-dries green to get moisture, and tastes one Ethiopian with and without steam. Results are small: steam raised weight loss slightly (0.4 percentage points at a pre-crack drop), did not lower the heat needed, and gave at most a slight fruitiness difference that other members say could be roast variation. The thread also has several practical numbers on green moisture and weight loss.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — On an IKAWA with a humidity sensor, moisture release does not stop at "dry end": it keeps rising until about first crack and stays well above ambient afterwards.
   > "The beans never 'dry out'. The moisture released actually keeps increasing until they reach a temperature kinda around first crack." — *post #1 (GDM528's post recreated by the moderator)*
2. **C2** — The shape of the moisture-release curve can be changed through the heating rate (bean temperature rate of rise); whether that changes the cup was not known to the poster.
   > "I've found that by adjusting the rate of rise of the BT, I can 'park' the moisture so it's increasing, flat, or decreasing." — *post #1*
3. **C3** — GDM528 later corrected himself: the beans account for only about a fifth of the moisture reaching the sensor, the rest being ambient air; and he cautions that the sensor is not absolute.
   > "The moisture produced by the beans is only around 20% of the total moisture flowing over the sensor." — *post #22*
   > "enough for me to punt any attempt at absolute accuracy" — *post #22*
4. **C4** — Test with dehydrated green beans: even with no free water left to dry out, the roast still produced audible cracks at the same temperature as normal green. (Relevant to what sets the first-crack reading.)
   > "The beans in the roast still produced audible cracks at the same temperature as 'normal' greens..." — *post #15, GDM528*
5. **C5** — Steam added to a Quest M3s intake gave only a small difference in weight loss for a pre-first-crack drop, and the roaster needed more power, not less.
   > "But there was a clear difference in moisture losses. Only 0.4% delta." — *post #26, EddyQ*
   > "I had to add 50-100W more power (baseline has 800W) after 3min on the roasts with added moisture." — *post #26*
6. **C6** — Repeating the comparison with a drop at 300 °F gave identical weight loss for steam and no steam; so steam did not add moisture early that was then lost later.
   > "Both had exactly 2.7% moisture loss." — *post #42, EddyQ*
7. **C7** — A very different charge temperature (420 °F vs 320 °F, same power and air at charge) did not change weight loss in the way "case hardening" would suggest; the slower roast lost slightly less even though it was longer.
   > "Fast roast had charge temp of 420F and slow roast 320F." — *post #42*
   > "The result shows a little less moisture loss for the slower roast even though its length exceeded the baseline roast." — *post #42*
8. **C8** — EddyQ oven-dried the Kenyan green and other coffees and found about 9.6 % moisture.
   > "all coffees I oven dried had around 9.6% moisture content." — *post #42*
9. **C9** — A very light Ethiopian dropped only 30 s after first crack lost 8.2 % in both steam and no-steam roasts; a home colour meter read 125.3 (dry) and 131.7 (steam), compared with about 139 for ultra-light roasts he had measured.
   > "only 30sec of development resulted in only 8.2% ML for both roasts!" — *post #44, EddyQ*
   > "measured 125.3 with dry, 131.7 moist." — *post #44*
10. **C10** — Tasting: EddyQ and jgr314 found the steam roast slightly fruitier or with more acidity, but the coffee was only about 18 days off roast and Jonk points out that normal roast-to-roast variation could explain it.
    > "The moist added roast did have a little more fruitiness." — *post #47, EddyQ*
    > "those differences in taste could just as well be explained by the small variations." — *post #46, Jonk*
11. **C11** — EddyQ's own summary: steam in the intake raised weight loss, did not reduce the heat needed, and the exhaust temperature suggests no better heat transfer to the beans.
    > "Adding hot steam to roaster input air did not lower the needed heat from the roaster." — *post #47*
    > "higher moisture air appears to have not transferred heat from the air to the beans any better than drier air." — *post #47*
12. **C12** — Weight loss progressed steadily when roasts were dropped at successive minutes; and weight loss is used as a proxy for roast degree.
    > "the end result was a progression of weight loss." — *post #20, EddyQ (describing a test with jgr314 on a Fresh Roast)*
    > "Weight loss has been used as a proxy for roast degree, it's pretty predictable." — *post #21, yakster*
13. **C13** — Second-hand claim in the thread: yakster posts an mtpak.coffee article saying humid air inside the chamber transfers heat more efficiently (faster roast), while humid ambient air drawn in unheated competes with the beans for heat (slower roast); the article cites Rao. The thread does not settle it.
    > "humidity in the roasting air increases the efficiency of heat transfer, causing the bean to lose moisture quickly." — *post #5, yakster quoting the mtpak.coffee article's paraphrase of Rao's* The Coffee Roaster's Companion *(not verified against the article or the book)*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Gives the owner several concrete numbers for green moisture (~9.6 % measured on one home roaster's coffees), light-roast weight loss at a short development (8.2 %), and evidence that dehydrated green still cracks at the same temperature — useful for the open questions on why first-crack readings differ and what "weight loss above moisture" means. The steam experiments themselves are not transferable to a sealed IR lamp roaster, and the fluid-bed moisture curves are not drum data. Charts (images) were not extractable.
