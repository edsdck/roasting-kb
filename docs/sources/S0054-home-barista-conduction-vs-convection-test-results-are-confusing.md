# S0054 — Home-Barista: conduction vs convection test results are confusing (2026)

**Author:** Wbj828 and others · **Type:** forum thread (60 posts, 6 pages) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple (mostly burner-heated drums) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/conduction-vs-convection-test-results-are-confusing-t104515.html)

## Summary
The OP held the environmental temperature (ET) to the same curve on a '2k MCR' drum roaster while setting four airflow levels (20-50 % of the pressure-drop range) and found the bean-temperature (BT) curves almost identical; a 750 g vs 1500 g test gave a delayed copy of the same curve. Replies explain it: more air makes the burner work harder, which heats the drum, so conduction does not fall. The OP later cites a relayed 90 / 7 / 3.5 % convection / radiation / conduction split for a 250 lb drum and another_jim's view that the debate is "pointless", and ends by keeping airflow constant. A drum-speed test (40 to 80 rpm, same ET) changed the BT slope strongly, and at 80 rpm the roaster struggled to hold the set ET, which the OP attributes to lofted beans taking heat from the drum. EddyQ argues radiation from a 500-550 °F drum may dominate late in the roast. No cup results are given for any test.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — OP's airflow test: identical ET curve, four airflow levels, BT curves nearly overlaid.
   > "I managed the Environmental temperature to follow identical curves in each roast." — *post #1, Wbj828*
   > "They almost lie right on top of one another." — *post #1*
2. **C2** — Reason offered: raising airflow raises burner output and so drum heat; airflow cannot cleanly change the conduction share.
   > "As airflow is increased, so is gas and hence the heating of the drum and hence "conduction"." — *post #6, EddyQ*
   > "More air (while holding ET steady) does not result in a measurable difference in the BT curve." — *post #58, Wbj828*
3. **C3** — A larger batch on the same ET and fan gave a later but similar curve.
   > "it looks like the larger batch is simply a delayed version of the smaller batch." — *post #19, Wbj828*
4. **C4** — A drum-speed test with ET held constant changed BT slope sharply; at the highest rpm the roaster struggled to hold the set ET, which the OP puts down to beans lofted into the air stream taking heat out of the drum.
   > "for the highest RPM(80), the BT was absolutely flying. For the slowest (40), it took its sweet time." — *post #51*
   > "the bean probe does not measure bean temperatures. It actually measures itself." — *post #51*
5. **C5** — Probe type shifted readings: after swapping to RTD probes the roaster read about 20 °F lower and first crack appeared at 365 instead of 385 (°F); an offset felt wrong, so the OP went back to K-type thermocouples and 385 returned.
   > "FC was usually at 385, and now it was at 365." — *post #15*
   > "The FC temp returned to 385 and I am much happier." — *post #15*
6. **C6** — EddyQ's drum measures 500-550 °F in development, and he argues radiation from the hot drum may dominate then; he calls it controversial.
   > "My drum in my MC North 1K measures between 500 and 550F during the development phase of a roast." — *post #18*
   > "I think when the drum is at 550F, the IR heating may dominate the heat transfer from the drum." — *post #18*
7. **C7** — A split relayed second-hand from a 2007 Roast Magazine article for a 250 lb drum roaster.
   > "most of the heat transfer in a traditional 250lb drum roaster is via convection around 90 %, followed by radiation (7%) followed by conduction (3.5%)." — *post #28, Wbj828 (his reading of Schwartzberg and Adluri)*
8. **C8** — Relayed views that the debate does not matter for taste, and that matched BT and colour give matched roasts (Hoos and Cooper, see S0056).
   > "The entire radiation versus conduction versus convection debate is pointless; it has no bearing on coffee taste." — *post #28, quoting another_jim*
   > "essentially no difference in roasts if you match the metrics of the BT curve as well as the final color." — *post #28*
9. **C9** — Drying is affected more by air than by the other heat paths.
   > "the drying that is occurring throughout the roast is MUCH more effected with air temperature and hence convection than the other two sources of heat." — *post #30, EddyQ*
10. **C10** — Several owners settle on constant airflow; one finds it gives a smoother RoR curve.
    > "The best air strategy is to keep it constant." — *post #40, Wbj828*
    > "I can produce a smoother, more consistent RoR curve if I leave the airflow at a fixed setting." — *post #14, Mathwiz*
11. **C11** — On electric roasters more airflow also needs more heater power, so a race-to-first-crack airflow test is not a clean convection test.
    > "on the electric Quest M3, I'm fairly certain more airflow also needs more power to the heating elements." — *post #43, Jonk*
12. **C12** — A user's rule of thumb: on a direct-heated drum airflow changes the conduction:convection ratio only slightly; figures given as about 30:70 (direct) and 15:85 (indirect); no source.
    > "a direct drum heating system of around 30:70 and an indirect drum heating system of around 15:85" — *post #60, BeWe*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The thread is about burner-heated drums, but two points connect to the Skywalker: (1) a 250 lb drum is said (second-hand) to get only about 7 % from radiation and the debate is called moot, while S0033 puts radiation ahead of convection, so the numbers on this are not settled; (2) the first-crack reading moved 20 °F with the probe type, which fits S0045 (probe depth) and shows why a Skywalker's reading should not be compared with another machine's.

## Gatherer notes
The 90 / 7 / 3.5 split is the OP's summary of an article he says is dense, not a quote from it; the original (Schwartzberg and Adluri, Roast Magazine Mar-Apr 2007) was not found. BeWe's 30:70 and 15:85 figures have no source. The OP stops the experiment without cupping. Charts are images and are not recorded. The relayed Hoos and Cooper conclusion is checked against the paper in S0056.
