# S0049 — Sage (SCAA, 2012): literature review on coffee staling and shelf life

**Author:** Emma Sage · **Type:** review article · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://sca.coffee/sca-news/2012/02/15/what-is-the-shelf-life-of-roasted-coffee-a-literature-review-on-coffee-staling)

## Summary
A short review of the staling literature (28 original studies are counted in the text, 12 of them not peer-reviewed). It concludes that oxygen is the main factor controlling shelf life, that loss of freshness is fastest early in storage, and that packaging changes (vacuum, nitrogen flushing) slow it several-fold. It also states that few of the studies were designed so that specialty coffee could use them. Secondary source: numbers are reported from the underlying studies, which were not checked here.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Oxygen is the most important factor controlling shelf life.
   > "oxygen was the most important factor controlling the shelf life of coffee" — *sentence beginning "Labuza et al. (2001) determined that…"*
2. **C2** — Lipids are only about 15 % of the dry weight of roasted arabica.
   > "In roasted arabica, lipids only account for about 15% of the dry weight" — *oxidation section*
3. **C3** — Methanethiol, a key aroma compound, falls quickly in storage.
   > "after eight days of storage, methanethiol decreased to about 30% of its original amount" — *volatile section*
4. **C4** — Degassing of whole bean roasted coffee has a temperature coefficient (Q10) of 1.5.
   > "Labuza et al. (2001) reported that degassing whole bean roasted coffee had a Q10 value of 1.5" — *degassing section*
5. **C5** — Vacuum packaging slows staling about fivefold relative to air.
   > "coffee vacuum packaged had a staling rate five times lower than beans packed in air" — *packaging section*
6. **C6** — Nitrogen flushing doubled sensory shelf life in the study cited: six months flushed vs three months not flushed.
   > "nitrogen-flushed coffee had a six-month shelf life" — *packaging section*
7. **C7** — Freshness loss is steepest early: the greatest rate in the first month (one study) and CO2/volatile losses most dramatic in the first few days (another).
   > "the greatest rate of freshness loss occurred in the first month of coffee storage." — *storage-time section*
8. **C8** — The review judges most staling research unsuitable for specialty coffee.
   > "very few studies reviewed here were able to meet the scientific needs of the specialty coffee community" — *conclusion*
9. **C9** — Almost half of the reviewed original studies were not peer-reviewed.
   > "almost half of the original studies researching coffee staling included in this review (12 of the 28) were not available in a peer-reviewed article." — *discussion*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Roaster-independent. For a home roaster working in 350 g batches, the useful points are the strong early decline (so "rest then use within weeks") and the oxygen finding, which bears on how to store opened bags (see S0048). The numbers are second-hand and mix packaging types and analytical methods, so they are directional, not parameters to plan with.
