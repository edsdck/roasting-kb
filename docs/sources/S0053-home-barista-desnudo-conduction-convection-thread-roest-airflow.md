# S0053 — Desnudo conduction vs convection thread: Roest high-airflow vs low-airflow test (Home-Barista, 2026)

**Author:** archipelago and others · **Type:** forum thread (9 posts) · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** electric-drum (Roest L200) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/desnudo-coffee-conduction-vs-convection-article-t102686.html)

## Summary
yakster posts the Desnudo Coffee essay (see S0052). archipelago replies that its test came from his own Roest work: the same coffee roasted with "high airflow, low inlet" and "lower airflow, higher inlet" profiles at the same roast duration and roast loss, with the drum pressure held equal. Air temperature inside the drum was about the same in both while the inlet temperature differed by nearly 50 °F at the peak; the high-airflow roast needed more heater power, and the lower-airflow roast ran lower drum and exhaust temperatures. Bean-probe readings were higher in the high-airflow roast, which he attributes to faster transfer to the probe. In the cup the two were "extremely similar"; the low-airflow roast had slightly better body and juicier fruit. Charts are in images and are not in the text; batch size and the coffee are not given. An EddyQ post adds that his Quest M3s barely responds to airflow changes.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Roest owner's design: same coffee, same roast duration and similar roast loss, one profile with high airflow and low inlet temperature, one with lower airflow and higher inlet temperature.
   > "looking at the differences in cup outcomes between a "high airflow, low inlet" profile versus "lower airflow, higher inlet" profile for a given roast duration and roast loss." — *post #2, archipelago*
2. **C2** — He kept drum pressure equal so pressure would not affect the result, and roasted to roughly equal weight loss (slightly less dehydration with high air).
   > "I controlled the pressure in the drums by adjusting heater fan ratio and exhaust together to ensure that pressure didn't impact the roasting results" — *post #7*
3. **C3** — Faster air raises the convective transfer coefficient, so the inlet temperature can be lower for the same result.
   > "when you increase airspeed, you're increasing the coefficient of convective transfer." — *post #7*
4. **C4** — The high-airflow roast needed more heater power despite the lower inlet temperature; in-drum air temperature was about the same although inlets differed by up to nearly 50 °F.
   > "the higher airflow roast, even though it calls for lower inlet temperatures, requires more power" — *post #7*
   > "inlets vary by, at peak, nearly 50ºf" — *post #7*
   > "because of reduced 'bypass' air, the lower airflow roast also had lower drum temperatures (purple line) and exhaust temperatures (teal)." — *post #7*
5. **C5** — Bean-probe readings run higher in the high-airflow roast, which he puts down to faster transfer to the probe.
   > "(Bean temps appear to be higher in the higher airflow roast because of the more rapid transfer to the probe)" — *post #7*
6. **C6** — Cup: nearly indistinguishable; the lower-airflow roast was slightly better. Not a blind or replicated test.
   > "In the cup, these two roasts were extremely similar" — *post #7*
   > "the lower airflow' roast did seem to have slightly better body and a bit juicier fruit" — *post #7*
7. **C7** — His practice: lower airflow with slightly higher drum speed on drum roasters; the minimum air that lofts the beans on fluid beds.
   > "On drum roasters, airflow is typically too high IME" — *post #7*
   > "I usually use the minimum amount of air required to loft and slightly tumble the coffee" — *post #7*
8. **C8** — Another owner reports his electric Quest M3s hardly responds to airflow changes.
   > "My Quest M3s sadly does not respond to airflow adjustments much at all." — *post #9, EddyQ*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A hot-air drum, so it says nothing direct about a lamp roaster. What carries over is the design: equal duration and loss, only the airflow/inlet split changed, and the result was a small cup difference at most. That is a counterweight to the strong airflow claims in S0040/S0041 and S0026/S0027. On a Skywalker the fan also changes the lamp's heat balance, so a fan-only test remains confounded.
