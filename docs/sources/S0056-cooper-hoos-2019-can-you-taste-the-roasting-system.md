# S0056 — Cooper and Hoos 2019: matched profiles on different roasters were hard to tell apart

**Author:** Anne Cooper, Rob Hoos · **Type:** magazine feature (PDF, pp. 24-42) · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.eqmr.com.au/_files/ugd/5d1111_982a7178771340dbb245730d19d67d8d.pdf)

## Summary
The authors roasted the same coffee on three different machines (a Probat UG15, a Loring S35 and a Diedrich IR-5) to profiles matched on time to yellow, time to first crack, development time and ratio, and end colour (within 3 Agtron points); temperature was deliberately not matched because probes differ. They then ran blind triangle tests at industry events. At the Firestarter event 163 of 529 triangles (about 31 %) picked the odd cup, against a stated chance level of 25-33 %; across all events, 499 of 1,689 (29.54 %). They read this as evidence that the machine is not what makes the cups different and that the roast profile is the main driver. It is a pilot: no significance test, and the coffee is not named.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Matching was done on time and colour, not on temperature, because temperature probes differ between machines.
   > "For our pilot project, we chose three roasting machines/systems" — *Method*
2. **C2** — Firestarter 2018 matched roasts (yellow / first crack / drop; development; Agtron whole / ground): Probat UG15 4 kg 4:30 / 8:56 / 10:43, 1:47 = 16.6 %, 65.8 / 85.7; Loring S35 7 kg 4:29 / 8:52 / 10:40, 1:47 = 16.74 %, 66.83 / 84.26; Diedrich IR-5 2 kg 4:36 / 9:06 / 10:51, 1:45 = 16.13 %, 65.9 / 84.1. (The three timing columns are read in that order from the table; the labels were not in the extracted text.)
3. **C3** — Test format: triangle of three cups, blind, with a "cannot taste the difference" option; eight triangles at Firestarter, 27 tasters on day one and 39 on day two.
   > "Their choices were Cup 1, Cup 2, Cup 3, or Cannot Taste the Difference." — *Triangle tests*
   > "pick the different cup in a blind triangulation cupping" — *Triangle tests*
4. **C4** — Result at Firestarter: 163 correct of 529.
   > "The global results showed that, out of 529 triangles tasted, those surveyed could only find the correct answer 163 times." — *Results*
5. **C5** — Across all events in Figure 9 the global total was 499 correct of 1,689 (29.54 %); individual rows ran from 21.3 % (SCA 2018) to 39.4 % (RG Retreat student roasts).
6. **C6** — The authors compare the result with chance and say expert panels have done barely better than chance in similar tests.
   > "The fact that the panels of specialty coffee professionals have been only barely outperforming random chance" — *Discussion*
7. **C7** — Their reading: with matched profiles the machine is not the cause of cup differences.
   > "we suggest that the machine itself is not causing the difference" — *Discussion, p. 40*
8. **C8** — The takeaway on what does cause differences.
   > "The first is that the roast profile is the primary driver of coffee flavor." — *Conclusion, p. 42*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* This is the strongest evidence in the base that, once total time, first-crack time, development time and end colour are matched, expert tasters mostly cannot tell roasters apart, including a 2 kg infrared-model drum (Diedrich IR-5; the article does not describe its heating, so treat "IR" as the model name only). It supports using the Skywalker to reproduce a drum-style time profile instead of worrying about the lamp. Limits: matching needed work on every machine, the coffee is unnamed, no p-values, and drums of 2-7 kg are not a 350 g lamp roaster.
