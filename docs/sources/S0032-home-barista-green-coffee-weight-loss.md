# S0032 — Green coffee weight loss (Home-Barista thread)

**Author:** Home-Barista members · **Type:** forum thread (14 posts over 2 pages, 2025-01-20 to 2025-01-27; both pages read) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple (machines mostly unstated) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/green-coffee-weight-loss-t97727.html)

## Summary
A beginner asks how much green coffee weighs after roasting, and experienced home roasters answer with their own logged ranges. Typical medium to medium-light roasts lose about 11–15 %, most around 13–14 %; darker roasts 15–16 % or more; very light roasts 9–11 %. Two posters note that moisture in the green coffee changes the figure. Near the end, a poster asks how others reach very low loss and another reports that very low loss tends to give an uneven roast in a 500 g batch. Small, anecdotal sample, but several independent logs agree.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The loss depends on roast darkness; one member has read ranges of 11–18 %.
   > "I read ranges from 11% to 18%." — *post #3, mgrayson*
2. **C2** — Typical home range 11–15 %; very light roasts can be 9–10 %; moisture of the green changes the number.
   > "My range is typically 11 - 15%, though I've clocked a few 9 & 10 % very light roasts." — *post #5, yakster*
   > "This can also vary depending on the moisture of your green coffee." — *post #5, yakster*
3. **C3** — Medium to medium-light roasters report most roasts near 13 %; others 13–14 %, with darker roasts at 15–16 %.
   > "I'm also in the 11-15% range, most are around 13%" — *post #6, randytsuch*
   > "darker roasts for F&F can be 15-16%" — *post #7, LBIespresso*
4. **C4** — A medium Brazil loses about 14 % for one member.
   > "When I roast a medium Brazil, for example, I'm looking at about 14% loss." — *post #4, Theophilus*
5. **C5** — Dropping an Ethiopian at the first stray crack sounds, before the main chorus, gave a moisture loss of 10.8 % (corrected from 10.3 % in post #10, which he first called "development").
   > "I had such a light roast when I dropped the beans after the first outlier FC sounds, but before the chorus of FC sounds." — *post #10, Theophilus*
   > "10.8% was the moisture loss." — *post #12, Theophilus*
6. **C6** — A coffee dropped 1:30 after first crack lost 12 %.
   > "The beans I am roasting resulted in 12% when dropped 1min 30sec past first crack" — *post #9, EddyQ*
7. **C7** — Very low weight loss on a 500 g batch of a Cormorant gave uneven roasts for one member who can reach it on an IKAWA but not on the Cormorant; he guesses larger thermal and bean mass helps even heating.
   > "When I try to roast with that little weight loss I find I am more prone to get an uneven roast." — *post #14, LBIespresso*
   > "I can get there with the Ikawa but not with the Cormorant." — *post #14*
8. **C8** — A roughly 20 % allowance covers both roast loss and culled green defects; a Sivetz chart linking weight loss to roast degree (for fluid beds) is mentioned but its image was not available.
   > "20% should account for culling green coffee defects and the weight loss of roasting" — *post #5, yakster*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Gives a handful of independent log-based ranges to compare with the owner's median 13.4 % loss: it sits in the middle of the common range, and the 9–11 % figures are what very light roasters get. The unevenness remark for 500 g and very low loss is a different machine type but is a hint about the risk of under-development inside the bean at light loss. Many posters do not name machines, and green moisture is mostly unknown.
