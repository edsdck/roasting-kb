# S0014 — Beyond Elevation: bean density and roasting (Roast Magazine, May/June 2025)

**Author:** Steve Lee · **Type:** trade-magazine feature · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://www.roastmagazine.com/stories/beyond-elevation)

## Summary
Argues that measured bean density is a better guide than growing elevation. Cites a Nepal study
where density rose with elevation (≈620 → 688 kg/m³). Experienced roasters describe how dense
beans take heat more slowly at first but then transfer it efficiently, hold more water (risk of a
stall before first crack), and tolerate more heat; lower-density coffees suit gentler heat and
longer browning. Hoos cautions that the flavour goal, not density, should drive profile design.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Density generally increases with elevation (slower ripening), e.g. 687.5 ± 8.18 kg/m³ at 1,400–1,500 MASL vs 619.75 ± 13.72 kg/m³ at 800–900 MASL in a Nepal study; but elevation alone is unreliable (latitude/climate).
   > "Kornman says that elevation alone should not dictate roasting decisions." — *main text*
2. **C2** — Dense beans absorb energy slowly at first, then transfer heat internally more efficiently.
   > "denser coffees are going to absorb and transfer heat internally more efficiently than less dense coffee," — *Ian Picco quote*
3. **C3** — For dense coffees: higher charge temperature, lower airflow and slightly lower gas at the start, then more heat after colour change to raise RoR and shorten the roast, preserving aromatics and acidity.
   > (paraphrase; see paragraph on adjusting for density)
4. **C4** — Dense seeds hold more water; the moisture release can cause a stall approaching first crack, so keep heat in the drum then.
   > "Don't be afraid to punch it during the developmental phase with a high-elevation coffee." — *Ian Picco quote*
5. **C5** — Dense coffee tolerates more heat; Picco drops a few degrees higher than usual for sweetness and complexity.
   > "It can handle more heat being a denser seed." — *Ian Picco quote*
6. **C6** — For lower-density coffees aimed at lower acidity and fuller body: slower, gentler heat, focus on sugar browning, longer Maillard, possibly darker.
   > (paraphrase of Kornman quote)
7. **C7** — Flavour goal should drive profile design; density only changes how you drive the roaster to get there.
   > (paraphrase of Hoos quote)
8. **C8** — Practical density measurement: weigh a fixed volume (graduated cylinder) or use a density meter, and log it.
   > (paraphrase; see measurement section)

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Measuring density (a kitchen scale + measuring cylinder) is cheap and would
give the owner a variable to log with each roast. The direction of the advice (dense → more energy
early/through first crack, low density → gentler) likely transfers; the specific levers (gas,
airflow, charge) need translating to lamp power and fan on the Skywalker, where fan removes heat
(S0003).

## Gatherer notes
Practitioner advice, not tested. The Nepal study's method/sample size are not given in the
article. Two dense-coffee strategies quoted (Picco extended drying/Maillard for his SL-34 vs
general advice to shorten the roast after colour change) aren't fully consistent — the article
doesn't reconcile them.
