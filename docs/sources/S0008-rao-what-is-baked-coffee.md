# S0008 — What is Baked Coffee? (Scott Rao, 2018)

**Author:** Scott Rao · **Type:** blog article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://scottrao.com/blog/2018/2/24/what-is-baked-coffee-most-pros-dont-know)

## Summary
Rao defends the "steadily declining RoR" principle. He defines RoR, explains that the RoR
"crash" around first crack is caused by moisture leaving the bean cores, and argues that the main
cause of baked coffee is a pronounced RoR crash (a drastic change in RoR slope) — not long roast
times. Baked coffee tastes hollow, flat and less sweet and loses sweetness/tartness as it cools.
He stresses that probe size and software smoothing change how crashes look, and gives settings
and a blind-cupping method to learn the taste.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — RoR tends to decline through a roast mainly because the temperature differential between beans and roasting environment decreases.
   > "The ROR tends to decline throughout a roast, primarily because the temperature gradient (differential) between the beans and the roasting environment decreases." — *What is an ROR curve?*
2. **C2** — Early-roast RoR shown by software is distorted by the probe shedding heat after charge; the real early RoR is high and drops fast.
   > "The real ROR at the beginning of a roast is quite high and drops precipitously during the first minute" — *What is an ROR curve?*
3. **C3** — Around first crack, moisture released from bean cores cools the probe/bean surfaces and can cause a rapid RoR drop ("crash").
   > "That moisture is cooler than the bean surfaces or the probe, and may cause the ROR curve to drop rapidly." — *What happens when the ROR crashes?*
4. **C4** — The main cause of baked coffee is a pronounced RoR crash (drastic change in RoR slope), not long roast times or negative RoRs; severity correlates with the crash.
   > "the main cause of baked coffee is a pronounced ROR crash, or more precisely, a drastic change in the slope of the ROR." — *What is baked coffee?*
5. **C5** — Baked coffee tastes hollower, flatter and less sweet, and loses tartness and sweetness as it cools.
   > "baked coffee seems hollower, flatter, and less sweet." — *What is baked coffee?*
6. **C6** — A crash in the last ~30 s does less damage than an earlier crash.
   > "if a crash happens very late in a roast, for example in the last 30 seconds, the coffee will be less baked" — *The fine print*
7. **C7** — Slow (thick) probes hide crashes; use a probe ≤4 mm (or check the turn shows by 1:30), 1 s sampling, Artisan delta span ≤15 s, no extra smoothing.
   > "an ROR interval ("delta span" in Artisan) of no more than 15 seconds, and no additional smoothing of curves" — *The fine print*
8. **C8** — Crashed roasts are more likely to flick and be less developed, so these issues get confused; isolate them when training.
   > "Crashed roasts are more likely to flick and more likely to be less developed" — *How do I know this is true?*
9. **C9** — To learn: cup blind, then compare to RoR curves after taking notes, looking for crash vs flatness/sweetness correlation.
   > "Always compare cupping results to ROR curves after you have tasted the cups and taken notes." — *How do I know this is true?*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The Artisan settings advice (C7) is directly actionable. Whether
"declining RoR / avoid crash" applies to an IR machine is open: S0002 argues a flat RoR is
normal for radiant heating. The moisture-release crash mechanism (C3) should still occur.
