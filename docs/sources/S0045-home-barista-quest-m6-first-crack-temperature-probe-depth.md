# S0045 — Quest M6 - First crack temperature? RTD probes (Home-Barista thread)

**Author:** Blroastin and others · **Type:** forum thread (11 posts over 2 pages, 2025-03-31 to 2025-04-06; both pages read) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** electric drum (Quest) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/quest-m6-first-crack-temperature-rtd-probes-t98852.html)

## Summary
A Quest M6 owner using Artisan and a Phidgets PT1000 probe sees first crack at 362–369 °F and asks whether that is low. Other owners give the usual Quest reading, about 392 °F, and suggest why a probe can read low: the tip is only about one inch into the drum, a small batch does not cover it, and the metal shield can pull heat out of the tip. After the owner pushed the probe in to about 1.75 in, first crack read 380–390 °F. The thread also gives one owner's Quest M6 method and charge temperatures. It is small and informal, but it is a clear example of the "why first crack reads at different temperatures" question (gap #4) on a small electric roaster, with a before/after.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — A Quest M6 with an RTD probe about 1 in deep read first crack at 362–369 °F with a charge of about 380 °F.
   > "I've been averaging about 362-369 F which seems low compared to a few M6 posts and many M3 posts I've seen." — *post #1, Blroastin*
   > "The probe location is at the standard Quest BT location, I have the probe in about 1 inch." — *post #1*
2. **C2** — Other Quest owners put first crack start at about 392 °F (200 °C) on the M3 and M6.
   > "On both my M3 and my M6, I've found 1Cs to be about 200 C (392 F) +/- a couple of degrees." — *post #2, Nunas*
3. **C3** — Two owners compare M3 and M3S: first crack around 392 °F on the M3 and around 400 °F on the M3S with the same probes and meter; FreudianSip's theory is that the M3S's perforated backplate gives more convection and hotter air around the probe.
   > "the start of first crack is right on 392ish for the M3 but usually 400ish for the M3S." — *post #4, FreudianSip*
   > "My theory is that the M3S with its perforated backplate and generally higher airflow roasts by convection more than the M3." — *post #4*
4. **C4** — A small batch may not cover the probe, and any thermocouple or RTD reading is only an approximation; Jonk expects 380–390 °F for similar probes.
   > "if your batch size is low it could be that not enough beans hit the probe to reach a more accurate temperature." — *post #3, Jonk*
   > "With similar probes I'd expect FC closer to 380-390F.. but it's always just an approximation" — *post #3*
5. **C5** — MaKoMo suggests the probe's metal shield conducts heat away from the tip; keep as much of the shield as possible in the hot drum.
   > "Your probes metal shield might cool down its tip (1st 1-2cm), where the sensor element is located, by sucking its heat out of the drum along the shield." — *post #5, MaKoMo*
6. **C6** — Result: with the probe pushed in to about 1.75 in, first crack read 380–390 °F on two roasts.
   > "My FC temp on 2 roasts was in the 380-390 F range." — *post #11, Blroastin*
   > "I was able to move the probe further in, about 1.75 in or so, before it would hit one of the drum vanes." — *post #11*
7. **C7** — Owner advice on charging: Quest drums are thin, so the drum temperature drops sharply once beans go in, and a hot charge (about 250 °C / 482 °F) works without tipping or scorching for Nunas. The OP then tried about 485 °F and got yellow at about 4 min and a total of about 9.5 min.
   > "I charge at about 250 C (482 F), sometimes higher depending on the outdoor temperature, without any tipping or scorching." — *post #2, Nunas*
   > "I also tried a charge of about 485 F and it went well: yellow right around 4 min and a total roast of about 9.5 min." — *post #11, Blroastin*
8. **C8** — Nunas's M6 method: heater at full and fan nearly off at the start, fan to maximum after BT 150 °C, heater to about half once first crack is under way, then heater off at about the middle of first crack.
   > "At the onset, fan is barely on and heater is full. Somewhere after BT 150 C, I boost the fan to max and leave it there." — *post #10, Nunas*
   > "Once 1C is underway, I cut the heater to about half." — *post #10*
   > "When 1C is about half over, I cut the heater entirely, but watch the BT to ensure that it does not go negative." — *post #10*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A concrete case for gap #4 (why first crack reads at a different temperature on a home roaster): the same roaster read 362–369 °F with a shallow probe and 380–390 °F with a deeper one, so first-crack temperature is partly a property of the probe. That is relevant to the owner's Artisan set-up, where only a record of the reading on the owner's own machine is meaningful. The charge and fan advice is for a Quest, not an IR lamp roaster, and is not transferable as settings. No weight loss, development time or cup notes are given.
