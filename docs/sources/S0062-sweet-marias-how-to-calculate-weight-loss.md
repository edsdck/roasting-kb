# S0062 — Sweet Maria's: weight loss by roast-level name, and why it can mislead

**Author:** Thompson Owen · **Type:** how-to article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://library.sweetmarias.com/how-to-calculate-weight-loss-in-coffee-roasting/)

## Summary
A how-to on measuring weight loss, with Sweet Maria's approximate loss and end temperature for each of its roast-level names, averaged across air and drum roasters: first crack 412 °F / 10.3 % up to French 448 °F / 15.6 % and Burnt 455+ °F / 16.6+ %. The article warns that green moisture (typically about 10.5 %, higher or lower for some Ethiopian, Yemeni or monsooned lots) changes how dark a given loss will look, so the same loss gives different roasts on a 9 % and a 10.5 % coffee. The values are the shop's own rules of thumb, not measurements from a stated sample set.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Formula: loss % = (green weight - roasted weight) / green weight x 100.
   > "(Green Weight – Roasted Weight) / Green Weight * 100 = Weight Loss %." — *How do I calculate weight loss in coffee roasting?*
2. **C2** — Coffee swells in volume but loses weight, mostly water.
   > "it diminishes in weight, mostly because of loss of water weight." — *introduction*
3. **C3** — Approximate loss by roast name (first crack to Burnt): 10.3, 11.5, 12.7, 13.3, 14.5, 15.1, 15.6, 16.6+ %, with end temperatures 412 to 455+ °F; these are averages for air and drum roasters and may not fit a given machine.
   > "These are average between air roasters and drum roasters so they might not fit 100% with your machine" — *Roast Weight Loss and Degree-Of-Roast Names*
4. **C4** — Green moisture varies: generally around 10.5 %, with outliers in Ethiopian, Yemeni and monsooned lots.
   > "Generally yes, they are in a range hovering around 10.5% or so." — *Problems and Pitfalls*
5. **C5** — The same percentage can mean different roasts on different moistures.
   > "You can roast to the same weight loss percentage overall but will have 2 different roast results." — *Problems and Pitfalls*
   > "your roast will end up a lot darker than you expected." — *Problems and Pitfalls (9 % moisture, 15 % target)*
6. **C6** — Within one coffee, weight loss helps repeat a roast weeks later; across coffees moisture can throw it off; green moisture meters range from roughly $250 (not accurate in his experience) to about $1,800.
7. **C7** — A commenter points out that chaff leaves with moisture and so inflates the apparent loss; the author agrees to check it.

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A second, independent set of loss-to-roast-level numbers to set beside Hoos (S0059): Sweet Maria's puts City+ at 13.3 % and Full City+ at 15.1 %, where Hoos calls 11-13 % light and 14-16 % medium, so the two broadly agree. For a 350 g batch the table works as a check on the Artisan weight-loss readout (derived: 13.3 % of 350 g is about 46.5 g lost). The temperatures are for home air/drum roasters and shop probes, so do not map them onto a Skywalker bean probe.
