# S0066 — A universal colour curve for roasted arabica (UC Davis, 2025)

**Author:** Anokye-Bempah, Styczynski, Ristenpart, Donis-Gonzalez · **Type:** peer-reviewed paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-06601-w)

## Summary
The same 39 roasts as S0007 (titratable acidity) were measured for colour instead. Seven very different profiles of the same total length (16 min, 5 kg Probat), three coffees, a sample every minute, ground-bean colour in CIELAB. Despite wildly different profiles and origins, every sample fell on one curve in L*a*b* space, and the colour at colour change, first crack and second crack was nearly the same whatever the profile. The authors checked the curve against 20 published studies and propose using it for roast-level standards. No sensory data.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — All colours followed a single curve in L*a*b* space, from about L* 60 (green) to about L* 20 (very dark), whatever the profile or origin.
   > "starting near L* = 60 for green coffee and reaching about L* = 20 for very dark coffee"
   — *Results, Correlations of the L*a*b* color coordinates*
2. **C2** — Colour at the milestones was nearly the same for all profiles and origins; at first crack the average L* was 29.74 (SD 1.9) and at second crack 19.96 (SD 1).
   > "all samples had an average L* value of 62.38 ± 2.6 at color change, 29.74 ± 1.9 at first crack, 19.96 ± 1 at second crack, and 17.19 ± 1.6 at the end of the roast, irrespective of the roast profile or coffee origin"
   — *Results, Effect of roast profiles and green coffee origin on color*
3. **C3** — The same holds for a* and b* (values in the numbers table).
   > "despite the differences in roast profiles and coffee origins, all samples exhibited a similar color at these major roast milestones"
   — *Results*
4. **C4** — Faster profiles changed colour faster; the "defect" profiles (negative rate of rise, exaggerated flick) had colour paths almost the same as the fast-start profile.
   > "Roast profiles with a high initial rate of rise (RoR)—the rate of temperature increase per 30 s—such as the FS profile, demonstrated faster color changes compared to roast profiles with a low initial RoR"
   — *Results, first paragraph*
5. **C5** — The curve fits the authors' data closely (mean colour distance 1.19; 86 % of points under 2, which the paper calls no perceptible difference).
   > "The average ΔE* was 1.19 ± 0.76."
   — *Results, Correlations section*
6. **C6** — A meta-analysis of 20 published studies (392 L*a*b* values) mostly followed the curve; the five that did not had unusually grey or whitish values, unreported methods or instrument differences.
   > "In total, these publications include 392 distinct L*a*b* values."
   — *Results, A universal roasted coffee color curve*
7. **C7** — There is no agreed definition of light, medium or dark roast, and different roast analyzers use different scales, so an Agtron number does not transfer between devices.
   > "may not correspond to the same value on other devices"
   — *Discussion*
8. **C8** — Colour was measured on ground coffee with a laboratory spectrophotometer, not whole beans.
   > "The color of each ground sample was measured using a HunterLab ColorFlex EZ Spectrophotometer"
   — *Methods, Color measurements*
9. **C9** — Limits: specialty-grade arabica only, no decaf, long (16 min) roasts; the authors ask for shorter roast times, other scales (Agtron, Colorette, Colortrack) and other origins in future work.
   > "Future work should explore a broader range of coffee species, origins, and postharvest processing methods—including decaffeinated coffee—as well as other roast profiles, including shorter roast times."
   — *Conclusion*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Useful as a reference for what colour means: at first crack a ground sample reads about L* 30 on a laboratory spectrophotometer, and that point barely moves with the profile, so colour is a profile-independent marker of how far along the roast is (relevant to the owner's question of how to tell roast degree at home, and to why weight loss and colour should be recorded together). It does not say how colour relates to weight loss, Agtron or phone/DiFluid readings, and the authors themselves say scales differ between instruments. The roasts are 16 min on a 5 kg commercial drum, so the colour-versus-time paths do not transfer to a ~9 min lamp roast; only the milestone colours might.

## Gatherer notes
- Same dataset and group as S0007 (titratable acidity), so the two are not independent confirmations of each other.
- A Correction (10 Oct 2025, doi 10.1038/s41598-025-23127-3) fixed the x-axis labels of Fig. 4 and wrong citation numbers (53-55) in the Discussion; the numbers above come from the corrected article page and match the original.
- A popular SCA summary exists ("What Color is Your Coffee?", 25 Magazine Issue 21) with a 22-row draft colour-name table (survey of 125 Roasters Guild attendees, Sept 2023); the table is an image and was not read, so it is not extracted here.
- Figures and Table 1 (roast-profile parameters) are images and were not read; profile timings are in that table and possibly in S0007 (not checked).
