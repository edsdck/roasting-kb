# S0005 — Why Rate of Rise is a bad reference point (CoffeeMind podcast ep. 17)

**Author:** Morten Münchow · **Type:** podcast with full transcript (also a blog post) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** gas-drum (general) · [Original](https://coffeemind.buzzsprout.com/1897439/11992254-why-rate-of-rise-is-a-bad-reference-point-for-optimizing-flavour-in-coffee-roasting)

## Summary
A critique of using RoR — and especially the idea of a constantly declining RoR — as the main
reference for optimizing flavour. Münchow argues RoR is just the slope of the BT curve, amplifies
probe noise, makes no specific flavour predictions, transfers poorly between machines, and
encourages mid-roast improvisation. He recommends time, temperature and colour as core
variables, a Plan–Execute–Learn loop, one-variable-at-a-time experiments at equal colour, and
discrimination testing before trusting small differences. RoR remains useful as a "speedometer"
and to categorize profiles by average RoR after first crack on one machine.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — RoR is useful as a speedometer: predicting whether you'll hit a waypoint (e.g. first crack) early or late.
   > "for predicting where the roast is heading just like the speedometer on your car." — *Where is RoR useful*
2. **C2** — Average RoR after first crack can categorize profiles on one machine; short development (higher RoR) → fruitier/more acidic, long development → less fruity, more chocolate/nutty (citing S0006).
   > "This number is highly machine dependent so you can only use it to compare your roasts on your machine." — *Where is RoR useful*
3. **C3** — With constant heat and airflow, a hot-air roaster naturally shows a steadily declining RoR, because heat transfer falls as the air–bean temperature difference shrinks.
   > "Constantly declining Rate of Rise is a natural behavior in a roaster anyway" — *section heading*
4. **C4** — Small RoR fluctuations are likely below sensory threshold; their data show professionals struggle to detect small profile differences at equal colour.
   > "small variations in the roast profile typically cannot be detected by anybody, not even skilled coffee professionals as long as the color of the coffee is the same." — *Improvising…*
5. **C5** — Don't improvise heat mid-roast; late corrections don't undo earlier decisions. Follow Plan–Execute–Learn and adjust the next roast.
   > "we in the CoffeeMind roasting training tell people to NEVER IMPROVISE but follow the iterative and circular process of ‘Plan-Execute-Learn’." — *Improvising…*
6. **C6** — RoR noise is amplified by derivative calculation and smoothing; visual bumps overstate real differences.
   > "You don’t taste the curve - you taste the coffee!" — *We already have the data!*
7. **C7** — RoR curve shapes depend on probe type and placement, so they transfer poorly between machines; time, temperature and colour transfer better.
   > "you get very different shapes of RoR curves due to probe type and placement" — *Difficult to compare…*
8. **C8** — In experiments (e.g. airflow, drum speed), keep end colour equal; otherwise colour confounds the result.
   > "Colour is in this scenario a confounding factor on the decisions on the cupping table" — *Ceteris paribus*
9. **C9** — First test whether a difference is perceptible at all (discrimination test) before building theories around it.
   > "the very first criteria for even spending time with the theory is to test if the claims of the theory makes any perceivable sensory difference at all!" — *Pangborn’s Razor*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The method advice (plan–execute–learn, equal colour, one variable,
discrimination tests) is machine-independent and very relevant for a home roaster learning
fast. His physics of "naturally declining RoR" is for hot-air roasters; on IR the natural shape
may be flatter (S0002). Agrees with S0001 on not micromanaging heat.
