# S0020 — Bean Temperature at First Crack (Home-Barista)

**Author:** five Home-Barista members · **Type:** forum thread (5 posts, June 2025) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/bean-temperature-at-first-crack-t99923.html)

## Summary
A short thread asking whether a fast, hot profile raises the bean-probe reading at first crack
compared with a slow approach to crack (because the probe reads the bean surface while crack
depends on the interior). Experienced roasters split: one says FC temperature is very roaster-,
bean- and profile-dependent and enters FC at 10–20 °F/min with BT of 392–405 °F; two others
(Quest M3/M6, Aillio Bullet) report that FC happens at essentially the same reading, ~200 °C ±1–3,
for all beans over hundreds of batches, and that probe type and placement matter far more than the
bean. Also discussed: light-roast development of 30 s–1:30 and closing the inner/outer temperature
gap in late Maillard rather than in development.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The original poster's hypothesis (untested): a fast/hot approach might give a higher probe reading at FC than a slow one, because the probe reads the bean surface while FC depends on the inside temperature.
   > "I'm wondering if a fast and hot profile would lead to a higher bean temp at first crack vs. a slower profile due to the temperature gradient between the inside and outside of the bean." — *#1, FreudianSip*
2. **C2** — On a Quest M3/M6, FC starts at the same reading for all beans — 200 °C, within a degree or two, over hundreds of batches; probe type and placement change the apparent FC temperature far more than the bean does.
   > "my experience is that 1Cs always occurs at the same temperature, regardless of the type of bean." — *#4, Nunas*
   > "differences in the type of probe and its placement have much more effect on the apparent temperature of 1Cs than does different beans. On my Quests, it is consistently at 200C =/- no more than a degree or two over hundreds of batches." — *#4, Nunas*
3. **C3** — The same on an Aillio Bullet across many origins: FC at 200 °C ± 2–3.
   > "They all hit first crack at 200C +/- 2 or 3" — *#5, lagoon*
4. **C4** — Contrary view: absolute FC temperature is very roaster-, bean- and profile-dependent; on a 1 kg drum with 1–2 lb charges and moderate airflow FC is fairly stable, entered at 10–20 °F/min RoR with BT 392–405 °F; when he can't hear it he marks FC at 395 °F.
   > "As for absolute first crack temperatures, this is very roaster, bean and profile dependent." — *#3, EddyQ*
   > "For me, I typically enter FC with a RoR between 10 and 20F/min and this results with a bean temp between 392 and 405F." — *#3, EddyQ*
5. **C5** — Light-roast development of 0:30–1:30 with drop at BT 405–410 °F (EddyQ); 30 s–1 min after gliding into FC at ~10–12 °F/min (Milligan). Some roasters drop before FC.
   > "Drop 0:30 to 1:30 and with BT 405-410." — *#3, EddyQ*
   > "Then start slowing down half way through to glide into FC around 10-12F/min. Then give it 30s to 1min depending on the coffee." — *#2, Milligan*
6. **C6** — The 20–25 % development rule is a starting point, not gospel; the firmer part is not to exceed 25 %, and light roasts may suffer even at 20 %.
   > "20% dev is like a 30s 1:2 shot. It's a good starting spot but not gospel." — *#2, Milligan*
   > "The only thing gospel about 20-25% is to not exceed 25% or you will loose some of the coffees goodness (light roasts may suffer even with 20%)." — *#3, EddyQ*
7. **C7** — Close the gap between inner and outer bean temperature at the end of Maillard rather than relying on development time for it.
   > "The key is to close the gap between inner and outer bean temp during the end of Maillard instead of relying on development for that." — *#2, Milligan*
8. **C8** — Entering FC with high RoR and dropping very quickly to stay light gave Milligan uneven roasts; on some coffees he can't even hear crack before dropping.
   > "to get a light roast with a high ROR entry to FC, you have to discharge quickly which tends to give an uneven roast for me." — *#2, Milligan*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Two experienced users with consistent machines see FC at the same reading
for every bean, which makes the owner's honey ~190 °C vs washed ~184 °C (gap #3) look more like a
measurement/profile effect than a bean property — unless the IR heating makes the surface–interior
gap larger than on these convection machines (C1, untested). Light-roast developments of 30 s–1:30
from experienced roasters support the owner's ~1:00 not being unusual for light roasts (gap #2).
