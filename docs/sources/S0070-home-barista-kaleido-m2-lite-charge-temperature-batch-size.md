# S0070 — Home-Barista: charge temperature, batch size and a Kaleido M2 Lite

**Author:** BKSinAZ2 and others · **Type:** forum thread (17 posts, both pages read) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** IR/hybrid (OP's Kaleido) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/question-from-new-coffee-roaster-charge-temperatures-t104791.html)

## Summary
A new roaster asks how to choose charge temperature and reveals he roasts 1 lb on a Kaleido M2 Lite with a 12:52 roast and an 8 min dry end. Replies say charge temperature depends first on batch size and on the machine's thermal mass, and several experienced posters tell him to use about 70 % of rated capacity (350 g), preheat above the charge, and compare two roasts of one coffee at different charge temperatures. A Kaleido-using reply describes the machine as a hybrid of heat sources. The OP never reports a result or cup notes.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Charge temperature depends first on batch size, second on bean density.
   > "Charge temp varies first and foremost with batch size."
   > "higher density beans can typically handle a higher charge temp without scorching, but the effect may not be significant enough to need to change the charge temp"
   — *post #3, FreudianSip*
2. **C2** — More beans need more heat; with a small batch a high charge temperature sends the RoR very high (one poster's gas roaster, 225 g).
   > "If I used a charge temp that high R0R would go through the roof using 225g of beans."
   > "The more beans you charge the more heat you need."
   — *post #2, mkane*
3. **C3** — A rule of thumb for good control on an electric roaster: about 70 % of rated capacity, which for a 500 g rating is 350 g.
   > "the sweet spot for good control and roast results on a roaster is typically 70% of rated capacity so I'd recommend you try 350 g batches"
   — *post #12, yakster*
4. **C4** — Reading the OP's profile: eight minutes to dry end is long, and a mid-roast power increase with no RoR change means too little headroom.
   > "eight minutes to dry end seems quite long and I see the power level increase midroast with little to no change in the ROR, I think you need more headroom to control the roast."
   — *post #12, yakster*
5. **C5** — A counter-example: one member's 600 g roaster works best near 460 g (77 % of rating); a 12:52 roast is called long, and a smaller batch should bring it near 9 minutes.
   > "my 600 gram roaster can handle 500 grams but works best at around 1 pound (460 grams)"
   > "Is the roast time 12:52? That is pretty long. Again, smaller batch will help you get that closer to 9 minutes or so."
   — *post #13, LBIespresso*
6. **C6** — A preheat routine for repeatable first and later batches.
   > "Try heating to 30-40 degrees above charge then letting it fall to 30 below charge"
   — *post #13, LBIespresso (unit not stated; the OP uses F)*
7. **C7** — Thermal mass varies between roasters; for small home and sample roasters charge temperature mostly affects the early roast.
   > "One variable that I think about is that some roasters have a lot of thermal mass and some do not."
   — *post #6, LBIespresso*
8. **C8** — A view from a poster who roasts on a traditional Probat-style machine, not on a Kaleido: first find the machine's maximum RoR at maximum heat while varying airflow, then aim to land end of drying at 4-5 minutes; his description of the Kaleido's heat sources is second-hand.
   > "If using a Kaleido for the first time I would want to know the maximum ROR the machine could deliver."
   > "How to land EOD 4-5 minutes?"
   — *post #14, hankua*
9. **C9** — The OP's own numbers: preheat and charge were both 370 F.
   > "My preheat and charge temperature was 370 fahrenheit."
   — *post #11, BKSinAZ2*
10. **C10** — Advice to run controlled tests: same coffee, different charge temperatures; charge temperature is one of the last things to change to fix a roast.
    > "Also would be helpful to post 2 of the same coffee with different charge temps."
    > "charge temp may be where you start the roast but it is among the last things I would look at to fix my roast."
    — *post #10, LBIespresso*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The 70 %-of-rating rule gives 350 g for a 500 g rating, the same figure the owner uses; with the V1 rated 400-500 g by different sources, 350 g is 70-88 % of the rating, so the rule neither confirms nor warns against it. The only IR-type data point is a Kaleido M2 Lite roasted 1 lb with a long dry end, a different machine from the Skywalker. Charge-temperature advice here is for drums and the thread has no light-roast or cup data (gap 10 asks for these).
