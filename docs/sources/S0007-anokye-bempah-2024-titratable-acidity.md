# S0007 — Roast profiles and titratable acidity dynamics (Anokye-Bempah et al., 2024)

**Author:** Anokye-Bempah et al. (UC Davis) · **Type:** peer-reviewed paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-57256-y)

## Summary
Seven deliberately different roast profiles (fast start, slow start, medium, production,
exaggerated flick, negative RoR, extended Maillard), all 16 min long and ending at ~237 °C, were
sampled every minute (39 roasts, 663 samples) and titratable acidity (TA) of the brew was
measured. TA rose from the start, peaked during first crack, and fell back to about its initial
value by second crack. Peak TA was nearly identical across profiles (~4.9 mL NaOH/40 mL) and
origins, but the timing of the rise and the final TA differed by profile.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Titratable acidity always peaks during first crack, then decays to about its original value by second crack.
   > "the TA invariably peaks during first crack and then decays to its original value by second crack." — *Abstract*
2. **C2** — Peak TA showed almost no significant differences between roast profiles (avg 4.9 ± 0.2 mL NaOH/40 mL).
   > "the peak TA surprisingly exhibited almost no statistically significant differences among roast profiles." — *Abstract*
3. **C3** — Peak TA appears to depend on the beans' inherent composition rather than profile.
   > "the peak TA does not depend on the roast profile but may depend on other factors" — *Results*
4. **C4** — Final TA differed by profile: fast start, negative RoR and medium ended lower (~1.2 ± 0.1) than extended Maillard, production, slow start and exaggerated flick (~1.7 ± 0.2).
   > "The FS, NROR, and MD roast profiles had low final TA values with an average of 1.2 ± 0.1 mL NaOH/40 mL sample" — *Results*
5. **C5** — The roast profile changes the dynamics (timing) of TA development, so profile can be used to steer sourness — mainly via where you stop relative to first crack.
   > "Our results provide insight on how to manipulate and achieve desired sourness during roasting." — *Abstract*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The key finding (acidity peaks at first crack and falls with further
roasting) is chemistry and should transfer to any heat source: for brighter coffee, drop
relatively soon after first crack. These roasts were long and very dark (to second crack), so
the "final" values don't describe light roasts. TA ≠ perceived acidity (not measured).
