# S0021 — Development time of light roasts (Home-Barista)

**Author:** several Home-Barista members · **Type:** forum thread (18 posts, May 2021) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/development-time-light-roasts-t73248.html)

## Summary
A roaster finds that aiming for 20 % development gives him roasts closer to full city than light.
Experienced members answer that their light roasts sit around 13–16 % DTR (~1:20–1:30 after FC),
that development percentage is not a good drop criterion (drop on temperature or colour instead),
that Rao himself now plays DTR down, and that the same development time means different things
depending on total roast speed and RoR at first crack. One member found slow (<10 °F/min) entry
into FC to reach 20 % still tasted underdeveloped, with better results from shorter, hotter
development.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Hitting 20 % development on a 1 kg perforated drum tends to push roasts toward full city rather than light.
   > "I try and aim for a 20% dev time but I find it's difficult to get a light roast doing that and what I end up with usually is closer to a full city roast." — *#1, DamianWarS*
2. **C2** — Experienced members' light roasts land around 13–16 % DTR: drop at ~385–390 °F with FC ~370 °F (~16 %); 14–16 %, often 1:20–1:30 after FCs; one Ethiopia anaerobic natural dropped at 13 % (~10 min roast) was "stellar".
   > "I drop light roasts around 385-390*F (370* 1C), which correlates to around 16%." — *#3, Almico*
   > "correlates to 14%-16% depending on how light I want to go. Often that's 1:20-1:30 after 1Cs." — *#5, Peppersass*
   > "recently I roasted an Ethiopia Anaerobic Dry that I dropped at 13%, and it's stellar (total roast time of around 10 minutes)." — *#8, bicktrav*
3. **C3** — Development percentage is a poor drop criterion; drop on temperature or colour. Almico drops on temperature ("temp rules"); dark roasts at ~415 °F run 28–30 %.
   > "Development percentage is really a meaningless parameter." — *#3, Almico*
   > "I don't drop based on development time either. I drop on temp." — *#6, Almico*
4. **C4** — Second-hand report: Rao reportedly wishes he hadn't emphasised DTR so much and now advises dropping by colour; DTR remains useful for planning gas reductions that avoid flicks.
   > "He seems to wish he hadn't emphasized DTR as much as he did in Coffee Roaster's Companion and now advises that roasters drop based on color rather than percentage." — *#8, bicktrav*
5. **C5** — The same development time is not equivalent across roast speeds: a 1:15 development after an 8:00 roast is "more developed" than 1:15 after a 12:00 roast at the same drop temperature (Almico's view, endorsed by Chert).
   > "A 1:15 drop for an 8:00 roast is more \"developed\" than a 1:15 drop for a 12:00 roast, given the same drop temperature." — *#6, Almico*
6. **C6** — Momentum at FC changes what a given development time means: entering FC at 16 vs 8 RoR, 1 min of development in the first may match 2 min in the second for colour and drop temperature (though they won't taste the same). DTR only compares cleanly between identical profiles.
   > "If you hit first crack at 16 ROR vs 8 ROR then a 2 minute development for each of these will result in drastically different outcomes." — *#16, Vince_in_Montreal*
   > "Again if your profiles are identical, then DTR matters." — *#16, Vince_in_Montreal*
7. **C7** — Stretching to 20 % by entering FC below 10 °F/min kept drop temperature light but often still tasted undeveloped; shorter, hotter development gave better results for that roaster.
   > "If I drop my rate of rise below 10*F/minute at FCs I can get 20% development and still keep the drop temp in the \"light roast\" range. The thing is, though, it still often tastes undeveloped" — *#4, JNick916*
   > "I've had better results with shorter and hotter development phases." — *#4, JNick916*
8. **C8** — Practice examples: light Ethiopians not past 1:30 development, drop ~7–10 °C above FC (FC 196 °C → drop 203–206 °C on a 500 g solid drum); a Huky light filter roast with FC at 8:00–10:00 and 1:30–2:00 development, ~15 °F rise, to avoid grassiness.
   > "I don't usually run light roast Ethiopians past 1:30 roast development time" — *#9, hankua*
   > "If 1C was at 196c, then 203-206c would be in the ballpark for my solid drum 500g roaster." — *#9, hankua*
   > "I usually hit 1C at 8:00-10:00 and aim for 1:30-2:00 of development with as minimal temp increase as possible during post 1C development." — *#17, STG*
9. **C9** — Development also happens during Maillard; lengthening or shortening it changes flavour (from an SCA 2018 panel, as relayed).
   > "especially the concept that development takes place in Maillard, too, and lengthening or shorting it can bring out different flavors and characteristics." — *#5, Peppersass*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Supports gap #2: experienced light roasters commonly sit at 13–16 % DTR and
~1:20–1:30 development, shorter than Rao's 20–25 % (S0018) — and C4 suggests Rao himself softened
the rule. The owner's ~1:00 / 7–12 % is still shorter than these. C6/C7 matter on the Skywalker,
where RoR at FC is typically low (6–7 °C/min, S0015 C9): low momentum means the same development
time does less, so short development may suit it better than stretching.
