# S0048 — Smrke et al. 2022: how to store coffee after the bag is opened

**Author:** Smrke, Adam, Mühlemann, Lantz, Yeretzian · **Type:** paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/26687/3/2022_Smrke-etal_Effects-of-different-coffee-storage-methods-on-coffee-freshness_FPSL.pdf)

## Summary
Roasted Arabica whole beans from one roast batch (Tchibo, Hamburg) in nitrogen-filled, valved foil bags were rested 3 weeks, then opened and kept for 9 weeks with four ways of closing or re-housing them: the bag's screw cap, a clip, a canister, and tape. Five parallel bags per method were sampled twice a week (49 g each) and freshness was tracked with volatile-compound ratios by GC/MS. By the 2-butanone/2-methylfuran index the screw cap kept freshness best and the canister (beans transferred out of the bag) worst, with all pairwise differences significant. Headspace oxygen rose to air-like levels inside every opened package. There was no sensory work, and the authors say an acceptance threshold still needs sensory studies. Roast degree and origin are not given.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The bag's screw cap was the best of the four storage methods tested.
   > "The screw cap packaging was the best performing storage method." — *Abstract*
2. **C2** — Moving beans into a container made them lose freshness faster than keeping them in the original package.
   > "transferring beans into a container resulted in a faster loss of freshness" — *Abstract*
3. **C3** — Slope of the 2-butanone/2-methylfuran index (higher = faster loss): canister 0.06669, tape 0.05827, clip 0.03987, screw cap 0.03269; the unopened reference was stable with a slight downward slope (-0.0099, p < 0.001). Slopes differed significantly between any two methods (p < 0.001).
4. **C4** — Once opened, the headspace inside the package is replaced by air regardless of closure.
   > "the whole HS inside the packaging is rapidly replaced by air" — *Section 3.4 (CO2 and O2 content)*
5. **C5** — Methanethiol fell to about 15 % of its start within eight weeks in every opened package (about 33 % in the unopened reference), so that marker did not separate closures; dimethyl disulfide rose, mostly in the first three weeks, as an oxidation product.
6. **C6** — Hexanal rose only slightly; the authors judged it too small to serve as a practical staling marker.
7. **C7** — The authors say chemical results need sensory confirmation.
   > "sensory studies need to be conducted in addition to chemical analyses" — *Conclusion*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Roaster-independent. For a home roaster who stores 350 g batches in small containers, this is one of few replicated, measured comparisons of storage after opening: the original valved bag with its closure beat a transferred canister on the chemical index. Limits: the beans were a commercial roast in a nitrogen-flushed bag, not a home roast, the container was not purged, and without sensory data "faster loss of the index" is not "worse taste".
