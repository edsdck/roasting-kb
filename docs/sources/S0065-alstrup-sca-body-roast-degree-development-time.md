# S0065 — Alstrup (SCA 25 Magazine): body, roast degree and development time

**Author:** Jesper Alstrup · **Type:** magazine article on a master's-thesis experiment · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** n/a (machine not stated) · [Original](https://sca.coffee/sca-news/25-magazine/issue-5/modulating-perception-25-magazine-issue-5-dxzar)

## Summary
A trained 10-person panel tasted nine roasts of one coffee in blind booths. Five roasts changed only colour (Agtron 98 to darker) at a fixed development time of about 2:15; four changed only development time (shortest 1:15, longest 6:32) at a fixed colour of Agtron 75. Colour changed the cup clearly (lightest: acidic, dried fruit; darker: bitter, gritty, roasted). Across the development-time roasts the differences were "much smaller", and body in particular did not rise with longer development, which disproved the starting hypothesis. The text gives no means or p-values; the figures are images. The shortest development tested (1:15) is the closest controlled value to the owner's ~1:00.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Experts said extending development at the same colour adds body; this is what the study set out to test.
   > "extending the roast development time was a method to increase body"
   — *The Experiments / Intro, paragraph on expert beliefs*
2. **C2** — Design: nine roasts; colour roasts keep development near 2:15; development roasts keep colour at Agtron 75; the medium roast (Agtron 75, 2:15) is the shared anchor.
   > "having a medium roast degree of Agtron 75 and a development time of 2 min. 15 sec."
   — *Fig. 1 caption*
3. **C3** — Development was defined as first crack to the end of the roast, and the range tested was 1:15 to 6:32 at one colour.
   > "our shortest roast development time was 1 min. 15 sec., and our longest was 6 min. 32 sec."
   — *Development Time Modulations*
4. **C4** — Across those development times the panel found much smaller differences than across colours, and body did not change much; the hypothesis that body rises with development was not supported.
   > "We found much smaller differences in the sensory perception of each sample."
   > "body was not perceived as much different throughout the development time modulations"
   — *Development Time Modulations*
5. **C5** — Sweetness and bitterness were among the attributes that did not differ significantly across the development-time roasts (the figure caption says so; which attributes did differ is only in the image).
   > "Other attributes were included in the study, such as sweetness and bitterness, but they were not statistically significantly different."
   — *Fig. 3 caption*
6. **C6** — Colour effect at fixed development: the lightest roast (Agtron 98) was rated high in acidity and dried fruit and low in bitter and roasted; darker roasts gained bitter, gritty and roasted and lost acidity and fruit.
   > "Reducing the Agtron number (i.e. roasting darker) led to increased perceptions of"
   — *Roast Degree Modulations*
7. **C7** — Body followed a hump over colour, but the decline at darker roasts was not statistically significant.
   > "this decline in body did not prove to be statistically significant in this study"
   — *Roast Degree Modulations*
8. **C8** — Measured viscosity was lowest in the darkest coffee and peaked near Agtron 75, but the author says this does not prove a link to perceived body.
   > "the darkest coffee having by far the lowest viscosity of all the samples"
   — *Viscosity Measurement*
9. **C9** — Panel method: 10 trained panelists, blind, private booths, continuous line scale, 10 attributes tracked.
   > "Our panel of 10 participants evaluated each sample in a private, blind setting"
   — *Sensory Descriptive Analysis*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* This is the nearest controlled sensory test to the owner's ~1:00 development, but it stops at 1:15, is at medium colour (Agtron 75), not light, and has no "grassy" or "underdeveloped" attribute, so it cannot say whether 1:00 at a light colour tastes raw. It does show that at equal colour a 1:15 roast was not rated very different from longer ones on body, and that sweetness and bitterness did not differ significantly. Machine, batch size and the first-crack mark are not stated, so the times cannot be translated to a Skywalker probe reading.

## Gatherer notes
- Summarises a master's-thesis experiment; the full thesis was not found. The article text has no means, standard deviations or p-values; Figs. 2-4 are images that were not read.
- Companion to S0006 (same research group; four development times 90-390 s at Agtron 76, 46 tasters, shorter = sweeter and more acidic). The two do not obviously agree on sweetness: S0006 found a development effect, this article's Fig. 3 caption says sweetness and bitterness did not differ significantly. The difference may be panel size, colour (Agtron 75 vs 76), profile design or the coffee; the article does not say.
