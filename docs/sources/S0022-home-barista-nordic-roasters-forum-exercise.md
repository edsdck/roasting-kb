# S0022 — Nordic Roasters Forum roasting exercise (Home-Barista)

**Author:** several Home-Barista members · **Type:** forum thread (15 posts, Oct 2012 – Apr 2013) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/nordic-roasters-forum-roasting-presentation-and-roasting-exercise-t23553.html)

## Summary
Discussion of a 2012 Nordic Roasters Forum session where four Scandinavian roasteries roasted the
same Kenya. Viewers were surprised by very short development — about 45–47 s from first crack to
end for Tim Wendelboe's roast — achieved with very high airflow throughout. Home roasters then
tried it: short (1:15–1:47) finishes on washed Kenyas did not taste underroasted if final
temperature was reasonable; another found a sweet, clear Kenya at ~1:30 from FC; a third found a
2-minute finish made a Gesha sweet and acidic but lost its floral/spice notes. One presenting
roaster later said "Nordic style" is really about buying top coffees and highlighting them, and that
he chose a longer drying phase for a dry, lower-quality Kenya.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — In the presented roasts, post-crack development was very short; Tim Wendelboe's was about 45 s from FC to end of roast.
   > "Its safe to say Ive never had a roast with 47 seconds of development that tasted great." — *#2, dustin360*
   > "I was really shocked to see Tim Wendelboe's roast and the 45sec 1C-EOR development." — *#5, MSH*
2. **C2** — As relayed from Wendelboe's talk: such short roasts need very high airflow for the whole roast (on their Probat) to develop properly; Kenyans respond well, Brazilians would not.
   > "since they were using such a short roast time that they have to utilize extremely high airflow for the entire roast on their Probat to get the proper development." — *#5, MSH*
   > "He also indicated that Kenyans typically respond very well to this type of profile (in their specific roaster), but that it would not work well with a Brazilian for example." — *#5, MSH*
3. **C3** — Home trials: three washed Kenyas with 1:15–1:47 finishes on a Quest didn't taste underroasted; conclusion that very short finishes work on washed coffees if final temperature is reasonable. The best of that batch, though, had a 2:30 finish (the roaster prefers sweetness to acidity).
   > "none of them tasted underroasted" — *#8, dustin360*
   > "it taught me that as long as your final temp is reasonable, you can do ridiculous short finish phases on washed coffees." — *#8, dustin360*
   > "Actually the best of the batch is one I still did \"fast\" but with a 2:30 min finish" — *#10, dustin360*
4. **C4** — A Kenya taken from FC at 9:00 (392 °F) to drop at 10:30 (410 °F) was very sweet, juicy and clear; holding airflow high was key — reducing airflow instead of adding power gave terrible results. An accidental 45 s finish (9:00 → 9:45 at 410 °F) on a mediocre Mexico tasted grainy.
   > "I took from 9min 392F first to 10:30 410F dump.
   > I like it along. Very sweet and juicy." — *#7, endlesscycles*
   > "I've done something like this in the past by dropping airflow instead of raising power and got absolutely terrible results. Holding air on high seems to be the ticket." — *#9, endlesscycles*
   > "9:00 first, 9:45 finish @ 410F. It was as I expected: grainy." — *#3, endlesscycles*
5. **C5** — Short finishes shift flavour toward sweetness and acidity: a Bench Maji Gesha moved from a 3.5-min to a 2-min finish (2.5 °C lower drop) became sweet and acidic but lost its floral and spice notes.
   > "I am surprised that it made the Bench Maji Gesha sweet and acidic." … "It has lost all the spice and floral notes I enjoyed before." — *#13, tamarian*
6. **C6** — Dry-processed coffees seemed to do better with shorter development for one roaster (2 min was his shortest at the time).
   > "dry processed beans seem to do better with shorter RD(roast development)." — *#2, dustin360*
7. **C7** — One presenting roaster: "Nordic style" is historically about buying the best coffees and roasting to highlight them (today usually short light roasts); for a dry, lower-quality Kenya he chose a longer drying phase to get a fully developed cup.
   > "There is no longer any nordic style of roasting." — *#14, rocklobster*
   > "I did a moisture analysis and visual check, my conclusion was that it was a bit dry low quality kenya. I then decided on a profile with a longer \"drying\" part" — *#14, rocklobster*
8. **C8** — Short-development light roasts suit only very good coffees, in one roaster's view.
   > "However, most coffees aren't good enough to shine at this level." — *#12, endlesscycles*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The strongest practitioner precedent for the owner's ~1:00 development
(gap #2): top roasters used ~45 s on a high-quality washed Kenya. But it came with very high
airflow — which the Skywalker can't use as a heat-transfer tool (air mostly cools, S0017 C7) —
and renatoa's experience is that fast Skywalker roasts came out underdeveloped inside (S0015 C10).
Coffee type matters (C2, C6, C8).

## Gatherer notes
- Core numbers come from viewers of a video, not the presenters; the 1h35 video itself wasn't watched.
- rocklobster says the wrong profile was shown on screen during the presentation, so viewers' reading of specific profiles may be off.
