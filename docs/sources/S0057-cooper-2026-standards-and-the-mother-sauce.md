# S0057 — Cooper 2026: proposed phase-time recipe standards for a light roast, from blind tastings

**Author:** Anne Cooper · **Type:** tasting report / article (PDF) · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.eqmr.com.au/_files/ugd/5d1111_5466a213fe04470b9eb4e4152287679e.pdf)

## Summary
At Roast Summit Melbourne (March 2026) attendees tasted one washed Costa Rica roasted 13 different ways, blind, mostly batch-brewed on a Moccamaster with some samples cupped. From three tasting sets (sample roasts, the effect of time, roast levels) the author proposes "recipe" standards for the Oceania region: a light roast of 8:30-9:30 total at 800 micron ground Agtron 85-88, with phases of 4:00-4:30 / 3:00-3:30 / 1:00-1:20 (preparation to yellow, Maillard to first crack, development after first crack; see claims for the post-first-crack finish), and a sample roast of 7:30-9:00 at Agtron 80-85. Preferred roasts were the faster ones in the time set, and region palates leaned towards light-medium. No scores or taster counts are given, so the proposals are a practitioner judgement, not measured results.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Design: one coffee, 13 roasts, blind tastings.
   > "We had roasters do a set of blind tastings of the same coffee, a washed Costa Rica, roasted 13 different ways" — *Method*
2. **C2** — In the sample-roast set, C and D were favoured over A and B (A is the SCA-style sample roast).
   > "It can be clearly seen that samples C and D were favoured over A and B." — *Sample roast set*
3. **C3** — In the time set, B (fast, bright, tart) and D (balanced) were preferred, C close behind, and A (slow) least; the long roast showed as a negative in cupping but not necessarily brewed.
   > "the length of the roast did show up as a negative flavour but not necessarily in the brewed tastings." — *Impact of time set*
4. **C4** — Proposed light roast: 8:30-9:30 total, Agtron 85-88 (800 micron ground), phases 4:00-4:30 / 3:00-3:30 / 1:00-1:20; a larger batch may need 30 s to 1:00 longer but the last two phases stay the same once at yellow; a 3:30 Maillard phase is floated as a minimum standard.
   > "An 8:30 - 9:30 total roast time" — *Light Roast recipe*
   > "A larger batch size may need 30sec to 1:00 longer, but once at yellow, the times for the last 2 phases must be the same." — *Light Roast recipe*
5. **C5** — Proposed post-first-crack finish for the light roast, by process: washed +4-5 degrees in 1:20, honey +4-5 in 1:00, natural +4-5 in 45 s-1:00, experimental +4-5 in 45 s. The unit of "degrees" is not given anywhere in the document; a heading calls them "Development Degrees (to help determine an end temperature from your specific probe)", so they are read on the roaster's own probe.
   > "Washed: +4-5 degrees post marked FC in 1:20" — *Light Roast recipe*
6. **C6** — Proposed sample roast: 7:30-9:00 total, Agtron 80-85, phases 3:00-4:30 / 3:30 / 0:45-1:00, +3 degrees post-FC in 1:00 (washed, honey), 45 s (natural), 30-45 s (experimental).
7. **C7** — First crack is to be called at consecutive pops, and development ratio should not be the only data point.
   > "first crack was called when consecutive pops were heard, not the first," — *Standards*
   > "Dev. ratio % is a very inconsistent data point" — *Standards*
8. **C8** — Palates in the region lean light-medium.
   > "palate preferences for the Oceania region are erring more toward the Light-Medium range." — *Roast Levels set*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A recent, recipe-style target for very bright light roasts: about 1:00-1:20 of development, and a post-first-crack rise of only a few degrees. That is shorter than most sources in the base and is relevant to gap 1 (short development), with the same limits: unknown machine, unknown number of tasters, no scores, and the "degrees" are whatever the roaster's own probe reads, so the temperature rise cannot be compared to a Skywalker bean probe directly. Phase times can be compared directly.
