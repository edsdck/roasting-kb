# S0064 — Vezzulli et al. 2023: volatile fingerprints of origin, process and roast level (46 specialty lots)

**Author:** Vezzulli, Lambri, Bertuzzi · **Type:** paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** fluid-bed (IKAWA V2-PRO) · [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9914344)

## Summary
Forty-six lots of specialty Arabica (cupping score 80 or more, no primary defects; 17 dry, 4 semi-dry and 25 wet processed) were analysed green and after roasting at three levels on an IKAWA V2-PRO (50 g): light at 205 °C in 5.46 min, medium at 210 °C in 6.16 min, dark at 214 °C in 6.46 min (as printed). That gave 138 roasted samples, analysed by HS-SPME/GC-MS. In green coffee, origin separated samples better than process; hexanal was higher in washed lots. In roasted coffee, composition differed first by roast level, then origin, then process, and ten compounds (pyridine, furfural, maltol and others) rose across all three levels while one floral pyrazine fell. Roast level on its own could not be classified to the authors' 90 % standard until samples were grouped by origin or process; the lightest roast kept the most of the green-coffee (fermentation/drying) profile. No sensory data, colour or weight loss for the roasts appear in the parts read.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Design: 50 g batches on an IKAWA V2-PRO, three roast profiles, all lots from specialty suppliers.
   > "The sample roaster IKAWA Model V2-PRO was used to roast all the coffee samples" — *2.2 Roasting*
   > "Each roasting batch was 50 g (±0.5 g)." — *2.2 Roasting*
2. **C2** — The three profiles.
   > "Light roasting was achieved at 205 °C in 5.46 min, medium roasting at 210 °C in 6.16 min, and dark roasting at 214 °C in 6.46 min." — *2.2 Roasting*
   > "The chamber was preheated to 174–175 °C before the coffee was dropped in." — *2.2 Roasting*
3. **C3** — Ten compounds were discriminant for all three roast levels and rose with roast level; only one (3,5-diethyl-2-methylpyrazine, floral) fell.
   > "the abundance of 10 compounds was discriminant for each of the three roasting" — *3.2 Volatile Compounds in Roasted Coffee*
   > "Only one compound (3,5-dietil-2-metil pyrazine; floral odor) decreased with an increasing roasting level." — *3.2*
4. **C4** — Ordering of effects on roasted-coffee composition.
   > "In roasted coffees, the differences in composition were due to roasting levels, countries of origin, and the post-harvesting processes, in descending order of significance." — *Abstract*
5. **C5** — Roast level alone was not classified well: under 90 % correct association; best discrimination was within one origin/process, and on the light roast, because the lighter the roast the more of the fermentation/drying profile is kept.
   > "the lighter the roast, the more preserved the" — *4.2 Discrimination* (the sentence continues "acidic and volatile profile deriving from the fermentation/drying phases")
6. **C6** — Hexanal was more concentrated in washed green coffees.
   > "hexanal was more concentrated in washed varieties (p < 0.05)" — *Abstract*
7. **C7** — Origin has a larger effect on the volatile profile than post-harvest process, and these differences persist in roasted coffee.
   > "It is confirmed that the origin has a stronger impact on the volatile profile than the post-harvesting process." — *5. Conclusions*
8. **C8** — The authors say the link between green and roasted volatiles still needs study.
   > "Further studies are needed to identify the correlation between the volatile profiles of green and roasted coffee" — *5. Conclusions*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Weak for roasting practice, useful for gap 14 (roast chemistry): hexanal (the raw/vegetal marker suggested in S0023) is higher in washed green coffees, so a "grassy" cup from a washed lot is not necessarily the profile's fault. It also shows that the roast level, not the batch's roasting history, is the main source of volatile differences, and that light roasts keep the green coffee's fermentation and drying signature. A 50 g hot-air sample roast is far from a 350 g lamp roaster, and no sensory or colour data link these profiles to what a Skywalker owner tastes.
