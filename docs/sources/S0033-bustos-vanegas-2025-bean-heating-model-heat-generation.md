# S0033 — Modeling and simulation of coffee bean heating during roasting: effect of heat generation

**Author:** Bustos-Vanegas et al. · **Type:** paper (Frontiers in Food Science and Technology, 2025, open access) · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** gas drum, single bean at constant air temperature · [Original](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/food-science-and-technology/articles/10.3389/frfst.2025.1603783/full)

## Summary
The authors build a lumped (single-temperature-per-bean) model of bean heating that includes the heat taken up by evaporation and the heat released by exothermic reactions. They fit it to measured temperature curves of single Catuaí Vermelho beans roasted at constant air temperatures of 200, 220, 240, 260 and 280 °C in a gas-fired drum roaster, and use the fit to estimate an overall heat transfer coefficient. The fitted overall coefficient is several times the calculated convective one, from which they conclude that radiation is the dominant heat transfer mechanism in this roaster. Evaporation affects the modelled temperature more than the exothermic reactions do, and the exothermic term starts at about 150 °C. No sensory work.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Method: one bean per run, held on a thermocouple at the centre of a gas-fired drum, at five constant air temperatures; Catuaí Vermelho, dry process, sieve 18 and above.
   > "A direct gas-burning roaster (Rod Bel, 4 burners) with a rotating cylinder at 45 rpm was used for the roasting operation." — *Methods*
   > "Processed coffee beans (dry process), Coffea arabica L., Catuaí Vermelho cultivar, with size above sieve 18 were used." — *Methods*
2. **C2** — The fitted model reproduces the measured bean temperatures well (R² 0.973–0.994).
   > "The temperatures estimated by the model showed R2 values between 0.973 and 0.994 at different roasting temperatures." — *Abstract*
3. **C3** — The overall heat transfer coefficient (convection plus radiation) is far above the convective one; the authors conclude radiation dominates.
   > "its value indicates that radiation is the governing phenomena." — *Results, discussion of Table 2*
   > "allowing one to find that the dominant heat transfer mechanism is radiation." — *Conclusions, point 3*
4. **C4** — The convective coefficient is small because the air moves slowly inside the rotating drum, and it changes little with temperature, while the overall coefficient rises strongly with roasting temperature.
   > "and its value is low because of the low air velocity that is generated by the rotation of the roasting drum." — *Results*
   > "increased exponentially with the temperature of the process," — *Results*
5. **C5** — Evaporation (endothermic) affects the modelled bean temperature more than the exothermic reactions; ignoring evaporation overestimates temperature in the exothermic period that starts at 150 °C, ignoring the exothermic term underestimates it.
   > "evaporation has a greater effect on the temperature estimated by the model" — *Conclusions, point 2*
   > "By contrast, when exothermic reactions are ignored, the model underestimates the temperature of the bean" — *Abstract*
6. **C6** — The authors recommend a low initial moisture content to make the roast easier to control.
   > "a low initial moisture content in the beans is recommended." — *Conclusions, point 2*
7. **C7** — The first stage is heating to about 100 °C with moisture roughly constant.
   > "It can be seen that the first step corresponds to the grain heating up to reach approximately 100°C," — *Results*
8. **C8** — The lumped approach is borderline: the Biot numbers (0.48–0.82) are not small, meaning the bean's inside lags its surface; the model gives one temperature per bean.
   > "Values of the Biot number obtained for overall heat transfer coefficients were 0.48; 0.58; 0.54; 0.65 and 0.82" — *Results*
9. **C9** — Model intended for optimization and real-time control; main simplification is merging convection and radiation into one coefficient.
   > "The model can be used as an optimization and controlling tool in the roasting process." — *Abstract*
   > "in an overall heat transfer coefficient and considering a constant bean thermal conductivity." — *Conclusions, point 1*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* One of few measured sources that quantifies radiation's share of bean heating, even in a gas-fired drum where convection is weak. For a lamp-heated roaster the radiant share is plausibly larger still (inference, not tested here). Limits: a single bean at constant air temperature (not a 350 g batch with a changing profile), no sensory result, one temperature per bean (so no surface-versus-core split), and the radiation share is inferred from a coefficient comparison rather than separately measured.

## Gatherer notes
- The authors' Conclusion 3 says the overall coefficient is "between 80% and 90% higher" than the convective one. From their own Table 2 the overall values are about 3.8–5.9 times the convective values, i.e. convective is roughly 74–83 % lower. The wording and table do not match exactly; the table numbers are recorded as given.
