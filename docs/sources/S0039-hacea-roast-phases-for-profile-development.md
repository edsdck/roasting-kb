# S0039 — Roast phases for profile development (Hacea, 2026)

**Author:** Hacea Coffee Source · **Type:** blog article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://haceacoffee.com/blogs/roasting/roast-approach-roast-phases-for-profile-development)

## Summary
The writer replaces the usual drying–Maillard–first-crack view with four phases (drying, Maillard, caramelization, post-crack development) whose start and stop points are bean temperatures read from the thermocouple. Time in each phase is the roadmap; heat application sets the speed (more heat, shorter phase). Each phase is linked to a flavour effect: short Maillard for brighter acidity, longer caramelization for sweetness and body but less acidity, long post-crack for bitterness. No example numbers, profiles or cupping data are given, and the profile examples are diagrams.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Phase boundaries come from the bean thermocouple, which makes the method machine-specific; moving to another roaster means shifting the boundaries by matching sensory observations.
   > "Key to this methodology is using seed temperature (from thermocouple reading) to establish consistent and objective "start" and "stop" points for each phase." — *intro*
   > "going from the Proaster I normally use over to an Aillio bullet has a difference of -25 degrees F in the readings." — *intro*
2. **C2** — The boundaries (°F): drying until 300; Maillard 301–340; caramelization from 340 to first crack; post-crack development from first crack to the end.
   > "this phase is marked as time spent between 301 degrees Fahrenheit and 340 degrees." — *Maillard*
   > "the caramelization phase is marked as time spent between 340 degrees and first crack." — *Caramelization*
   > "It is marked as the time between first crack and the end of the roast." — *Post Crack Development*
3. **C3** — Heat controls phase speed: short phases need more heat, long phases less.
   > "To move quickly through a phase, thus a short time segment, more heat is required." — *Developing a roast profile*
4. **C4** — A short Maillard phase is the route to brighter, more intense acidity; to keep that acidity the caramelization phase must also be short, because every change is a trade-off.
   > "If I want the coffee to have a brighter and more intense acidity, I need to spend a short amount of time in Maillard." — *Dialing in a roast profile*
   > "Because roasting is the constant breakdown and reconfigurement of molecules, we know that there will always be tradeoffs in flavor." — *Dialing in a roast profile*
5. **C5** — More time in caramelization means more sweetness and body at the cost of acidity; too much gives a "bready"/baked cup.
   > "More time spent in this phase means more creation of reducing sugars like fructose and more creation of melanoidins." — *Caramelization*
   > "Spending too much time in this phase can result in coffee that is bready." — *Caramelization*
6. **C6** — More post-crack time raises bitterness and degrades acids, sugars and melanoidins.
   > "More time during this phase will mean prevalence of bitter flavors." — *Post Crack Development*
7. **C7** — Moisture claim in the text: green coffee is 9–12 % moisture and must fall to 6–7 % before Maillard starts; unreleased water in the seed centre is said to be needed for the first-crack "pop".
   > "In order for the Maillard phase to begin, the coffee must get down to between 6-7% moisture." — *Drying*
   > "This bit of moisture in the center will be critical to the audible "pop" experienced at first crack." — *Drying*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A clear statement of a four-phase, bean-temperature method and a rule of thumb (heat sets phase length) that the owner can use as a vocabulary and as hypotheses to test with Artisan phase marks. It gives no data, is from a gas drum, and says that other machines' readings differ (−25 °F Bullet vs Proaster), so the 300/340 marks must be re-found on a Skywalker via sensory checks. Not evidence that the claimed flavour effects hold on a lamp roaster.

## Gatherer notes
- Mechanism claims are asserted without references: the 6–7 % moisture threshold for Maillard, water "consolidating" in the seed centre (disputed in S0038), and fructose "up to 80% more perceived sweetness than sucrose".
- The example profiles are diagrams; no numbers were extractable. The earlier "Roast Strategy" article is cited by two later Hacea posts but is not on the first blog page and was not found.
