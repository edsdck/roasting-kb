# S0027 — Skywalker Roasters (CoffeeSnobs thread)

**Author:** TomPoland and others · **Type:** forum thread (12 posts with comments, 2026-01-30 to 2026-05-30) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** IR (owners describe the heater as a lamp inside the drum) · [Original](https://coffeesnobs.com.au/forum/coffeesnobs-discussions/home-roasting-tips-tricks-ideas/1032121-skywalker-roasters)

## Summary
Australian forum thread on the Skywalker (white-labelled; OEM said to be Foshan Akimita). It collects owner impressions of V1 (two owners at 350–400 g batches, using presets, Artisan or HiBean) and V2/Delta (two owners), including build and reliability remarks. The most useful roasting content is a light-roast exchange: one member advises very short development after FC for filter roasts, and a V2 owner posts a full 250 g Artisan schedule with targets (development 85–100 s, DTR 16–18 %, drop 192–194 °C, weight loss 12–13 %); asked how it tasted, he answers with "Nice smooth acidity and fruit" (it is not stated that this cup came from a roast that followed the schedule exactly). A V1 owner reports a good first light roast from the preset but no cup result yet.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — A V1 buyer used preset 11 for a light natural Brazilian at 350–400 g and judged the machine simple and even; the cup result was still pending.
   > "an absolute stone cold beginner can roast a very good batch if they read the manual and simply select the preset that matches the bean origin, method of process and desired roast degree" — *post #4, TomPoland, 2026-02-01*
   > "the jury is out for three weeks while I let the beans rest" — *post #4, TomPoland*
2. **C2** — A V1 owner with 45 batches of 350–400 g says the lamp-based roast needs a different mental model of the RoR curve and that Artisan auto control worked poorly for him.
   > "I found the change to be 100% positive once I got used to the idea that the RoR curve is different." — *post #7, royk1000, 2026-02-14*
   > "The Artisan auto-control did not work so well" — *post #7, royk1000*
3. **C3** — Gen 1 owner: stable at 400 g; known weak points are the in-drum heat lamp, the drum motor and the glass tube; the earlier importer called it a "beta version".
   > "very stable for a 400g load" — *post #2, KenCasAus (quoted from an earlier thread)*
   > "the heat lamp (what they call indirect fire as the lamp is within the drum) failing, drum motor failing, or even rarer the outer glass tube cracking" — *post #2, KenCasAus*
   > "it feels like a beta version at the moment" — *post #3, Andy (quoted, 2025-06-16)*
4. **C4** — The exhaust fan/filter unit is weak; a V2 owner replaced it with an external cooler straight away.
   > "The fan in the exhaust unit is, as you say, next to useless and I moved to an external cooler almost straight away." — *post #6, sorenk*
5. **C5** — Filter-style light roast advice (not from a stated Skywalker owner): drop within 3–4 degrees and 20–30 s after FC; too long gives roast notes, too short hay notes; oil on a bean means too dark.
   > "From this point, aim for 3-4 degrees of development time. It can be as little as 20-30 seconds. Anymore and you will get roast notes. Too little and you will get hay notes." — *post #10, Adamscoffee90*
   > "Oil on a bean means you have roasted it too dark." — *post #10, Adamscoffee90*
6. **C6** — A V2 owner found Ethiopian Shakisso went "pretty dark even with very low development time" and showed oil within days.
   > "they all turn out pretty dark even with very low development time" — *post #9, gtan74*
7. **C7** — V2 owner's light-roast recipe (250 g, repositioned BT probe): higher fan and drum speed suit light roasts and make the heater respond better; targets listed.
   > "I find that higher fan and drum speed is best for light roasts on this machine. It also makes the heater adjustments more responsive." — *post #11, ultrasuede*
   > "Targets: dev 85-100s | DTR 16-18% | drop BT 192-194 | weight loss 12-13%" — *post #11, ultrasuede*
8. **C8** — Cup remark and a colour warning from the same V2 owner (in answer to a question about taste): smooth acidity and fruit; roasts look darker than the actual roast degree on these machines, so trust development time, weight loss and drop temperature.
   > "I had a cup this morning. Nice smooth acidity and fruit." — *post #12, ultrasuede*
   > "They dont come out looking light on these roasters." — *post #12, ultrasuede*
   > "Trust in the development time, weight loss and drop temp over colour." — *post #12, ultrasuede*
9. **C9** — Open question: whether beans cool below 40 °C within 4 minutes on the stock tray (no reply in the thread).
   > "Interested to know if users found that the beans cool to under 40c within 4 minutes using the stock cooling tray?" — *post #5, TomPoland*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Partly fills gap #3. There are two V1 owners at 350–400 g, but only one gives a cup remark and that is for a V2 at 250 g, so a 350 g V1 profile with tasting is still missing. The V2 recipe targets 85–100 s development and its author reports a pleasant cup, about 20–40 s longer than the owner's roughly 60 s, with FC around 185–187 °C, which matches other V1 reports of FC near 180–185 °C (S0015–S0017). The "looks darker than it is" remark matches the dark-outside/under-developed-inside concern in gap #6 and in S0026. V2 (Delta) differs from V1 in the controller, drum motor and tablet (C3 context), so recipes may need scaling.
