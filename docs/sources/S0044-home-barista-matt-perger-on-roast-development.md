# S0044 — Matt Perger on Roast Development (Home-Barista thread)

**Author:** many forum members · **Type:** forum thread (68 posts over 7 pages, 2015-03-08 to 2015-03-20; all pages read) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple (mostly gas drums; one Quest electric) · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/matt-perger-on-roast-development-t34804.html)

## Summary
The thread opened with a link to Matt Perger's Barista Hustle article "Let's talk about roasting" (the page is now a 404, so the article's own text could not be read; everything below about Perger is second-hand through forum quotes). Members debate his three faults — underdevelopment, baking, stalling — against Scott Rao's rule of a steadily declining rate of rise (RoR) and a 20–25 % development share. Most posts are opinion. The useful parts are a few practitioner numbers from a small electric roaster (a Quest: about 1:45 from first-crack start to drop, 12–13.5 % weight loss), the reasons given for why Rao-style declining RoR suits heavy gas drums better than light electric ones, and the view that Nordic-style light roasts cannot meet a 20 % development share.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Perger (quoted by a member) says most specialty coffee he tastes is underdeveloped. *Second-hand; the article is no longer reachable.*
   > "A vast majority of the coffees I am currently tasting from Specialty Coffee Roasters around the world exhibit underdevelopment" — *post #2, Boldjava quoting Perger*
2. **C2** — Perger's definition of "baked" as quoted in the thread: lack of sweetness or dark-roast flavours in a coffee that is not dark. *Second-hand.*
   > "This is characterised by the coffee exhibiting a lack of sweetness and/or dark roast flavours, even though it might not be a dark roast." — *post #15, zammie quoting Perger*
3. **C3** — Perger's grid of light/dark against developed/underdeveloped, as quoted: dark and underdeveloped is "ashy, bitter" with no sweetness; light and developed is bright and sweet. *Second-hand.*
   > "Ashy, bitter, no sweetness and savoury green flavours." — *post #66, endlesscycles quoting Perger*
4. **C4** — A reader's reading of Perger on baking: it is a temperature rise that accelerates mostly toward the end of the roast.
   > "His terms for baking is that the temp rise accelerates predominantly at the end of the roast." — *post #8, TomC*
5. **C5** — A forum member's view of the faults: a stall has no taste of its own but usually leaves an underdeveloped coffee; a baked roast is flat.
   > "As far as I can tell, "stalled" doesn't have a taste. However, stalled roasts are almost always underdeveloped." — *post #11, boar_d_laze*
   > "Baked roasts are flat, with most of the bean's flavor characteristics cooked out." — *post #11*
6. **C6** — The mechanism usually quoted for baking is a stall letting sugar chains cross-link (Rao, as quoted by NoStream); NoStream doubts it applies before the development phase.
   > "The leading theory is that a stall in a roast causes developing sugar chains to "cross-link," which decreases sweetness and increases baked flavors." — *post #30, NoStream quoting Rao*
   > "I've found declining RoR in development to be very important, but I've had increasing RoR earlier in the roast (ramp) without issue." — *post #30, NoStream*
7. **C7** — Practitioner numbers on a Quest (small electric, light drum): aggressive power at the end of the ramp, a momentum entry to first crack, then heat cut and fan up; this gave ~1:45 from first crack start to drop at about 8–10 °F/min, dropped 15–25 °F after first-crack start with 12–13.5 % weight loss and about a 20 % development ratio.
   > "This gets me ~1:45 from 1Cs to drop and ~8-10 F/min." — *post #16, NoStream*
   > "I drop my roasts at 15-25 degrees after first crack start (1Cs) with 12-13.5% weight loss." — *post #36, NoStream*
   > "With a ~20% development ratio, you won't get the fruited sugars that show up in a lot of Nordic roasts" — *post #36, NoStream*
8. **C8** — On a light roaster there is not enough momentum to coast through first crack; without a power push at the endothermic point before crack the roast stalls, and the roaster then bakes to compensate.
   > "If I don't supply quite a bit of power at the endothermic flash immediately prior to first crack start, my roast will stall and I'll end up needing to bake to compensate." — *post #36, NoStream*
9. **C9** — Members say a declining RoR is easier on a heavy, stable gas drum than on lighter perforated electric ones, and that probe differences make comparisons unreliable.
   > "It's been mentioned already that a declining ROR profile is much easier to pull off with a roaster on greater thermal mass and relatively greater thermal stability." — *post #34, millcityroasters*
   > "I suspect a lot of the difference is variation in temperature readings." — *post #29, millcityroasters*
10. **C10** — Counter-view: one roaster has not found declining RoR to give better coffee; another says forcing it down after first crack makes things worse.
    > "I still haven't personally found correlation between declining rate of rise and better coffee." — *post #28, dustin360*
    > "every time I goof around during development, I screw it up." — *post #23, bohemianroaster*
11. **C11** — Light "Nordic" roasts, dropped near the end of first crack, cannot reach a 20 % development share; Rao's proportions are said to cover only roughly City to Full City.
    > "Rao's proportions apply to the C/C+ through FC/FC+ range, and that's about it." — *post #42, boar_d_laze*
    > "they won't reach 20% development time, instead opting for a reduced development time to keep the sugars intact." — *post #43, NoStream*
12. **C12** — A different member (cimarronEric) says a 20 % development share with a hard charge and a light drop is possible, giving timings of 4.5/3.5/2 (units and stage names not defined in the post; probably minutes).
    > "4.5/3.5/2 = 20% development. It's not easy without stalling or baking, but it's doable." — *post #50, cimarronEric*
13. **C13** — Reported workshop teaching (Zaneus, second-hand): raising RoR at any stage widens the temperature gap between bean surface and centre.
    > "by increasing the Ror at any stage, you will increase the temperature on the outside of bean relative to the inside." — *post #57, Zaneus*
14. **C14** — Arithmetic from 9Sbeans: with first crack read at 390 °F and second crack at 440 °F, getting to Full City+ in a 4 min development needs an average RoR of at least 12.5 °F/min, which conflicts with a "car slowing to a stop" finish.
    > "the average RoR has to be at least 50F/4min, or 12.5F/min." — *post #62, 9Sbeans*
15. **C15** — Neal (N3Roaster) clarifies that the profile in a video was only a first test on a lab roaster, not a standard default.
    > "that isn't any kind of standardized default. It's merely the first thing that I tried at the lab roaster." — *post #61, N3Roaster*
