# S0023 — Transition from vegetal flavor during first crack (Home-Barista)

**Author:** drgary and others · **Type:** forum thread (30 posts, 3 pages, 2022-10-10 to 2022-10-19) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/transition-from-vegetal-flavor-during-first-crack-t83033.html)

## Summary
N3Roaster reports (from trier-sample cupping on a lab roaster) that a raw, vegetal "green" flavour disappears abruptly once the coffee passes a specific temperature, about 10 °F above first-crack start on his machines, and that time does not matter. Marcelnl independently reports a similar threshold on his roaster. Almico reports very light drops (about 10 °F above FC, about 1 minute after FC) from fast roasts with no vegetal or underdeveloped notes, but with thin body. Others discuss whether the cause is uneven inside/outside development, and GDM528 measures how quickly beans follow air temperature on a fluid bed. All numbers are recollections; no cup data, curves or sample sizes are shown in the thread. The first post (drgary) refers to an earlier thread about Anthony Douglas's 2022 WBC roast profile, which was not read.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — N3Roaster: a vegetal / "raw coffee" flavour goes from present to absent quickly and the switch is tied to the coffee's maximum temperature, so it can be used to match light roasts between machines.
   > "this flavor component undergoes a rapid transition from present to absent that is strongly connected to maximum temperature of the coffee" — *post #2, N3Roaster*
2. **C2** — N3Roaster says the effect was repeatable under blind, randomized evaluation across different coffees and profile shapes (method stated, no data shown).
   > "it also turned out to be fully repeatable on blind and randomized evaluations across a range of very different coffees and very different roast profiles" — *post #2, N3Roaster*
3. **C3** — On his machines the note persists until about 10 °F above the start of first crack, regardless of the rest of the profile.
   > "never goes away until 10F° above the start of first crack regardless of anything else in the roast profile" — *post #1 (drgary quoting N3Roaster's earlier post)*
4. **C4** — It is the temperature that matters, not the time: the change is effectively instant, whether the roast is stretched out or fast.
   > "The change I'm talking about is effectively instant and just happens at that temperature regardless of whether things are really stretched out" — *post #6, N3Roaster*
5. **C5** — Reference temperatures on his machines, and a rule of thumb for other machines: first crack start 380 °F, threshold 390 °F, second crack start 430 °F; about one fifth of the way between the cracks.
   > "start of first crack at 380°F, this temperature we're discussing now at 390°F, and start of 2nd crack at 430°F" — *post #6, N3Roaster*
   > "it should still be in the neighborhood of 1/5th between cracks" — *post #6, N3Roaster*
6. **C6** — N3Roaster says he narrowed the threshold to ±0.2 °F (on his equipment).
   > "I've been able to narrow that down to ±0.2°F" — *post #2, N3Roaster*
7. **C7** — Even so, he finds production roasts better a bit hotter than the threshold, and others may reasonably choose the lower end temperature if other flavours are well expressed.
   > "in production I tend to find benefit to going a little hotter still" — *post #8, N3Roaster*
   > "one might still reasonably choose something at these lower ending temperatures" — *post #4, N3Roaster*
8. **C8** — Marcelnl (own roaster, FC about 198 °C) independently reports a chlorophyll-like note below about 204–205 °C and finishes at 206–207 °C.
   > "anything below 204-5'C ish (399F) leaves me with a 'chlorofyl-like' note I do not like" — *post #9, Marcelnl*
9. **C9** — Almico: with dry end reached in 4 minutes or less on high-density beans he dropped about 10 °F above FC roughly one minute after FC, and found no vegetal or underdeveloped notes, but body was thin and acidity high.
   > "by hitting DE in 4 minutes or less in high density beans I was able to drop coffee around 10*F above FC in around a minute" — *post #24, Almico*
   > "there were no signs of vegetal or underdeveloped flavor notes" — *post #24, Almico*
   > "The coffee was very acidic and lacked the body I prize, but very drinkable." — *post #24, Almico*
10. **C10** — Almico reports a Rob Hoos theory (second-hand): faster pressure build-up inside the seed speeds heat transfer from outside to inside; Marcelnl doubts the mechanism and DeuxInfuso proposes a steam-front explanation.
    > "the time that it takes pressure to build up in the coffee seed can have a significant effect on the speed of heat transfer from the outer to inner" — *post #24, Almico (reporting Hoos)*
    > "I struggle understanding why the speed of pressure build up would impact the speed of heat transfer" — *post #28, Marcelnl*
11. **C11** — mikelipino associates vegetal notes with a raw core relative to the outside, which he sees as easy to cause on fast air roasters; he extends the browning phase between DE and FC to avoid it.
    > "I associate vegetal flavors with unevenness of roast in the bean, i.e. the core of the bean is more raw than the outer layers." — *post #13, mikelipino*
12. **C12** — GDM528 (fluid bed, fast thermocouple, small dose, aggressive airflow) measured that beans follow air temperature quickly, and concludes gradients inside the bean are small for gently ramping roasts.
    > "It takes a bit over 60 seconds for the greens to reach roughly 90% of the inlet temperature for an abrupt 40C ramp in temperature." — *post #15, GDM528*
    > "they're actually very nimble, especially for gently ramping roasts" — *post #15, GDM528*
13. **C13** — N3Roaster guesses the culprit is a single compound in the green coffee removed by a pyrolytic reaction; another member suggests hexanal (literature link, untested); GDM528 questions whether hexanal could still be present at FC.
    > "it's likely a single molecular species" — *post #11, N3Roaster*
    > "the mystery compound may be hexanal" — *post #16, bshaw3852*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The owner drops about 1:00 after FC (DTR 7–12 %). If the vegetal switch is temperature-driven as N3Roaster says, then the useful question is how many degrees above the owner's own FC reading the beans end, not how many seconds. With the owner's FC around 184.5 °C, ten °F is about 5.6 °C, but the thermometry (NTC probe, IR lamp, FC marking convention) differs from N3Roaster's, so the offset cannot be assumed to transfer. Almico's fast, very light drops with no vegetal notes also match the S0015 anecdote (~45 s). GDM528's fluid-bed measurement does not transfer directly to radiant heating, where the outer layer absorbs most of the energy (S0015–S0017).

## Gatherer notes
- The blind-test claim is unverifiable (no data, no n). N3Roaster himself says his taste for the note is personal.
- The thread is from 2022. Related Home-Barista threads linked at the bottom: "No audible sound of first crack, does this happen often?" (2026-07) and "Add moisture during a roast?" (EddyQ, 2025-10) were not read.
- Almico's ten-degree drop and N3Roaster's threshold are consistent with each other only if both use similar FC-start definitions; neither states how FC was called.
- Relates to gaps #1 (short development) and #6 (inside vs outside development).
