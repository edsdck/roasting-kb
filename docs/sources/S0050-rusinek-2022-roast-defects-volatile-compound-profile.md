# S0050 — Rusinek et al. 2022: volatile-compound fingerprints of underdeveloped and overdeveloped roasts

**Author:** Rusinek, Dobrzański, Oniszczuk, Gawrysiak-Witulska, Siger, Karami, Ptaszyńska, Żytek, Kapela, Gancarz · **Type:** paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** gas-drum (Coffed SR 5) · [Original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9737409)

## Summary
The authors roasted Typica Arabica from Huehuetenango (Guatemala) in a 5 kg drum roaster at three starting temperatures, 210, 220 and 240 °C, and call the results underdeveloped, standard (medium light) and overdeveloped. They then profiled volatiles by GC-MS and an electronic nose. The defects are defined only by those starting-temperature settings: the paper reports no roast times at first crack or drop, colour, weight loss or sensory scores in the parts read. Group shares differed: the "standard" roast had the most acids and esters, the underdeveloped roast the most azines and aldehydes, the overdeveloped roast the most alcohols and furans. Useful as a chemical marker study, weak as evidence for how to roast.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Design: three roasting modes defined by starting temperature, three repetitions each, full 5 kg load.
   > "each batch of beans represented a full load (5 kg)" — *3.2 Roasting Procedure*
2. **C2** — Mode settings: 240 °C start for overdeveloped, 220 °C standard (medium light), 210 °C underdeveloped.
   > "roasting at an initial temperature of 240 °C to produce an overdevelopment defect" — *3.2 Roasting Procedure*
3. **C3** — The reference probe in the roaster read 180 / 200 / 240 °C for the underdeveloped / standard / overdeveloped procedures, and the temperature dip after the charge ended after about 0.5 / 1 / 1.5 minutes (the "turning point").
4. **C4** — Azine, aldehyde and acid shares of the volatile fraction (underdeveloped / standard / overdeveloped): azines 45.65 / 35.04 / 42.88 %; aldehydes 15.96 / 15.12 / 16.06 %; acids 11.87 / 16.96 / 10.75 %.
5. **C5** — Properly roasted coffee had more esters and acids; overdeveloped had more alcohols (15 %) and furans (12 %); underdeveloped had the most aldehydes (16 %) and azines (46 %).
   > "Properly roasted coffee is characterized by a substantially higher percentage of esters and acids" — *Conclusions*
6. **C6** — Compound counts: 29 in the standard roast, 25 in the underdeveloped roast and 24 in the overdeveloped roast.
7. **C7** — The introduction describes underdeveloped beans as grassy and lacking caramelised sugars, typical of an insufficient-temperature light roast (a definition from the literature, not tested here).
   > "Underdeveloped beans are usually grassy and devoid of the caramelized sugars" — *Introduction*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* It does not tell a Skywalker owner how to roast. It is of interest for gap 14 because it gives measured volatile groups for "under" and "over" roasts, but the groups are built from different starting temperatures with no development time or sensory check, so the labels cannot be mapped to a development-time target (gap 1) or to tipping/astringency. A 5 kg gas drum differs from a 350 g IR roaster in every heat-transfer respect.
