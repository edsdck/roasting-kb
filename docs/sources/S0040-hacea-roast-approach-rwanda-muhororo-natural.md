# S0040 — Hacea Roast Approach: Rwanda Muhororo Natural (2026)

**Author:** Hacea Coffee Source · **Type:** blog article · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://haceacoffee.com/blogs/roasting/roast-approach-rwanda-nyamasheke-muhororo-natural)

## Summary
A natural Red Bourbon from Rwanda behaved oddly on a small gas drum: four standard profiles all tasted green, tart and sweet-less, yet roast loss was over 15 % even with minimal post-crack time (the writer normally sees 12–13 % for this density and moisture). The writer's working theory was that the coffee lost too much water in drying, leaving too little for Maillard. Restricting airflow for the whole roast kept the roast loss at 14.5 % (about 0.75 points less) and gave a balanced, fruit-forward cup. One coffee, no replicates; the explanation is untested.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Setup: Proaster 1.5 kg gas drum at 400 g (about 35 % capacity), small to mimic the gas power of a larger machine; the writer warns not to read too much into exact temperatures.
   > "Batch size is 400g, approximately 35% of roaster capacity." — *The Roaster*
   > "don't read too much into the exact temperatures" — *The Roaster*
2. **C2** — First set of roasts: all tasted "green", underdeveloped and sweet-less, with lemongrass, lemon balm and sharp tart acidity; pushing development only produced bitterness without sweetness.
   > "all of the coffees were tasting "green", underdeveloped and lacking in sweetness." — *The Goal*
   > "I ended up jumping right to bitterness and totally seemed to bypass caramelization." — *The Goal*
3. **C3** — Roast loss was unusually high for the colour of the cup: more than 15 % with minimal post-crack development, against 12–13 % normally for similar density and moisture, while the cup stayed underdeveloped.
   > "I was getting over 15% roast loss on these roasts, even with minimal post crack development." — *The Goal*
   > "In any other coffee, I would say that a 15% roast loss is a medium-dark roast." — *The Goal*
4. **C4** — The writer's untested theory: the coffee lost too much water during drying, so little remained for Maillard and caramelization precursors; supported by a cited Kornman (Daily Coffee News, 2017) water-activity article.
   > "My working theory became that the coffee lost more than a normal amount of water during drying" — *The Goal*
5. **C5** — Fix: a very restricted airflow schedule through the roast; roast loss ended at 14.5 % versus 15.25 % for the first round with similar end temperature and development time.
   > "My strategy was to use a very restricted air flow schedule." — *The Approach*
   > "I still ended this roast with a roast loss of 14.5%" — *The Approach*
   > "My first round of roasts had roast loss of 15.25%, with a similar end temperature and development time." — *The Approach*
6. **C6** — Cup result of the restricted-airflow roast: peach, white grape and Fuji apple, balanced malic acidity, more body.
   > "Peach, white grape, Fuji apple were predominate tasting notes for the cup." — *The Approach*
   > "The acidity was finally balanced" — *The Approach*
7. **C7** — The IKAWA sample roast of the same lot did not show the deficits, which the writer attributes (as a guess) to the fluid bed holding a moister environment during drying.
   > "in the sample roast, which was done on a fluid bed Ikawa with convective heat transfer, the coffee did not have the flavor deficits that I found on the traditional drum roaster." — *The Takeaway*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A warning that weight loss and cup development can disagree: 15 % loss gave a "green"-tasting coffee, so loss is not a safe proxy for development on a dense lot. The owner's roasts (median loss 13.4 %) are not in that range. The airflow link is relevant only loosely, since the Skywalker has little airflow control, and the moisture-in-the-drum theory is untested.

## Gatherer notes
- The page text has no chart data, so temperatures and phase times are missing; the 0.75-point difference comes from one pair of roasts.
- The writer's explanation (too much drying) is a hypothesis; an alternative the page itself raises for a related coffee is uneven seed development (see S0041).
- The sample roast on an IKAWA is mentioned but no profile is in the text.
