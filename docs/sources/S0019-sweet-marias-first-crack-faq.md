# S0019 — Sweet Maria's First Crack FAQ

**Author:** Thompson Owen · **Type:** article / FAQ · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://library.sweetmarias.com/first-crack-faq-what-is-first-crack-what-is-second-crack/)

## Summary
An FAQ for home roasters on first and second crack: what causes them (mainly steam pressure for
first crack, mainly CO₂ for second), how to tell them apart by sound, look and smell, at what
temperature and time they typically happen, and why those numbers vary between machines and
probes. Notable for the gaps: it says first crack temperature depends on the measurement (probe,
placement, roaster type) and on altitude, that dry-process coffees can have quiet first cracks, and
that stopping right at the first pops tends to taste grainy. Experience-based, no data.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — First crack happens mainly because water in the bean turns to steam and builds pressure; steam is the main driver but not the only one.
   > "It cracks because the water trapped in the bean creates pressure as it turns to steam." — *What is First Crack?*
   > "First crack is not solely due to the pressure from water vapor, but it is the main driver." — *What is First Crack, in more depth please?*
2. **C2** — Green coffee holds roughly 10–12 % moisture; at first crack the bean enlarges, the centre crease may open and release chaff, and moisture can be seen leaving.
   > "Coffee is a dried seed, but has residual moisture in it of roughly 10-12%." — *What is First Crack, in more depth please?*
3. **C3** — With probes in direct contact with the coffee on the author's commercial roasters, first crack happens at about 395–405 °F (201–208 °C, roughly); in a later comment he gives 403–406 °F as typical when a sensitive probe reads the beans directly.
   > "In my experience with temperature probes that directly contact the coffee, this will happen at 395 to 405 fahrenheit (201-208 C roughly)." — *At what temperature does first crack happen?*
   > "Usually when the coffee is directly probed by a sensitive thermometer, first crack starts around 403-406 f in most roasters" — *comment reply, Thompson Owen, 2025-01-02*
4. **C4** — First-crack temperature readings vary with the measurement: no probe reads the bean interior, most read a mix of beans and environment, and placement, probe type and roaster type all change the number. It also varies with altitude. Use the numbers only as a rough guide.
   > "We can’t measure the internal temperature of the bean with a probe, which would be the most consistent measurement. And most ways to measure temperature are not strictly reading bean surface temperature … there’s always some environmental influence. It also varies based on altitude." — *At what temperature does first crack happen?*
   > "Temperature measurement in different roasters varies, based on thermometer placement, type, roaster type etc." — *FAQ intro*
5. **C5** — Second crack is driven more by CO₂ (plus CO and N₂) pressure in an increasingly brittle bean; its temperature seems more coffee-dependent than first crack's.
   > "It has seemed to me that second crack has a more variable temperature range based on the coffee." — *What temperature does second crack happen at?*
6. **C6** — Older or drier green coffee, and less dense (often lower-grown) coffee, can have a louder first crack; some high-grown dry-process (natural) coffees have a very quiet, short first crack, some almost silent.
   > "I have noticed that when green coffee is older, or more dried out, it can have a more percussive and louder first crack." — *What does it mean when first crack is very loud?*
   > "(Conversely we have had some high grown dry-process coffees that have a very quiet and short audible first crack!)" — *same section*
7. **C7** — There is no correct time to first crack; it depends on heat transfer type, batch size, the coffee and ambient. Typical: 3–7 min in small air roasters, 10–12 min in a Behmor drum, 9–12 min in 5–12 kg shop roasters.
   > "For quality, there is no “correct” timing for first crack. It depends on the type of thermal transfer, the batch size, the coffee, ambient temperature, and many other variables." — *What time does first crack happen in the roast?*
8. **C8** — A coffee is "in first crack" once regular successive pops are heard, not at the first isolated pops; stopping at the first sounds tends to taste grainy and possibly astringent.
   > "I would say coffee is truly in first crack when a regular sound of successive pops is heard." — *Do we say a coffee is “in first crack” …?*
   > "(If a roast is stopped right when the initial sound of first crack is heard, even accounting for some forward “momentum” from the heat stored in the coffee, it will likely taste very grainy and possibly astringent)." — *same section*
9. **C9** — Non-sound cues for first crack: patchy bean colour, a slightly opened crease and rounder edges, a grain/bread-like smell, and moist steam from an air roaster; the transition is from endothermic to exothermic.
   > "The smell of first crack is more like grain, like bread baking, whereas second crack has more pungency." — *How else can I tell the difference …?*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Helps frame gap #3. If first crack is mostly steam pressure (C1), coffees with
different moisture or structure could crack at different bean temperatures, and C4 says the probe
reading adds its own variation. The source does not say naturals/honeys crack hotter or cooler; it
only notes they can crack quietly (C6), which could also make FC be marked later by ear. The
201–208 °C contact-probe range is ~20 °C above typical Skywalker readings (~180–185 °C),
consistent with S0017 C9/C13 that Skywalker numbers aren't comparable. C8 is relevant to very short
development.
