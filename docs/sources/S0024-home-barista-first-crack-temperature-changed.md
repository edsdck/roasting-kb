# S0024 — First crack temperature changed, now what? (Home-Barista)

**Author:** jramey0406 and others · **Type:** forum thread (11 posts, 2 pages, 2026-03-12 to 2026-03-14) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/first-crack-temperature-changed-now-what-t103250.html)

## Summary
A drum-roaster owner whose coffees always crack at 386 °F hits first crack 10 °F higher with a new origin, shifts his drop temperature by 10 °F, and gets a darker roast with a lower development percentage. Replies split into three camps: drop by development time and treat FC as a marker (Theophilus, CarefreeBuzzBuzz); FC temperature is the same for nearly all coffees on a calibrated machine and drop by absolute temperature (Nunas); or FC varies with bean, speed and profile (cannonfodder, EddyQ). EddyQ adds a measurement: ground colour tracked drop temperature better than development time in his DIY colour-meter logs. No profiles, curves or cup data are shown.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Setup: a coffee that cracked at 395 °F instead of the usual 386 °F; matching the drop temperature shift gave a darker roast at lower development percentage.
   > "ended hitting first crack about 10 degrees higher at 395f" — *post #1, jramey0406*
   > "it came out darker than I normally roast and at a lower development percentage" — *post #1, jramey0406*
2. **C2** — Theophilus: drop by a fixed development time and treat FC temperature as just a marker.
   > "if I know I need 90s of development from FC until the time I drop the coffee, then I will count those 90s and drop the coffee regardless of what the temperature is" — *post #2, Theophilus*
   > "We often treat FC temperature as something more than it is: a marker." — *post #2, Theophilus*
3. **C3** — CarefreeBuzzBuzz: different density and water content make FC times differ between beans; for typical drinkers, time from FC to drop matters most.
   > "They will not likely be the same FCs times across a wide selection of bean." — *post #3, CarefreeBuzzBuzz*
   > "the time between FCs and drop is the factor mostly likely to influence their reaction to the coffee" — *post #3, CarefreeBuzzBuzz*
4. **C4** — Nunas (Quest M6): FC temperature is the same ±2 °C for nearly all greens, 200 °C on his Quests, and probe placement matters more than bean type; he drops by absolute bean temperature.
   > "the 1Cs temperature for nearly all greens is the same +/- 2 C (roughly 3.5 F)" — *post #4, Nunas*
   > "probe placement can cause a greater variance than the type of green" — *post #4, Nunas*
   > "I drop by absolute BT temperature, and try to manage the time to get there." — *post #4, Nunas*
5. **C5** — cannonfodder: FC varies by coffee on his roaster; a new Ethiopian started FC at about 400 °F and finished at about 420 °F.
   > "Most roll into first crack in the 390-395F range on my roaster." — *post #5, cannonfodder*
   > "did not start 1st crack until 400F and finished at around 420" — *post #5, cannonfodder*
6. **C6** — EddyQ marks FC at a set temperature because FC is often inaudible, believes FC onset depends on bean, entry rate and profile, drops by time past FC for light and medium roasts, and reports a colour-meter observation.
   > "I do believe beans start cracking at different temperatures depending on the bean, the rate you enter FC and the general profile." — *post #8, EddyQ*
   > "found ground color correlates better with drop temperature than development time" — *post #8, EddyQ*
   > "the taste correlation was not conclusive" — *post #8, EddyQ*
7. **C7** — Mathwiz and Nunas suggest first checking that the change in FC reading is not a shifted, loose or bent probe; the OP says his probe is threaded into the front panel and solid.
   > "Don't discount the possibility that your BT probe may have come loose or bent." — *post #4, Nunas*
   > "is it inserted to the same depth as before? I know that an inadvertent change in that regard can cause this type of discrepancy." — *post #9, Mathwiz*
8. **C8** — LBIespresso: judges a roast from time and temperature rise after FC.
   > "I like to look at time and temp rise post first crack as a quick judge of the roast." — *post #6, LBIespresso*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Bears directly on gap #2. Practitioners disagree, and the two camps map onto the disagreement already recorded between S0020 and the owner's own honey-versus-washed offset (S0017 C10). Nunas's and EddyQ's remarks both point to probe placement and FC-marking convention as confounders, which fits the owner's NTC-probe setup (S0017, S0019). EddyQ's colour-meter result (ground colour follows drop temperature) is a reason for the owner to log drop temperature and ground colour together if he gets a colour reading. None of the machines is infrared.
