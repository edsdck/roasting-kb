# S0002 — Convection vs Radiant (Skywiki / Renatoa)

**Author:** Renatoa, summarized on Skywiki · **Type:** wiki article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** IR · [Original](https://skywiki.coffee/books/general-information/page/convection-vs-radiant)

## Summary
A short physics-based argument for why a flat RoR is normal on a radiant roaster but suspicious
on a convection roaster. Convection heat transfer falls as beans approach air temperature
(Newton's law), so RoR declines at constant input. Radiant transfer scales with the difference of
fourth powers of source and bean temperatures; with a very hot lamp the energy delivered stays
nearly constant, and beans absorb more IR as they darken.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — In convection, heat transfer is proportional to (T_air − T_bean), so with constant heat input the RoR falls naturally as beans warm.
   > "So if your heat input stays constant, RoR falls naturally." — *main text*
2. **C2** — Radiant heating follows Stefan–Boltzmann (∝ T_source⁴ − T_bean⁴); with a very hot lamp, energy input stays nearly constant through the roast.
   > "Radiant heating behaves differently follows Stefan–Boltzmann law" — *main text*
3. **C3** — Beans' emissivity (absorption) increases as they darken, so they absorb more IR later in the roast; reducing heater power after drying keeps energy input roughly constant.
   > "Beans absorb more IR energy later in roast" — *main text*
4. **C4** — Flat RoR on a radiant roaster indicates stable energy delivery, whereas on a convection roaster a flat RoR usually means increasing energy input, which can taste baked or dull.
   > "Flat RoR in a radiant roaster = stable energy delivery" — *TLDR*

"steadily declining RoR" advice (S0008), which comes from convection/conduction drum roasters.
Suggests that the shape of a "good" RoR curve is machine-dependent.
