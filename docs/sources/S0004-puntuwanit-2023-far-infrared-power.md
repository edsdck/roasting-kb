# S0004 — Optimization of Far Infrared Radiation Power for Roasting Coffee (2023)

**Author:** Puntuwanit, Suriwong, Maneechot · **Type:** paper (J. Renewable Energy and Smart Grid Technology 18(2):1–9) · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** IR · [Original](https://ph01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/RAST/article/view/252799)

## Summary
An engineering study of a 1 kg horizontal roaster heated by far-infrared (FIR) heaters at five
powers (1000–3000 W). It measured bean temperature, roast time, colour (CIE L*a*b*) and energy
use against a consumer-acceptable colour range. Higher FIR power raised bean temperature and
shortened roast time dramatically (81 → 18 min); specific energy was lowest at 2500 W. It is an
energy/feasibility study, with no sensory evaluation.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Higher FIR power increased bean temperature and decreased roasting time.
   > "as the FIR power increased, the coffee bean's temperature increased while the roasting time decreased." — *Abstract*
2. **C2** — Colour values (L*, a*, b*) decreased significantly with higher roasting temperatures and shorter times.
   > "the color attributes (CIE L*, a*, and b* values) decreased significantly with higher roasting temperatures and shorter times." — *Abstract*
3. **C3** — Specific energy requirement was lowest at 2500 W (3750 J/g) and highest at 1000 W (6075 J/g), associated with reduced drying time.
   > "This decrease in specific energy requirement was associated with reduced drying time." — *Abstract*
4. **C4** — The authors conclude FIR heaters are a viable, controllable heat source for coffee roasting.
   > "These results indicate the potential of using Far Infrared heaters for efficient coffee roasting" — *Abstract*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Low–moderate. Confirms IR can roast coffee and that power drives time,
but the roast times (18–81 min) and >250 °C temperatures are far from specialty practice, and
there is no flavour data. Mainly useful as background on IR heating.
