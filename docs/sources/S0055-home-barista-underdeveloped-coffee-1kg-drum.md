# S0055 — Home-Barista: underdeveloped coffee on a 1 kg drum (2024)

**Author:** Johnnyburns and replies · **Type:** forum thread (25 posts) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** gas-drum · [Original](https://www.home-barista.com/roasting/underdeveloped-coffee-t93631.html)

## Summary
A beginner on a 1.5 kg solid-drum gas roaster says every light roast tastes grassy and underdeveloped, even one dropped about a minute after first crack. Replies agree that he is aiming too short for a new machine and coffee: another_jim takes a new coffee to at least 2:15 after first crack starts, Trjelenc suggests a baseline of about 2 minutes and 15-20 °F, and drgary points at a cool charge. There are no cup results for the changes; at the end the OP reports a Colombian roast with a hot start (RoR very high, then a stall) and says he will use less gas from the start next time. Useful here as practitioner counter-evidence to very short development and for how first crack is called.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The OP's complaint: grassy and underdeveloped on every roast, 1 kg in a solid drum, including a drop about 1 minute after first crack.
   > "each roast I do tastes underdeveloped and grassy." — *post #1, Johnnyburns*
   > "dropped about 1 minute after 1st crack and that tasted underdeveloped and grassy as well." — *post #1*
2. **C2** — another_jim: most of his coffees are not developed until about 2 minutes after first crack starts; a new coffee goes to at least 2:15; he smells the trier sample for sweetness before dropping.
   > "Most coffees I roast are not developed until about 2 minutes after the first crack starts" — *post #3, another_jim*
   > "they should smell sweet, not vinegary or grassy." — *post #3*
3. **C3** — He starts timing the final phase at a fixed temperature (190 °C, about 45-60 s before first crack on his machine) because first-crack start is a poor marker.
   > "I start timing the final phase of the roast at 190C" — *post #3*
   > "IMO, the start of the first crack is not a particularly good marker." — *post #3*
4. **C4** — Trjelenc: the first stray pop can precede the real first crack by 10-15 °F; call first crack at 3 or more pops in about a second, or a 1-minute development may be far too short.
   > "The first stray pop of a bean can happen 10-15°F or more before the start of the rest of the popping." — *post #4, Trjelenc*
5. **C5** — Trjelenc's baseline for a new roaster or machine: about 2 minutes and 15-20 °F of development, as a time AND temperature target.
   > "A development of around 2 minutes and 15-20°F seems to be a good starting point" — *post #4*
6. **C6** — Heat arriving too fast into first crack can outrun the interior; first crack start is not a real flavour boundary.
   > "You might be carrying too much heat through the end of the middle phase going into first crack and "outpacing" the interior development of the bean." — *post #4*
7. **C7** — Milligan: more Maillard time is not the usual answer; a good light roast groups the cracks tightly by keeping RoR up into first crack, but light roasts suit dense beans and Brazils are harder.
   > "More time in Maillard isn't typically the answer." — *post #5, Milligan*
   > "a good light roast procedure typically involves trying to group the cracks very closely together" — *post #5*
8. **C8** — drgary: a cool charge (6 minutes to dry end) drives too little heat in early and risks uneven development; he suggests an ET near 380 °F at charge; his own Yemen profile charged at 421 °F / 216 °C.
   > "by charging too cool you aren't driving enough heat into the beans early on and are setting yourself up for uneven development." — *post #8, drgary*
   > "My charge temperature was 421°F/216C." — *post #13*
9. **C9** — RoR above 20 °F/min is not a stall; Milligan says mid-40s RoR can work for some coffees but is high for low-density beans.
   > "here its above 20°F/min." — *post #10, Trjelenc*
   > "I've had some coffees that get into the mid 40's and do well" — *post #23, Milligan*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Several experienced roasters here put the safe development baseline at 2:00-2:15 and 15-20 °F for a new machine, and the OP's roast of about 1 minute gave grassy cups. That bears on gap 1 (very short development), but the thread is gas-drum and beginner-led, so it does not settle the question for a Skywalker; what transfers is the advice to call first crack consistently and to set a temperature-plus-time development target.

## Gatherer notes
The OP's ET probe sits in the exhaust, his fan changes between roasts, and his charge is cool, so the grassy cups have many possible causes. Corroborates S0044 and S0036 on development length and first-crack calling; no cup data for any fix.
