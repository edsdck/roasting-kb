# S0051 — Cho 2023: three light-roast profiles of one Colombian washed coffee, with defects and fixes

**Author:** Sungbin Cho · **Type:** vendor blog article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** multiple (Giesen W1A, Easyster 1.8) · [Original](https://eng.firescope.cropster.com/posts/how-to-make-light-roasting-profile-for-colombia-washed-coffee)

## Summary
A practitioner compares three roasts of the same Antioquia washed Caturra/Colombia lot, each 1000 g: a baked and over-acidic Giesen roast (14.8 % loss), a grassy Easyster roast (13.0 %), and an adjusted Giesen roast (15.0 %, heat +10 %, initial temperature -20 °C) that he found balanced. He ties underdevelopment to too little airflow or heat and baked notes to long low-temperature roasting, too little heat, or airflow out of balance with heat. The three roasts used two machines and different years, so the comparison is not controlled; no charge temperatures, times or RoR values are given in the text, and cup notes are the author's own.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The article defines light roast as the phase between the start and end of first crack, and says to cup within 24 hours of roasting.
   > "This article defines a light roast as the roasting phase between the start and end of the first crack." — *introduction*
2. **C2** — Underdeveloped flavours usually come from insufficient airflow or inadequate heat.
   > "Underdeveloped flavors: Usually emerge from insufficient airflow or inadequate heat." — *Identifying and Correcting Roasting Defects*
3. **C3** — Baked flavours come from prolonged low-temperature roasting, too little heat or airflow out of balance with heat.
   > "Baked flavors: Typically caused by prolonged roasting at low temperatures, insufficient heat delivery, or unbalanced airflow relative to the heat supply." — *Identifying and Correcting Roasting Defects*
4. **C4** — Profile 1 (Giesen W1A): 1,000 g to 852 g, 14.8 % loss; baked notes, excess acidity like underripe orange, low complexity and sweetness, dry mouthfeel; suggested fix is more airflow.
5. **C5** — Profile 2 (Easyster 1.8): 1,000 g to 870 g, 13.0 % loss; grass/straw, floral, malic acidity, weak body; suggested fix is more heat plus faster airflow.
6. **C6** — Profile 3 (Giesen W1A, optimised): 1,000 g to 850 g, 15.0 % loss; heat +10 %, initial temperature -20 °C; balanced citric/malic acidity, strong body, red apple, florals; both earlier defects resolved.
   > "Increased heat application by 10% compared to Profile 1, lowered initial temperature by 20°C." — *Profile 3 adjustments*
7. **C7** — Both defects at once point to misaligned airflow management; for Colombian washed coffees more airflow can fix underdevelopment.
   > "For Colombian washed coffees, enhancing airflow can effectively resolve underdevelopment." — *Identifying and Correcting Roasting Defects*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* The weight-loss figures are the most usable part: the baked roast (14.8 %) and the preferred roast (15.0 %) lost almost the same weight, and the grassy roast lost least (13.0 %). So loss alone did not separate baked from balanced; the difference was the heat and initial-temperature profile. It comes from two commercial drum roasters, one lot, one taster, and no replicates. The airflow advice rests on drum-roaster logic and may not carry to a lamp-heated roaster.
