# S0047 — Bullet R1 airflow test: setting 1 vs 9 on the same coffee (Sweet Maria's, 2019)

**Author:** Dan Wood · **Type:** blog article · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** induction-drum (Bullet R1) · [Original](https://library.sweetmarias.com/aillio-bullet-r1-roaster-testing-airflow-with-rwanda-kegeyo/)

## Summary
Wood roasts 440 g of Rwanda Kivu Kageyo twice on a Bullet R1 with the same heat and drum settings, changing only airflow (setting 1 vs 9 out of 12). The high-airflow roast ran about 30 s slower at every stage, peaked at a lower rate of rise (never above 22 °F/min), was pulled 6 °F lower after the same time past first crack, and lost 11.5 % against almost 13 %. In the cup the high-airflow coffee was brighter and more complex; the low-airflow one tasted a bit more developed. He says the result illustrates how airflow shapes roast dynamics, not that high airflow is better. One pair of roasts; no replicate and no blind cupping.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Test design: two 440 g roasts, airflow 1 vs 9; heat 9 until bean temperature reached 320 °F, then P5; slow drum speed because the batch was half capacity.
   > "I ran a pretty simple test of two 440 grams roasts (just shy of 1 LB), one with airflow at 1 and the other with airflow at 9." — *test description*
   > "Power/heat settings were set to 9 all the way up until the bean temp reached 320F at which point I dropped the power to P5" — *test description*
2. **C2** — Roast 1 (airflow 1): first crack 7:14 at 343 °F, pulled 9:02 at 377 °F, RoR about 30 °F/min after the drying phase, falling to 20 around 6:00 and 10 at the end.
   > "You can see that 1st Crack (“FC”) occurred at  7:14/343F, and I pulled the roast at 9:02/377F." — *Roast #1*
3. **C3** — A steep 28 °F climb in 1:48 after first crack is read as a false sensor reading from the original Bullet sensor.
   > "I believe the steep climb of 28F in 1:48 to be a false reading due to the original Bullet sensor" — *Roast #1*
4. **C4** — Roast 2 (airflow 9): about 30 s later at every stage, pulled 6 °F lower after the same time past first crack, RoR never above 22 °F, loss 11.5 % vs almost 13 %.
   > "There’s about a 30 second lag on all roast stages – yellowing, first crack, and pull." — *Roast #2*
   > "the weight loss came in at 11.5% compared to the almost 13% loss of roast #1." — *Roast #2*
5. **C5** — Ambient air entering the chamber cools the roast, so a sharp airflow increase slows it, depending on heat and drum speed.
   > "the introduction and increase of outside ambient air into the roast chamber cools down the roast." — *comparison of graphs*
   > "the higher the airflow, the greater the affect on cooling the drum temperature." — *comparison of graphs*
6. **C6** — Drum speed can also be used to slow a small batch: faster drum suspends beans in the airflow path longer.
   > "increase drum speed in order to suspend the beans in the airflow path longer which also has a cooling affect." — *discussion*
7. **C7** — Cup: the high-airflow coffee was much brighter; both had developed sweetness; roast 1 tasted a bit more developed; roast 2 was more complex and closer to what he wants in a Rwanda.
   > "The high airflow coffee was a much brighter cup, the first difference I noted when cupping these two side by side." — *cupping results*
   > "though roast #1 with low airflow tasted a bit more developed." — *cupping results*
8. **C8** — Brightness is attributed to a slightly lower end temperature and slower development; roast time was not much longer.
   > "the 6F difference in temp along with a slower overall development really came through as a brighter cup of coffee." — *cupping results*
9. **C9** — His own practice: raise airflow before first crack to stop the roast taking off, and at the tail end of the roast raise the air and dial back heat, around 20 °F before first crack.
   > "I am most likely to ramp up airflow before the beginnings of first cracks to keep the roast from taking off." — *closing section*
10. **C10** — Caution against generalising.
    > "there is no “one size fits all” approach" — *closing section*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A small induction drum with a half-full batch is not an IR roaster, but 440 g is close to 350 g and the direction of the effect is useful: more airflow slowed the roast, lowered peak RoR and gave a brighter cup at nearly the same time. On a Skywalker the fan also cools the lamp-heated chamber, so a similar fan step is a plausible lever for a stalled or too-fast roast. The cup effect rests on one roast pair and one taster.
