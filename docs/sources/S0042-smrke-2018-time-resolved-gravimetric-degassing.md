# S0042 — Degassing of roasted coffee measured by weight (Smrke et al., 2018)

**Author:** Smrke, Wellinger, Suzuki, Balsiger, Opitz, Yeretzian · **Type:** peer-reviewed paper · **Grade:** A · **Heat context:** gas-drum (roasting only; the topic is degassing) · [Original](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/12175/2/2017_Smrke-etal_Roasted-coffe-degassing-time-resolved-gravimetry.pdf) (repository PDF)

## Summary
The authors weigh a sealed container with a capillary outlet on a semi-micro balance to follow how fast roasted coffee loses gas (treated as CO2) over hundreds of hours. Whole Arabica beans from nine roast profiles (three degrees × three speeds) and ground Arabica and Robusta at different degrees were measured and modelled with a Weibull function. Darker roasts hold and release much more gas than light ones, faster roasts degas more than slower roasts at medium and dark degrees but not at light, and most of the trapped gas in ground coffee leaves in the first minutes after grinding. The paper does not link gas release to taste.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Roasting produces CO2 that is partly retained in the bean; the paper cites roughly 1–2 % of the weight of freshly roasted coffee.
   > "of the weight of freshly roasted coffee." — *Introduction (list item giving CO2 as 1 to 2 % of that weight)*
2. **C2** — Whole light roasts degas very little: weight losses of 2.7 mg/g (slow), 2.5 (medium) and 2.8 mg/g (fast) over the measurement, which ran at least 400 h (the paper does not tie these values to a specific hour).
   > "the resulting weight losses were 2.7 (slow roast speed), 2.5 (medium), and 2.8 mg/g (fast)." — *Results, Whole Roasted Coffee Beans*
3. **C3** — At medium and dark degrees faster roasts degas faster (and more) than slower ones; for light roasts speed matters little.
   > "the degassing is faster from faster roasts than from the slower roasts" — *Results, Modeling of Degassing from Whole Roasted Coffee Beans*
   > "less impacted by the roasting speed" — *Results, light roasts*
4. **C4** — Degassing of whole beans is slow and long: a light roast followed for 800 h had released 3.4 mg/g and was still releasing about 2 µg/g/h; the total at infinite time is unknown.
   > "after 800 h, a total of 3.4 mg/g weight loss from degassing was measured." — *Results, Whole Roasted Coffee Beans*
   > "The exact total degassed amount at infinite time remains unknown." — *Results, Whole Roasted Coffee Beans*
5. **C5** — For ground coffee, most of the gas leaves at grinding: up to 75 % of the trapped gas in the first 90 s.
   > "up to 75% of the trapped gas was released during and immediately after grinding." — *Results, Roast and Ground Coffee*
6. **C6** — For ground coffee, release increased from light to medium roasts and then levelled off toward dark.
   > "the degassing increased from light roasts to medium roasts." — *Results, Roast and Ground Coffee*
   > "reached a plateau toward darker roast degrees" — *Conclusion*
7. **C7** — Roast speed was set by burner gas flow, with speed classes defined by time.
   > "which were controlled by different gas flows to the gas burner (90% fast, 60% medium, and 40% slow" — *Materials and Methods, Sample Preparation*
   > "Roasting speed: fast = 4−5 min; medium = 9−10 min; slow = 11−12 min." — *Table 1 footnote*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Gives the owner a measured basis for the "rest light roasts longer" talk (S0036 posters say 1–6 weeks): a light roast has little gas to lose, so degassing alone is a weak reason to wait; whatever happens in the first weeks is unlikely to be CO2 mass and is not covered here. The roasts were made on a gas Probat, and speed here means 4–12 minutes, so no direct link to a 350 g IR roast; light roasts at any speed degassed about the same (2.5–2.8 mg/g). No taste data.

## Gatherer notes
- The PDF text had extraction artefacts (split words, unit slips such as g/cm³ shown as kg/m³); numbers were read through a summarising fetch tool in three passes and agreed each time; the weight-loss values for medium and dark are in the Results text.
- The paper is internally inconsistent about speed times (Table 1: medium 9–10 min; a figure caption gives about 7–8 min for ground coffee; the Conclusion gives 5–13 min).
- This is a degassing-by-weight method; it measures gas mass, not CO2 specifically, and the authors treat it as CO2 for simplicity.
- The "1 to 2 %" in C1 is a list item in the Introduction, not a finding of the paper. Dashes in the extracted PDF text are minus signs (U+2212), so the C7 table-footnote quote carries them ("4−5 min").
