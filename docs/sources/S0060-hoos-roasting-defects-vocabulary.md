# S0060 — Hoos: a vocabulary of roasting defects (tipping, scorching, facing, under/over-developed)

**Author:** Rob Hoos · **Type:** consultant blog article · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://hoos.coffee/blog/defects)

## Summary
A cheat sheet that separates "objective" physical defects (tipping, scorching, facing, charring, twice-roasted beans, chipping) from "subjective" ones (under/over-developed, under/over-roasted, baked), with causes and the taste effect of each. His key definitional choice: development refers only to the time from first crack to the end of the roast, while roast degree is end temperature, colour or weight loss. He declines to use "baked" because the word has too many meanings. No numbers are given.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Tipping is heat damage to the endosperm cap and possibly the germ; it can be seen after roasting and gives a burnt aftertaste.
   > "It is a result of the endosperm cap and possibly the germ of the coffee seed undergoing heat damage." — *Tipping*
2. **C2** — Early-roast scorching comes from contact with roaster metal that is too hot; it may not be visible in the finished roast but affects taste.
   > "When the metal of the roaster is too hot and where the coffee comes in contact, it burns the surface of the coffee." — *Minor Scorching*
3. **C3** — Facing is late-roast conduction damage, visible in the finished product, with a burnt flavour; causes listed are an overloaded drum, drum speed too slow, or too hot a drum / high heat late.
   > "Facing is caused by thermal damage due to conduction late in the roast." — *Minor Facing*
   > "an overloaded drum, too slow of drum speed, too hot of drum / high heat application at the end of the roast." — *Minor Facing*
4. **C4** — Charring is blackened beans from excess late heat, by conduction or convection.
5. **C5** — Over/under-developed describes only the time from first crack to drop; underdeveloped is described as vegetal, metallic, acidic, bright or grainy, overdeveloped as flat, low-acid, chocolaty, roasty or dull.
   > "we are specifically addressing the length of time from the beginning of the first crack to the end of the roast." — *Over and Under Developed Coffees*
   > "it probably means that it was too: vegetal, metallic, acidic, bright, or grainy for them." — *Over and Under Developed Coffees*
6. **C6** — Over/under-roasted is a separate matter: end temperature, end colour or weight-loss percentage.
   > "Separate from development time, this focuses on the end temperature, end color, or maybe end weight-loss percentage that was chosen for the coffee." — *Over and Under Roasted Coffees*
7. **C7** — He avoids the word "baked".
   > "it has become virtually meaningless, so I generally try not to use this terminology." — *Baked*
8. **C8** — Subjective defect words mostly mean the taster did not like it.
   > "If someone uses a subjective roasting defect to describe your coffee, it just means they didn’t like it." — *Subjective roasting defects*
