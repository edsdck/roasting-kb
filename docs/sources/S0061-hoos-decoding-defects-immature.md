# S0061 — Hoos: immature beans, quakers and astringency

**Author:** Rob Hoos · **Type:** consultant blog article (literature round-up plus opinion) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://hoos.coffee/blog/decoding-defects-immature)

## Summary
First of a series on green-coffee defects. Hoos gathers descriptions of immature (unripe) beans from the SCAA defect handbook, Kornman, Brown, Mersch and Wintgens, and then relays Rabelo et al. 2021: about 10.8 % quakers by weight pushes a natural coffee below 80 points, so the old limit of no quakers in a 100 g roasted sample is, in his words, "BS". He concludes that all quakers were immature beans but not all immature beans become quakers, and that immature beans that do not turn into quakers still hurt the cup (green/grassy and astringent). He describes how to spot them: inward-curved beans, tight silverskin, tight or void centre cut, not necessarily small or very green. The paper itself was not read here; the figures are as relayed.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — The SCAA handbook (quoted) gives immature beans as the main source of astringent flavours.
   > "the main source of astringent flavors in coffee" — *SCAA Arabica Green Coffee Defect Handbook, quoted*
2. **C2** — Kornman (quoted): quakers can only be identified after roasting, and not all underripe green becomes a quaker.
   > "quakers are only identifiable once the coffee has been roasted" — *Kornman, Green Coffee, p. 136, quoted*
3. **C3** — Old SCAA/CQI practice: up to 5 secondary defects (5 immature = 1 defect, so 25 immature beans in a 350 g green sample) but no quakers in a 100 g roasted sample.
4. **C4** — Relaying Rabelo et al. 2021: about 10.8 % quakers by weight is needed to score below 80 points; lower levels are still perceptible.
   > "it takes ~ 10.8% by weight of quakers to cause a coffee to score below 80 points" — *Hoos, on Rabelo et al.*
5. **C5** — Relationship: every quaker was an immature bean, but not every immature bean becomes a quaker.
   > "We may be able to say all quakers were immature beans, but not all immature beans become quakers." — *Framing the conversation so far*
6. **C6** — Immature beans that do not become quakers still hurt the cup, in his experience and per several authors.
   > "even if an immature coffee bean does not turn into a quaker, it has a detrimental impact on the flavor of the finished cup" — *Framing the conversation so far*
7. **C7** — His working description for spotting them in green coffee.
   > "curved inward toward the center-cut, tight clinging silverskin, tight or void center-cut." — *Identifying Immature Coffees*
8. **C8** — They need not be much smaller or intensely green.
9. **C9** — He proposes 10-11 % quakers by weight as a fairer threshold for producers.
