# S0015 — Skywalker Roasts (Home Roasters forum thread)

**Author:** several Skywalker owners, mainly renatoa · **Type:** forum thread (118 posts, Dec 2023 – Feb 2026) · **Grade:** B · **Heat context:** IR · [Original](https://homeroasters.org/forum/viewthread.php?thread_id=6980)

## Summary
The Home Roasters forum's thread where Skywalker owners post actual roasts: minute-by-minute
heater/fan/temperature tables, first-crack and drop readings, weight loss and (sometimes) tasting
notes. The most detailed logs are renatoa's fully manual 250 g roasts (including a Kenya he calls
the best of his first ~15 Skywalker roasts), plus factory auto/assist-mode roasts at 227–400 g from
other owners. The thread also covers practical lessons: how the factory preheat logic interferes at
charge, why bigger batches need proportionally longer roasts on a radiant machine, how ambient
temperature and airflow interact, an accidental ~45-second development that still cupped well, and
renatoa's (unsupported) view that flat RoR in drying is normal for radiant roasting.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — renatoa's long manual natural roast, 250 g: preheat 160 °C at 55 % heater / 50 % fan; TP 104 °C at 1:12; dry end at 5:00 (147 °C); FC at 9:30 (181 °C); drop at 11:04 (192 °C); 14 % weight loss; phases 45 / 41 / 14.2 %, average RoR 11 / 8 / 7 °C/min. Heater was stepped down 55→50→45→40 % and fan up 50→60→70→80→90 %.
   > "First time naturals attempt so stretched it as much as I could, the longest full manual roast so far,
   > PHT 160C HTR55 FAN50%" … "9:30 181C FC" … "11:04 192 Drop R6
   > 250 in 215 out 14% W/L" … "Dev phase 14.2% 7C/min avg RoR" — *post #8, renatoa, 2023-12-13*
2. **C2** — renatoa's best roast to date (a Kenya, 250 g, "best of about 15 roasts" by cupping): preheat stable at 200 °C on 55 % heater / 50 % fan, charged at those settings so the heater did not jump at charge; heater raised to 65→70 % around TP, then 65→60→55→50 % from dry end (3:31, 150 °C); fan 65→70→75→80 %; FC start 7:00 at 181 °C; drop 8:35 at 190 °C (heat 0, fan 100); 18 % DTR, 14 % weight loss.
   > "Roast today a Kenya, the cupping revealed the best of about 15 roasts with Skywalker to date.
   > Started with preheat at 200C, which was stable enough at 55% power and 50% fan.
   > Charged at these values, so no power bump at charge as happens in auto modes" — *post #21*
   > "7:00 181 50 80 7 FCs" … "8:35 190 0 100 5 DROP
   > 18% DTR, 14% W/L" — *post #21 (table)*; batch confirmed "Yes, 250g." — *post #23*
3. **C3** — Heater percentages depend on mains voltage: renatoa's earlier roasts ran at about 5 % lower power settings because his supply was 238 V instead of 230 V; a 125 V US user overshot badly using renatoa's numbers.
   > "The previous roast levels were about 5% lower because my higher mains voltage, at 238V." — *post #23*
   > "I believe my 125V didn't mesh well at all with the numbers I had on paper." — *post #38, Mike_Mathis*
4. **C4** — In manual mode, set the preheat target above the temperature you want, so the roaster's on/off preheat logic never cuts in and temperature is held only by the power level you choose.
   > "That's why advised to set a higher than 200C limit, to be sure the roaster logic for preheat is always ON, and temperature is controlled exclusive by modulating power." — *post #39, renatoa*
5. **C5** — Dry end should be judged as the moment all green/silver hue is gone but before yellow appears, or by smell (fresh grass → hay); several owners found the IR roast seems to go from green to brown with little visible in-between, and one marks dry end by a sweet-corn smell around 140–150 °C.
   > "Dry ends when any hue of green/silver is gone, but yellow not still there..." … "Other than vision, you can use smell to evaluate dry ends... when smell turns from fresh grass to hay." — *post #25, renatoa*
   > "My beans tend to go from green-brown with very little in between" — *post #41, makermods*
   > "I have been basing it on smell; I've noticed a distinct sweet corn smell at around 140-150C." — *post #45, makermods*
6. **C6** — On a radiant drum, a larger batch should take proportionally longer at the same heater %, because only the top layer of the bean pile absorbs radiation at any moment and then passes heat to the others as the drum turns.
   > "At any given time, heat is absorbed only by the top layer of the pile, which is directly exposed to radiation." … "If the heat% remains the same, the time should be longer for more beans to absorb same heat each of them." — *post #30, renatoa*
7. **C7** — Below ~250 g the stock probe may not be fully immersed in beans; at 85 g one owner saw first crack read ~173 °C versus ~183 °C with larger batches. Owners with 340–400 g batches consider 350–400 g the machine's comfortable range.
   > "Under 250g the risk having the probe not completely immersed into beans become significant, imo." — *post #7, renatoa*
   > "First crack occurs at about 173c when with larger batches it reads 183c." — *post #96, Tech2C*
   > "This roaster is designed for 350-400g. If you roast less, it obviously can be done, but you need to really know what you are doing." — *post #38, Mike_Mathis*
8. **C8** — A very short development roast cupped well: FC at 179 °C at 6:45; the heater was cut by accident at 7:00; bean temperature stayed at ~182 °C for 30 s with beans still cracking; dropped after ~45 s of development (~10 % DTR). renatoa found no cardboard/baked flavour despite the flat (zero) RoR and planned to repeat it deliberately with a sinking but positive RoR.
   > "After 30 seconds, when the temperature finally dropped to 181C, I said 45 seconds of development was acceptable, ie 10% DTR, so I opened the drop door." — *post #48, renatoa*
   > "Even in the cup today I was delighted to have no cardboard flavor, as is usually preached as a sign of the baked beans apocalypse... when the damn flat zero RoR happens during development." — *post #48*
9. **C9** — renatoa's typical RoR shape: ~11 °C/min at dry end (minute 4), entering FC about 4 minutes later at 6–7 °C/min; he rarely sees more than ~11 °C/min. The V1 panel RoR is a per-minute average, not a short-window RoR as in Artisan.
   > "I have typical 11C/min at DE, minute 4, and I plan entering into FC with 6-7 RoR value 4 minutes later, thus 4-5C RoR decrease spread over 4 minutes ..." — *post #81, renatoa*
   > "Keep also in mind that Skywalker RoR is computed as degrees per minute, literally as the definition." — *post #81*
10. **C10** — renatoa's few fast (~7 min) Skywalker roasts came out underdeveloped inside while looking darker than average outside; he attributes fast-roast advice to convection-heavy "Nordic school" machines.
    > "My fast roastings so far, in the 7 minutes ballpark, not so many, only two I think, have resulted in underdeveloped beans inside, while the exterior looks darker than average." — *post #81*
11. **C11** — Pushing heat hard is questioned for IR: renatoa doubts that high RoR (e.g. ~17–20 °C/min) is good on a radiant machine, because only the top layer gets energy and needs time to share it; HarryDog found the factory settings mostly under-developed his coffee and roasted hotter to a set drop temperature.
    > "Any power increase means some beans gets more energy than the others, and less time is available to share this energy with the others." — *post #108, renatoa*
    > "For me manufacturer settings most of the time yielded under developed coffee.
    > To mitigate this I roasted hotter and to certain drop temps." — *post #109, HarryDog*
12. **C12** — Ambient temperature affects the Skywalker mainly through the air drawn through the drum. In a ~14 °C basement the factory 22 program gave dry end around minute 6; lowering airflow from 65 % to 55 % restored normal times and power levels. renatoa suggests +5–10 % airflow in hot (~30 °C) ambient (not yet verified). Another owner roasting at ~19 °C room never saw RoR above 11 °C/min and roasts never shorter than 10 min.
    > "The cure was to reduce the airflow, from the default 65%, to 55%, and he is back in the average roast times and power levels." — *post #71, renatoa*
    > "my times are significantly longer (never had a roast that lasted less than 10mins) and I have never seen a RoR larger than 11C/min." — *post #80, Isdel7*
13. **C13** — Airflow on the Skywalker is a light breeze mainly for smoke removal and bean cooling, not heating; but modulating it gives faster, finer control in development than a 5 % heater step. The machine is also sensitive to drafts and wind.
    > "It can be also used in development phase control, modulating the airflow provides faster and finer control than a 5% heating step." — *post #66, renatoa*
    > "this machine is sensitive even to kitchen door opening when outside is 20 kmph wind." — *post #111, renatoa*
14. **C14** — A power profile found for 250 g can be applied to smaller loads with close results, because the radiant energy is the same regardless of load; fewer layers and faster recirculation increase exposure, though.
    > "if you found a good profile for 250 grams, you can apply same power changes to lower charges, and have close results, because IR has the same energy for any load" — *post #97, renatoa*
15. **C15** — Beans thrown up to lamp level burn: after fitting a slightly faster drum motor renatoa counted 8 charcoal beans in a 250 g roast (normally 3–4 at most) and planned to slow the drum.
    > "The effect is that I have a lot more beans jumping in the drum higher than before, up to the lamp level... and the result... more burnt beans." — *post #100, renatoa*
16. **C16** — Factory auto profiles can leave coffee under-developed: a Kenyan AA (255 g, auto 011, 8:55, 13 % loss) tasted flat and vegetal; renatoa notes that 30 s earlier drop would have taken his 14 % roast under 13 % loss.
    > "Very flat tasting with a vegetal after taste.
    > Needs more development for sure" — *post #14, HarryDog*
    > "The 14% loss was due to longer development especially.
    > 30 seconds faster drop and might be under 13%." — *post #19, renatoa*
17. **C17** — renatoa's opinion (stated without data): the "declining RoR" rule may not apply to radiant roasting; flat RoR in the drying phase is normal, and RoR should decline before FC for other reasons.
    > "On another track... I have some insight that declining RoR mantra no more apply for radiant roasting.
    > Especially during dry phase the new normal is to have flat RoR." — *post #111, renatoa, 2026-02-17*
18. **C18** — A decaf (HarryDog) cracked later than usual, ~189 °C, with only ~10 % weight loss yet dark-looking beans.
    > "This a decaf roast only 10% weight loss and I swear first crack was much later, (189c) I dropped at 193c as they were quite dark." — *post #78, HarryDog*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* Directly relevant — same machine, same probe, real logs. renatoa's 250 g
roasts show FC around 179–181 °C (owner's ~184.5 °C at 350 g fits C6/C7: bigger batch, more
immersion). His best cup had 18 % DTR and ~1:35 development, longer than the owner's ~1:00; but
C8 is a single anecdote where ~45 s development still tasted good, so neither settles gap #2.
The 350 g batch needs proportionally more time than renatoa's 250 g recipes (C6). C3 and C12 mean
power numbers must be scaled to the owner's mains voltage and room temperature, not copied.
