# S0030 — Understanding Roast Yield and Loss (Royal Coffee)

**Author:** Chris Kornman · **Type:** article (blog) · **Grade:** C · **Heat context:** n/a · [Original](https://royalcoffee.com/understanding-roast-yield-and-loss/) (final URL seen: royalcoffee.com/blogs/blog/understanding-roast-yield-and-loss)

## Summary
An accounting-oriented explainer on why roasted weight is lower than green weight. The author names three drivers of roast loss — roast degree, green moisture and quality-control sampling — and gives the working formula "moisture + roast degree = roast loss". He gives typical green moisture (10–12 %), loss at very light and very dark roast, and example yields per roast level. No measured experiment is shown; the numbers are illustrative examples from a working importer/roaster.

## Key claims
1. **C1** — Roast degree is the biggest single factor in how much weight is lost; darker roasts lose more.
   > "is likely the most significant factor in how much product is lost during the process." — *section "Degree of Roast"*
2. **C2** — Very light roasts dropped at the start of first crack lose about 10 % of green weight; roasts well past second crack can lose as much as 25 %.
   > "Very light roasts, dropped at the beginning of the first crack, will likely lose only about 10% of their green weight" — *section "Degree of Roast"*
3. **C3** — Typical example yields: light Kenya about 12 % loss, medium Brazil about 15 %, dark Sumatra about 20 %.
   > "Your Kenya loses about 12%, your Brazil about 15%, and your Sumatra roughly 20%" — *section "Degree of Roast"*
4. **C4** — Green coffee is usually exported at about 10–12 % moisture, and some lots fall outside that range.
   > "The moisture content of most coffee prior to export is set at around 10-12%." — *section "Green Coffee Moisture Content"*
5. **C5** — Roast loss is moisture plus roast degree; at the same very light roast, lots with different moisture end at different weights.
   > "Moisture + Roast Degree = Roast Loss" — *section "Green Coffee Moisture Content"*
   > "three samples of varying moisture will have different weights after roasting even if the roast degree is the same." — *same section*
6. **C6** — Example of moisture alone: higher-moisture Sumatra and drier Kenya roasted in the same medium style leave 4 % less product from the Sumatra.
   > "you’re left with 4% less product on the higher moisture content Sumatra" — *section "Green Coffee Moisture Content"*
7. **C7** — Comparing roasted output weights across batches can serve as an inexpensive but imperfect substitute for a colour meter.
   > "an inexpensive (albeit imperfect) alternative to color meters." — *section "Degree of Roast"*

## Relevance to Skywalker (IR, 350 g)
*Gatherer judgement:* A useful outside benchmark for weight loss at different roast levels and for why green moisture matters, since the owner's logs show about 13 % median loss with unknown green moisture. It is a production/inventory view, not about flavour or small IR roasters, and the "about 10 % at start of first crack" figure is a rough statement, not a measurement.

## Gatherer notes
- Moisture figures for the Kenya and Sumatra examples are not given, so the 4 % is not a result that can be checked.
