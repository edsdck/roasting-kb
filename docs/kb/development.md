# Development (first crack → drop)

!!! abstract "In short"
    - **Development time** = time from first crack to drop. **DTR** = development time ÷ total roast time.
    - At the **same final colour**, shorter development → sweeter, more acidic, fruitier, cleaner; longer → more bitter, roasty, nutty-chocolate, astringent. Body barely changes. `conf: medium` (one strong study)
    - Measured acidity is **highest around first crack** and falls the further you roast past it. `conf: high` (two studies agree)
    - Colour alone doesn't describe a roast — log development time too. But colour (roast degree) is still the **strongest** single driver of flavour. `conf: medium`
    - **How long is normal?** Rao's 20–25 % DTR rule vs experienced light roasters at ~13–16 % (1:20–1:30) and Nordic roasters at ~45 s. **Contested.**
    - The same development time does more after a fast roast or a high RoR at first crack — compare DTR only between similar profiles. `conf: medium`
    - **End by seconds or by temperature?** Roasters split: fixed seconds after first crack, absolute drop temperature, or both. One roaster reports a vegetal note that vanishes about 10 °F (~5.6 °C) above first-crack start whatever the time. `conf: low` **Contested.**

## Concepts

- **Acidity peaks at first crack.** In a study of seven very different profiles, titratable
  acidity always peaked during first crack, then fell back to roughly its starting value by second
  crack. Peak height was nearly the same for all profiles (≈ 4.9 mL NaOH/40 mL) and origins, so
  profile mainly changes *when* acidity rises and how much is left when you drop.
  `conf: medium` for the peak itself `ctx: gas-drum, 4 kg` [S0007](../sources/S0007-anokye-bempah-2024-titratable-acidity.md); the decline after first
  crack is `conf: high` — consistent with shorter development being more
  acidic at equal colour [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md).
- **Development time at equal colour.** One washed Colombian was roasted with the same path to
  first crack (~570 s) and the same final colour (Agtron 76), varying only development time —
  90 / 143 / 266 / 390 s — by dropping at lower temperatures for longer development
  (204.1 → 191.1 °C). 46 experienced tasters found (15-point scale, fast → longest):
  acidity 8.5 → 5.4, sweetness 8.7 → 6.3, fruit/berry 7.3 → 4.4, clean cup 7.5 → 5.5,
  bitterness 5.7 → 8.6, roasted 5.4 → 8.8, nutty/chocolate 6.0 → 8.4. Body: no significant
  difference. `conf: medium` (single study, one coffee, one roast degree) `ctx: gas-drum, 1 kg` [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)
- **Chemistry.** Longer development lowered chlorogenic acid (5-CQA 3.52 → 2.44 mM); caffeine
  stayed roughly constant. [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)
- **Colour isn't enough.** Two roasts at the same colour can taste clearly different because of
  development time; record both. `conf: medium` [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)

- **Multi-study confirmation.** Pooling eight sensory studies: colour was the stronger predictor
  overall; at equal colour, development time mattered more than time to first crack; ~3 min of
  development ≈ 2 cm less acidity on a 10 cm scale. `conf: medium` (same research group as the
  study above) [S0012](../sources/S0012-munchow-2020-roasting-conditions-multi-study.md)
- **What under/over-development taste like.** Under: malty/nutty, aggressive acidity, metallic or
  vegetal. Over: muted acidity, heavy, little nuance. See [Defects](defects.md). [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)
- **Development time depends on context.** Rao: development time alone is "relatively meaningless"
  without total roast time — use DTR [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md). Practitioners add that roast speed and momentum also
  change what a given time means: 1:15 after an 8:00 roast is "more developed" than 1:15 after a
  12:00 roast at the same drop temperature, and 1 min after entering first crack at 16 °F/min RoR
  can match 2 min at 8 °F/min for colour and drop temperature (though not taste). DTR compares
  cleanly only between near-identical profiles. `conf: medium` [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md) [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md) [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md)
- **Development also happens before first crack.** Some roasters close the inner/outer bean
  temperature gap in late Maillard rather than relying on development time [S0020](../sources/S0020-home-barista-bean-temperature-at-first-crack.md); an SCA panel
  (as relayed) described development taking place in Maillard too [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md). `conf: low`

- **A temperature threshold above first crack?** N3Roaster (lab roaster, trier samples) reports a
  raw, vegetal flavour that vanishes abruptly about 10 °F above first-crack start (380 → 390 °F on
  his machines; second crack 430 °F, so about a fifth of the way between the cracks), whatever the
  roast time, repeatable in blind, randomized tastings (method stated, no data shown). Marcelnl
  independently finds a "chlorophyll-like" note below about 204–205 °C with first crack ~198 °C
  (≈ +6–7 °C). Almico, after a fast dry end (≤ 4 min) on dense beans, dropped ~10 °F above first
  crack about a minute after it and found no vegetal or underdeveloped notes, but thin body.
  `conf: low` (forum accounts, each on their own thermometry) [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md)

## In practice

- **Brighter / fruitier cup:** shorter development, drop relatively soon after first crack.
  **Rounder / chocolatey / roastier:** extend development. `conf: medium` [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md) [S0007](../sources/S0007-anokye-bempah-2024-titratable-acidity.md)
- **Labelling profiles.** Average RoR after first crack is a machine-specific shorthand for
  development speed. [S0005](../sources/S0005-coffeemind-rate-of-rise-bad-reference.md)
- **Don't define the end by DTR alone.** A 20 °F and a 30 °F rise after first crack can both fit
  a 20 % DTR — also track degrees gained after first crack and end colour. `conf: medium` [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md)
  [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md) Experienced home roasters drop on temperature or colour, not percentage [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md); Rao
  reportedly now advises dropping by colour too (second-hand) [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md).
- **What light roasters actually do** (practitioner reports, not targets):
    - drop ~385–390 °F with first crack ~370 °F → ~16 % DTR; 14–16 %, often 1:20–1:30 after FCs;
      an Ethiopia anaerobic natural at 13 % DTR (~10 min roast) was "stellar" [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md)
    - light Ethiopians ≤ 1:30, drop ~7–10 °C above first crack (500 g drum) [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md)
    - 30 s – 1:30 after gliding into first crack at ~10–12 °F/min [S0020](../sources/S0020-home-barista-bean-temperature-at-first-crack.md)
    - Nordic roasters (2012): ~45 s (Tim Wendelboe) on a Kenya in a shared roasting exercise, with
      very high airflow the whole roast
      [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md)
  `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum / convection`
- **More light-roast reports (2022–2026):**
    - A fruit-forward natural: first crack ~7:30, development **1:00–1:10** ("slightly shorter … because
      it's a natural"), weight loss ~1 % above green moisture, smaller charge, higher airflow
      (Rob Hoos, drum roasters, interview) [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md) `ctx: drum`
    - Very fast dry end (≤ 4 min) and a drop ~10 °F above first crack about 1 min after it: no
      vegetal notes but thin body (Almico) [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md)
    - V2 Skywalker (250 g): development 85–100 s, DTR 16–18 %, drop ~192–194 °C [S0027](../sources/S0027-coffeesnobs-skywalker-roasters-thread.md)
  `conf: low`
- **How roasters end it.** Fixed time after first crack — "if I know I need 90s of development …
  I will count those 90s and drop the coffee regardless of what the temperature is" (Theophilus), and
  time from first crack to drop is the factor most likely to change how ordinary drinkers react
  (CarefreeBuzzBuzz). Absolute drop temperature — Nunas drops by bean temperature and manages the
  time to get there. Both — EddyQ drops by time past first crack for light and medium roasts, and
  found in his own colour-meter logs that ground colour followed drop temperature better than
  development time (taste correlation "not conclusive"). `conf: low` [S0024](../sources/S0024-home-barista-first-crack-temperature-changed.md) General filter-roast
  advice on a Skywalker thread: aim for 3–4 degrees (20–30 s) after first crack — more gives roast
  notes, less hay notes — and "roast to a colour" (oil on the bean means too dark); the poster does
  not say he owns a Skywalker [S0027](../sources/S0027-coffeesnobs-skywalker-roasters-thread.md).
- **Compare times, not just ratios.** Two roasts with the same dry and development times but a
  longer middle phase gave splits of 48/33/19 % and 40/43/16 % — the percentages hide that only one
  phase changed. `conf: low` [S0028](../sources/S0028-home-barista-roasting-phase-ratios.md)
- **Stretching to hit a %.** Slowing RoR below ~10 °F/min at first crack to reach 20 % kept the drop
  light but often still tasted under-developed; shorter, hotter development tasted better for that
  roaster. Holding airflow high mattered for short, hot finishes. `conf: low` [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md) [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md)
- **Short development shifts flavour**, not just "under-develops": a Gesha moved from a 3.5 to a 2-min
  finish turned sweet and acidic but lost its floral and spice notes [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md); consistent with the
  research above [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md). Short finishes worked on washed Kenyas if the final temperature was
  reasonable, but a 45 s finish on a mediocre coffee tasted grainy [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md). `conf: low`
- **When does development start?** Mark first crack at regular successive pops, not the first
  isolated ones; stopping right at the first pops "will likely taste very grainy and possibly
  astringent". `conf: low` [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md) [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md)
- **DTR for reference:** in the study above, the fast roast had DTR ≈ 14 % and the longest
  ≈ 41 % (computed from the reported times). [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)

## On the Skywalker (IR)

- **Your data (P, n=41):** development has drifted from ~1:37 (DTR 16.5 %) early in the year to
  ~1:00 (DTR ~9 %) since June — shorter than the shortest roast in the studies above. On your
  machine, development time tracks weight loss closely (r = 0.72). See
  [findings](../skywalker/findings.md).

- **Your drop relative to first crack (P, n=41):** median **+8.3 °C** above first-crack reading
  (range 5.6–22.4 °C); four roasts (id4, id19, id31, id32) ended under 6 °C above. It moves with
  development time (r = 0.78). id39 — the cupped roast (0:41, sharp, very acidic, bitter) — ended
  **+6.2 °C**; id40 (same coffee, 1:12) ended +12.0 °C. N3Roaster's ~5.6 °C threshold [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md) comes
  from other equipment, probe and first-crack convention, so it cannot be applied directly.
  **Inference — test it:** your shortest roasts may be ending close to such a threshold; see
  [experiment 2](../skywalker/experiments.md).
- **A V2 owner's light recipe:** targets 85–100 s development (DTR 16–18 %, drop BT 192–194 °C,
  weight loss 12–13 %, 250 g); asked how it tasted he answered "nice smooth acidity and fruit"
  (not stated to be the same roast) [S0027](../sources/S0027-coffeesnobs-skywalker-roasters-thread.md). Hoos' own fruit-forward recipe also sits at 1:00–1:10
  [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md). The V2 owner adds that light roasts on these machines look darker than their degree, so
  trust development time, weight loss and drop temperature over colour [S0027](../sources/S0027-coffeesnobs-skywalker-roasters-thread.md).
- **Other Skywalker owners:** renatoa's best roast of his first ~15 (a Kenya, 250 g) had 18 % DTR,
  ~1:35 development, first crack 181 °C → drop 190 °C [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md). His accidental ~45 s (10 % DTR)
  development with a flat RoR still cupped well, with no cardboard/baked taste — one anecdote
  [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md). Factory auto modes drop at a fixed temperature, so development varied from ~40 s to
  ~2 min with first crack at 191 vs 184 °C [S0016](../sources/S0016-homeroasters-skywalker-main-thread.md). Auto roasts often tasted under-developed
  (flat, vegetal; "green nasty acidic") [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md) [S0016](../sources/S0016-homeroasters-skywalker-main-thread.md).
- **Low momentum at first crack.** Skywalker owners enter first crack at modest RoR (renatoa
  6–7 °C/min [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md); you ~9 °C/min). **Inference — test it:** by the momentum argument above
  [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md), the same development time does less here than on a hot gas roast — so your ~1:00 may be
  "shorter" than it looks.
- **Steering development is limited:** temperature holds after a heater cut (182 °C for 30 s) and
  with the fan already at 90 % there's little air margin [S0017](../sources/S0017-homeroasters-skywalker-mods-thread.md). Plan development before first
  crack.
- The *direction* of these effects is chemistry and should hold on any heat source.
  **Inference — test it.**
- The *numbers* won't transfer: first crack reads ≈ 182 °C on the Skywalker [S0001](../sources/S0001-skywiki-infrared-roaster.md), far below
  the ≈ 200 °C drop temperatures in the study, and readings shift with batch size [S0003](../sources/S0003-skywiki-renatoa-words-of-wisdom.md). Use
  time after first crack, weight loss and colour, not imported temperatures.
- Your roast pages in [My Skywalker](../skywalker/roast-log.md) report development time and DTR
  automatically.

## Contested
**Is a 20–25 % DTR needed for a well-developed roast?**

- **Yes (Rao, 2014):** every great batch in thousands he cupped had first crack at 75–80 % of total
  time; a DTR of 20–25 % is a prerequisite (not a guarantee); development changed out of proportion
  to total time tends to taste baked and lose sweetness. Grade C, many commercial machines. [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md)
- **No, not for light roasts:** experienced light roasters sit around 13–16 % or 1:20–1:30 [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md);
  "20 % dev is ... a good starting spot but not gospel" — the firmer rule is not to exceed 25 %
  [S0020](../sources/S0020-home-barista-bean-temperature-at-first-crack.md); Nordic roasters used ~45 s [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md); a ~10 % DTR roast tasted fine on a Skywalker (one anecdote) [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md).
  Grades C–B. The research's shortest roast (1:30, ~14 % DTR) was the sweetest and brightest at equal
  colour [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md).

**Fixed seconds or a temperature after first crack?**

- **Seconds:** treat first crack as a marker and count development time; FC temperature varies by
  bean [S0024](../sources/S0024-home-barista-first-crack-temperature-changed.md) (Theophilus, CarefreeBuzzBuzz). Grade C.
- **Temperature:** drop by absolute bean temperature and manage the time to get there [S0024](../sources/S0024-home-barista-first-crack-temperature-changed.md)
  (Nunas); N3Roaster's threshold is a temperature effect independent of time [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md). Grade C.
- **Both:** EddyQ marks first crack at a set temperature when he cannot hear it, drops by time, and
  notes ground colour tracked drop temperature [S0024](../sources/S0024-home-barista-first-crack-temperature-changed.md). Nobody shows cup data for either strategy.

Rao says he rarely cups roasts dropped well before the end of first crack and doesn't assume the
ratio holds for them [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md). Not settled for very light roasts — see [experiment 2](../skywalker/experiments.md).
