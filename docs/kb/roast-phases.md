# Roast phases

!!! abstract "In short"
    - A roast is usually split into **drying** (charge → yellowing), **browning/Maillard** (yellowing → first crack) and **development** (first crack → drop).
    - At the **same colour**, development time changes flavour more than time to first crack. `conf: medium`
    - Practitioners still stress the early phases: a roast that rushes through drying/browning can taste worse. **Some tension** between sources.
    - Plan with **checkpoints** (time + temperature + RoR at yellow and first crack).
    - **Hacea's four-phase scheme** (drying → Maillard → caramelization → post-crack, with first crack fixed at a 385 °F mark) is a practitioner framework with no cup data behind it. Its marks translate to roughly 137 / 160 / 185 °C on your probe. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum`
    - Stretching the *drying* stage by ~35 s on a sample roaster gave a sweeter, more muted cup; shortening it by ~38 s gave a tart one with a short finish. `conf: low`

## Concepts
- **Phase timing vs development.** Across eight sensory studies, at equal colour, development time
  affected every attribute measured while time to first crack affected only some; colour (roast
  degree) was the strongest predictor of all. `conf: medium` [S0012](../sources/S0012-munchow-2020-roasting-conditions-multi-study.md) [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)
- **Early phases matter (practitioner view).** Of two espresso roasts, the one that went fast
  through the first two phases had low body and sweetness and a dry, tart, bitter finish compared
  with a more gradual roast; many roasters "aim for first crack, then think about development".
  `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum` [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md)
- **Matching phases across machines.** Coffees roasted on three machines with different probes
  tasted the same when phase timings (and end colour) matched — curve shapes didn't. `conf: low` [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md)
- **What first crack is.** Mainly steam pressure from the bean's ~10–12 % residual moisture (not
  the only driver); the bean swells, the crease opens and chaff is released; the roast turns from
  endothermic to exothermic. Cues besides sound: patchy colour, rounder edges, grain/bread smell.
  `conf: low` [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md)
- **Is moisture the whole story of first crack? Probably not.** A forum test roasted dehydrated green beans — no free water left to drive out — and still heard audible cracks at the same temperature as normal green. `conf: low` `ctx: IKAWA, fluid bed, one test` [S0031](../sources/S0031-home-barista-add-moisture-during-a-roast.md)
- **Time to first crack has no "correct" value** — it depends on heat-transfer type, batch size, the
  coffee and ambient: ~3–7 min in small air roasters, 10–12 in a Behmor, 9–12 in 5–12 kg shop drums.
  [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md)
- **The four-phase scheme (Hacea, 1.5 kg gas drum).** Phases run by bean temperature: drying to 300 °F (149 °C; adj. ≈ 137 °C), Maillard 301–340 °F (to 171 °C; adj. ≈ 160 °C), caramelization from 340 °F (it can start anywhere from 320 to 356 °F depending on the sugars) to a first-crack mark fixed at 385 °F (196 °C; adj. ≈ 185 °C), then post-crack development. The sample kit used marks of 305 / 350 / 385 °F (152 / 177 / 196 °C; adj. ≈ 140 / 165 / 185 °C). Time in each phase is the roadmap and heat sets the speed. Claimed flavour links: a short Maillard keeps brighter, more complex acidity (longer time at 300–350 °F goes with less complex acidity and aromatics); a longer caramelization adds sweetness and body but lowers acidity; a long post-crack adds bitterness; florals come from intense early heat and seem to be lost as body (melanoidins) builds. The articles give no cupping data. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum` [S0039](../sources/S0039-hacea-roast-phases-for-profile-development.md) [S0038](../sources/S0038-home-barista-hacea-coffees-roast-philosophy.md) [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md)
- **Matching the scheme on another roaster.** Line up *seconds in each phase*; if your first crack sits at another temperature, rescale the marks proportionally rather than copying them. One member reproduced the kit on a Quest M3s (91 g), where first crack read 10 degrees higher than Hacea's, so his roasts came out slightly less developed. `conf: low` [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md)
- **A skeptic's point:** Maillard continues to the end of the roast, so a short "Maillard phase" may not be a lever at all; first crack is still useful as a probe check and as the switch to development. `conf: low` [S0038](../sources/S0038-home-barista-hacea-coffees-roast-philosophy.md)
- **Stretching one stage at a time (Sweet Maria's, Probat PRE-1Z, Rwanda Bourbon).** With first-crack length (1:19–1:25) and the 30 s after the end of crack unchanged, the drying stage was lengthened or shortened so total time moved by about +34 s (10:10) and −38 s (8:58) against the 9:36 control (calculated from the printed times). The control was the most common panel favourite; the long roast had muted but balanced acidity and a long, syrupy, candy-like sweetness; the short one was tart with a short finish. Pour-over drinkers preferred the shorter roasts across the whole three-part series. `conf: low` (grade B, one coffee, informal panels) `ctx: electric drum, sample roaster` [S0035](../sources/S0035-sweet-marias-stretchin-out-the-roast-pt3.md)

## In practice
- **Checkpoints (example, not a target):** Cooper's milk-espresso profile on a 10 kg drum: yellow at
  5–6 min, ≥ 4 min yellow → first crack, 3:00–3:30 first crack → drop. [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md)
- **Log phase durations** for every roast and compare like with like.
- **Priority order** (from the research): get roast degree right first, then tune development,
  then early phases. **Inference** from [S0012](../sources/S0012-munchow-2020-roasting-conditions-multi-study.md).

## On the Skywalker (IR)

  Maillard and development durations. Your medians: drying 4:40, Maillard 4:06, development 1:08
  ([findings](../skywalker/findings.md)).
- Phase *durations* transfer between machines better than temperatures; yellowing at "160–165 °C" on a drum (adj. ≈ 145–150 °C) won't match the Skywalker's lower-reading probe [S0001](../sources/S0001-skywiki-infrared-roaster.md) [S0009](../sources/S0009-skywiki-putrid-upstairs-response.md).

- **Judging dry end under IR.** Owners find beans seem to go from green to brown with little
  in between. Call dry end when the last green/silver hue is gone but before yellow, or by smell
  (fresh grass → hay; one owner notices "sweet corn" at ~140–150 °C). Clear yellow shows at
  ~150 °C when first crack is ~181–182 °C. `conf: low` [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md) [S0016](../sources/S0016-homeroasters-skywalker-main-thread.md)
- **renatoa's operating rules for a Skywalker** (grade C, version and batch not stated): in drying, a RoR marker that dips and stays down for more than 5 s means add power; in browning, one that rises means less power or more air. Dry-end target 3:30–4:30 from charge; one example schedule is 65 → 70 → 75 % power, then 70 % with more air, dry end near 4:00. His add-on's dry-end estimate is reliable after minute 2 with a 5–10 s error. Other owners see dry end at nearly 6 min, and one slow case ended drying past minute 5 at 90 % power — he suggests checking real wattage. `conf: low` [S0029](../sources/S0029-homeroasters-roast-assistant-for-skywalker.md)
- **Your dry end (P, n=41):** median 4:40, range 3:55–7:00; 12 of 41 roasts ended drying by 4:30 and 29 later, so most of your roasts sit outside renatoa's window. **Inference — test it:** whether a dry end nearer 4:00 changes the cup is unknown; the Probat result [S0035](../sources/S0035-sweet-marias-stretchin-out-the-roast-pt3.md) says a 30–40 s change in the drying stage was audible on another machine, which makes it worth a single-variable test.
- **Inference — test it:** the Hacea marks adjusted to your probe (≈ 137 / 160 / 185 °C) put the end of drying about 8 °C below your typical dry-end reading (~145 °C) and the last mark right on your median first crack (184.5 °C). Treat that as a rough checkpoint grid, not a recipe: the Proaster-to-Skywalker shift is an estimate (±4 °C, see [Temperatures](temperature-adjustment.md)).

## Contested
**How much do pre-first-crack phases matter?** Sensory research: less than development at equal
colour, but not zero [S0012](../sources/S0012-munchow-2020-roasting-conditions-multi-study.md). Practitioner experience: rushing early phases hurts body and
sweetness [S0011](../sources/S0011-cooper-cropster-rate-of-rise-production.md). Not strictly contradictory — the practitioner example may also differ in
development and colour. Other practitioners also put weight on late Maillard (closing the
inner/outer temperature gap there [S0020](../sources/S0020-home-barista-bean-temperature-at-first-crack.md); development "takes place in Maillard, too" [S0021](../sources/S0021-home-barista-development-time-light-roasts.md)), and
one Nordic roaster chose a longer drying phase for a dry, lower-quality Kenya [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md). On an IKAWA a longer browning phase went with less vegetal and astringent character [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md), and mikelipino extends the browning phase between dry end and first crack to avoid raw, vegetal light roasts [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md). Counter-note: one roaster measured fast internal response on a fluid bed, doubting large internal gradients [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md). Open.

**Newer evidence on the early phases.** The one controlled stage-by-stage test found a ±35 s change in drying audible in the cup [S0035](../sources/S0035-sweet-marias-stretchin-out-the-roast-pt3.md), which supports the practitioner side, but it changed total time as well, and a Gayo study that fixed drying and Maillard at 4:00 each says nothing about them [S0037](../sources/S0037-fadhil-2025-gayo-arabica-development-phase-roasting-time.md). Hacea's own stance — drying and Maillard time are levers for acidity and body — rests on tasting notes, not data [S0039](../sources/S0039-hacea-roast-phases-for-profile-development.md) [S0038](../sources/S0038-home-barista-hacea-coffees-roast-philosophy.md).

## Phase ratios
- Percentages hide which phase changed: equal dry and development times with a longer middle phase
  gave 48/33/19 % and 40/43/16 % splits. Track durations as well. `conf: low` [S0028](../sources/S0028-home-barista-roasting-phase-ratios.md) Forum
  rules of thumb: 45-35-20 % phase split and 17-11-5.6 °C/min RoR checkpoints (stated as 30-20-10 °F/min) [S0028](../sources/S0028-home-barista-roasting-phase-ratios.md). `ctx: gas drum`
