# Resting & degassing

!!! abstract "In short"
    - Fresh roasted coffee holds CO₂ (about 1–2 % of its weight) and releases it for a very long time — a light roast followed for 800 h had lost 3.4 mg/g and was still losing about 2 µg/g/h. `conf: medium` `ctx: gas-drum, Arabica`
    - **Darker roasts hold and release much more gas** than light ones: whole-bean losses were ~2.5–2.8 mg/g (light), 4.7–6.6 (medium), 8.5–11.9 (dark). `conf: medium`
    - **Roast speed matters at medium and dark degrees** (faster roasts degas faster and more) **but hardly at all at light degrees.** `conf: medium`
    - **Ground coffee loses most of its gas at once:** up to 75 % of the trapped gas leaves in the first 90 s after grinding. `conf: medium`
    - The degassing paper does not link gas release to taste. How long to rest is practitioner opinion: "at least a week" for light samples, 4–6 weeks or more for very light roasts, and one roaster who "wouldn't worry too much". **Contested.** `conf: low`

## Concepts

- **What degasses.** Roasting makes CO₂ that is partly kept inside the bean; the paper cites roughly 1–2 % of the weight of freshly roasted coffee. [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md)
- **How the study measured it.** A sealed container with a capillary outlet on a semi-micro balance showed the mass lost over hundreds of hours. Whole Arabica beans came from nine roast profiles (three degrees × three speeds, 1 kg gas-fired Probatino; speed set by burner gas flow: fast 4–5 min, medium 9–10, slow 11–12 min), ground Arabica and Robusta from a 100 g Probat sample roaster. Replicates differed by ~2 %. Roast temperatures and first-crack readings are not reported. `conf: medium` (grade A, one lab, gas-drum) [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md)
- **Whole beans, by degree and speed (mg/g lost):** light 2.7 slow / 2.5 medium / 2.8 fast; medium 4.7 / 5.6 / 6.6; dark 8.5 / 10.0 / 11.9. The fitted total release over infinite time was 3.4–3.7 mg/g for light, 5.7–7.3 for medium and 10.2–12.7 for dark roasts. After 400 h the residual rate was 3.1–9.2 µg/g/h. [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md)
- **It is slow.** A light roast followed for 800 h had released 3.4 mg/g in total and was still releasing ~2 µg/g/h; the total at infinite time is unknown. [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md)
- **Ground coffee.** Up to ~3.0 mg/g (Arabica) and ~3.9 mg/g (Robusta) was released, up to 75 % of it during and right after grinding (first 90 s). Release rose from light to medium and then levelled off toward dark. [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md)

## In practice

- **Rest advice is opinion.** From a tasting-kit thread: "at least a week" for these light samples so the beans can degas; very light roasts "tend to be best 4–6 weeks in"; Nordic roasts dropped less than 30 s into first crack "need a month or more"; and the kit's designer "wouldn't worry too much about the rest time" for the exercise. Informal, a handful of home roasters. `conf: low` [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md)
- **One tasting at 18 days off roast** (espresso, Flair, 12 g doses): a steam-assisted roast gave "mild acidity, cola notes" and the unassisted one tasted "more muted"; a repeat was planned for 2–3 weeks later. `conf: low` [S0031](../sources/S0031-home-barista-add-moisture-during-a-roast.md)
- **Compare samples at the same rest.** Gas release and flavour change over days to weeks, so cup experimental roasts on the same day after the same rest. **Inference — test it.**
- **Grind just before brewing.** Most of the ground coffee's trapped gas leaves in the first minutes [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md); the paper says nothing about flavour, so how much this matters is untested here.

## On the Skywalker (IR)

- Your roasts are light (median development 1:08, weight loss 13.4 %). By the lab data, light roasts degas little and roast speed barely changes it, so don't expect a faster or slower Skywalker roast to need a different rest. **Inference — test it:** the data are from a gas drum; no lamp-roaster data exist.
- Your one cupped roast (id39, Costa Rican Natural, 0:41 development) was tasted after 5 days. The forum range for light roasts is a week to several weeks, so part of "unpleasant bitterness, very acidic when cooled, no clear flavour" may be rest rather than roast. The sources do not show that; it is a reason to re-cup the same coffee at several rests — see [experiment 2](../skywalker/experiments.md). **Inference — test it.**

## Contested

**How long should light roasts rest?**

- **A week or more, up to 4–6 weeks for very light roasts:** [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md) (flamenkito, SutterMill).
- **Don't worry too much:** the kit's designer thinks it matters little for her cupping exercise [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md).
- Neither side shows cup data; the degassing paper deliberately stays out of taste [S0042](../sources/S0042-smrke-2018-time-resolved-gravimetric-degassing.md).
