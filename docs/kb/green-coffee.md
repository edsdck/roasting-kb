# Green coffee

!!! abstract "In short"
    - **Density** is a better guide than altitude: denser beans take heat slowly at first, then transfer it efficiently, tolerate more heat, and hold more water. `conf: low`
    - Measure it cheaply (weigh a fixed volume) and log it with each roast.
    - Let the flavour you want drive the profile; density only changes *how* you drive the roaster.
    - First crack is driven mainly by steam from the bean's ~10–12 % moisture; older/drier coffees crack louder, some naturals crack very quietly. `conf: low`
    - Processing changes how coffees are roasted (the Skywalker's own presets differ by natural/washed/honey), but whether it changes the first-crack *temperature* is **contested**.
    - **Roast weight loss ≈ green moisture + roast degree.** Green coffee arrives at ~10–12 % moisture; home roasters log 11–15 % loss (most 13–14 %), ~9–11 % for very light roasts, 15–16 % or more for dark. Compare weight loss only within the same lot. `conf: medium`
    - Two reports of coffees that tasted green or astringent on standard gas-drum profiles (a dense Rwandan natural and its decaf) were fixed by restricting airflow and, for the decaf, a lower-heat "soak" — one coffee each, mechanism untested. `conf: low`
    - **Two more practitioner loss tables agree with the home logs:** Hoos calls 11–13 % light and 14–16 % medium and distrusts under 11 % [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md); Sweet Maria's averages put City at 12.7 %, City+ at 13.3 % and Full City at 14.5 % [S0062](../sources/S0062-sweet-marias-how-to-calculate-weight-loss.md). But loss alone does not tell good from bad: a baked, a balanced and a grassy roast of one lot lost 14.8 %, 15.0 % and 13.0 % [S0051](../sources/S0051-cho-2023-firescope-light-roast-colombian-washed-2.md). `conf: low`
    - **In a 46-lot volatile study, roast level changed the aroma chemistry most, then origin, then process;** hexanal (a raw-note candidate) was higher in washed green coffee [S0064](../sources/S0064-vezzulli-2023-volatile-compounds-origin-process-roast-level.md). `conf: medium` `ctx: IKAWA 50 g, no sensory data`

## Concepts
- **Density and elevation.** Higher-grown coffee ripens slower and tends to be denser — e.g.
  687.5 kg/m³ at 1,400–1,500 m vs 619.75 kg/m³ at 800–900 m in a Nepal study — but latitude and
  climate make elevation alone unreliable. `conf: low` [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Heat behaviour.** Dense beans absorb energy slowly at first, then transfer heat internally more
  efficiently; they also tend to contain more water, which can cause a stall approaching first
  crack. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum` [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Defect susceptibility.** Bean shape, density and growing altitude affect how easily beans tip.
  [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)
- **Moisture and first crack.** Green coffee holds roughly 10–12 % moisture; first crack is mainly
  steam pressure from it. Older or drier coffee, and less dense (often lower-grown) coffee, can
  crack louder; some high-grown dry-process (natural) coffees have a very quiet, short first crack.
  `conf: low` [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md)
- **Processing and first-crack temperature.** On the same machine, some roasters see first crack at
  the same reading for all beans; others find it bean-dependent. A decaf cracked late (~189 °C) on a
  Skywalker. **Contested** — see [Measurement § Contested](measurement-and-artisan.md#contested).
  [S0020](../sources/S0020-home-barista-bean-temperature-at-first-crack.md) [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md) [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md)
- **Coffee type and very short development.** Nordic roasters found Kenyans respond well to very
  short, hot roasts but Brazilians wouldn't [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md); one home roaster found dry-processed coffees did
  better with shorter development [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md); another says only very good coffees "shine" with short
  light roasts [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md). `conf: low`
- **Roast loss = moisture + roast degree.** An importer's working formula: very light roasts dropped at first-crack start lose about 10 % of green weight, roasts well past second crack up to ~25 %; his examples are 12 / 15 / 20 % for light / medium / dark, and a higher-moisture lot gives roughly 4 % less roasted product at the same degree (lot moistures not given). `conf: low` (illustrative numbers, no measurement) [S0030](../sources/S0030-royal-coffee-understanding-roast-yield-and-loss.md). Home roasters' own logs agree: 11–15 % typical, most about 13–14 %, 15–16 % for darker roasts, 9–11 % for very light ones. `conf: medium` (several independent logs, anecdotal, machines mostly unstated) [S0032](../sources/S0032-home-barista-green-coffee-weight-loss.md). One roaster oven-dried his greens to about 9.6 % moisture [S0031](../sources/S0031-home-barista-add-moisture-during-a-roast.md).
- **Moisture keeps leaving through first crack, and the heating rate steers it.** IKAWA humidity-sensor curves show beans releasing water throughout first crack (only ~20 % of the humidity the sensor saw came from the beans; the rest was room air). `conf: low` `ctx: IKAWA, fluid bed` [S0031](../sources/S0031-home-barista-add-moisture-during-a-roast.md).
- **Adding steam changed almost nothing.** Steam (~26 g/min) injected into a Quest M3s intake raised weight loss by 0.4 percentage points at a pre-crack drop, needed 50–100 W more after 3 minutes on an 800 W baseline, gave identical loss (2.7 %) at a 300 °F drop, and made at most a slight fruitiness difference at 18 days off roast that other members put down to roast variation. `conf: low` `ctx: electric drum, Quest M3s` [S0031](../sources/S0031-home-barista-add-moisture-during-a-roast.md).
- **Weight-loss bands from two practitioner sources.** Hoos: loss = (start − end) ÷ start; most of it is water, then CO₂ (about 70 : 30 of the degradation products, quoted from an ETH Zurich thesis); specialty green holds 8–12 % moisture and roasted coffee under 3 %. His own bands: under 11 % suspect (under-roasted or under-developed), 11–13 % light, 14–16 % medium, 17–18 % dark, 19–21 % very dark, over 22 % avoid ("a grain of salt"); a drier lot loses less for the same roast (10 % moisture at 12 % loss ≈ 9.5 % moisture at 11.5 %) [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md). Sweet Maria's averages for air and drum roasters give first crack 10.3 %, City− 11.5 %, City 12.7 %, City+ 13.3 %, Full City 14.5 %, Full City+ 15.1 %, French 15.6 %, and warn that a 9 % moisture lot taken to 15 % loss ends up darker than expected, because the same loss means a different roast on a different moisture [S0062](../sources/S0062-sweet-marias-how-to-calculate-weight-loss.md). The two sets broadly agree with each other and with the home logs above; their temperatures are shop-probe values and do not map onto a Skywalker probe. `conf: low` (practitioner tables, no stated sample)
- **A repeatability rule.** Hoos treats a loss 0.5 percentage points or more from the baseline of the same coffee and method as a sign something went wrong, and says such batches were also off on colour in his QC [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md). At 350 g that is 1.75 g (derived). A commenter on the Sweet Maria's page notes chaff leaves with the moisture and inflates the apparent loss [S0062](../sources/S0062-sweet-marias-how-to-calculate-weight-loss.md). `conf: low`
- **Origin outranks process in the volatiles (46 specialty lots, IKAWA 50 g, GC-MS).** In green coffee origin separated lots better than process, and hexanal was higher in washed lots (p < 0.05). After roasting at three levels (light 205 °C in 5.46 min, medium 210 °C in 6.16 min, dark 214 °C in 6.46 min on the IKAWA's own sensor), differences came first from roast level, then origin, then process; the lightest roast kept the most of the green coffee's fermentation and drying signature. The authors say the link between green and roasted volatiles still needs study; no sensory or colour data. `conf: medium` (grade A, one lab, small sample roaster) `ctx: IKAWA fluid bed, 50 g` [S0064](../sources/S0064-vezzulli-2023-volatile-compounds-origin-process-roast-level.md)
- **Process may set the finish (recipe sources).** A 2026 tasting report proposes a post-first-crack finish of "+4–5 degrees" in 1:20 for washed, 1:00 for honey, 45 s–1:00 for natural and 45 s for experimental lots (degrees on the roaster's own probe, unit not stated) [S0057](../sources/S0057-cooper-2026-standards-and-the-mother-sauce.md); the same author's chart pairs a natural with 1:00 and a washed coffee with 1:30 of development [S0063](../sources/S0063-cooper-eqmr-roast-level-flavour-profile-strategies-chart.md). Hoos's fruit-forward natural also runs shorter [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md). An informal panel preferred the *longest* of three profiles for a natural [S0036](../sources/S0036-home-barista-hacea-roast-approach-tasting-kit.md). Open — see [Myths & contested](myths-and-contested.md). `conf: low`
- **Low starting moisture is easier to control** — a conclusion of a single-bean heating model, not a test [S0033](../sources/S0033-bustos-vanegas-2025-bean-heating-model-heat-generation.md). `conf: low`
- **Water for Maillard (practitioner theory).** The Hacea writer says green holds 9–12 % moisture and Maillard needs about 6–7 % left [S0039](../sources/S0039-hacea-roast-phases-for-profile-development.md) (`conf: low`, a general statement without data). His Rwandan natural (11.8 % moisture, density 1.15 g/ml, normally 12–13 % loss) lost over 15 % on four standard profiles and tasted green, tart and sweet-less; restricting airflow for the whole roast cut loss to 14.5 % and gave a balanced, fruit-forward cup. His explanation — too much water lost in drying, too little left for Maillard — is untested. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum, 400 g in a 1.5 kg drum, one coffee` [S0040](../sources/S0040-hacea-roast-approach-rwanda-muhororo-natural.md).
- **Denser (decaf) seed.** The Swiss Water decaf of that lot (density 1.15 → 1.20 g/ml, moisture 11.8 → 11.6 %) was astringent and sweet-less on all four standard profiles and at three charge temperatures. Three roasts that combined a "soak" (lower early heat, 25–50 % starting gas) with restricted airflow all removed the astringency; the one with the least soak was clearly best. Three changes at once, so which one mattered is unknown. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum, 400 g, one coffee` [S0041](../sources/S0041-hacea-roast-approach-swiss-water-rwanda-muhororo.md).

## In practice
- **Dense coffee:** higher charge, then more energy after colour change; keep heat up into first
  crack to avoid a stall; can take more heat in development, possibly a slightly higher drop.
  `conf: low` [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Lower-density coffee** aimed at sweetness and body: gentler, slower heat, longer browning
  (Maillard), possibly darker. `conf: low` [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Flavour first.** Even if a dense coffee *can* take more heat, the desired cup decides the
  profile. [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Moisture sets how hard to push and how much weight to lose.** Per Rob Hoos: wetter coffee is
  harder to push heat into (water resists heat uptake after boiling point), so it needs more energy
  in the middle of the roast, shows a slightly higher turning point and can seem to run away early
  before it lags; drier coffee may need less. For light roasts he aims for weight loss ~1–2 % above
  the green moisture (10 % moisture → 11–12 %; 12 % → 13–14 %). `conf: low` `ctx: drum` [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md)
- **Cultivar and processing set the timings.** Hoos sets roast length, development time and
  first-crack time from cultivar and processing (naturals: slightly shorter development in his
  fruit-forward recipe), and heat application from moisture and density. Typica and Ethiopian-type
  cultivars prefer slightly faster roasts, Bourbon types slower; "fast" is a trend, not a rule.
  `conf: low` [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md)
- **Measure:** weigh a fixed volume of green (measuring cylinder), log g/L. [S0014](../sources/S0014-lee-2025-bean-density-roasting.md)
- **Plan green from the loss.** Green needed = roasted needed ÷ (1 − loss as a decimal) [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md). **Inference — test it:** use your own within-coffee median loss for this, not a table's.
- **Compare weight loss only within the same lot.** The same colour gives a different loss for a wetter or drier lot, so another roaster's "13 %" is a starting point, not a target. **Inference — test it.**
- **A coffee that tastes green or astringent on your usual profile:** the two gas-drum cases above changed airflow and early heat; change one at a time and cup each [S0040](../sources/S0040-hacea-roast-approach-rwanda-muhororo-natural.md) [S0041](../sources/S0041-hacea-roast-approach-swiss-water-rwanda-muhororo.md).

## On the Skywalker (IR)
- **Inference — test it:** gas/airflow levers translate to lamp power (slow-acting) and fan
  (removes heat) [S0001](../sources/S0001-skywiki-infrared-roaster.md) [S0003](../sources/S0003-skywiki-renatoa-words-of-wisdom.md). For a dense coffee that means starting higher and making the
  "push" earlier than you would on gas, to beat the lamp lag.
- Start logging density with each roast — see [experiments](../skywalker/experiments.md).
- The factory auto profiles treat processing differently: preheat 200 °C (natural) / 220 °C
  (washed) / 215 °C (honey); start power 65 / 70 / 65 % [S0016](../sources/S0016-homeroasters-skywalker-main-thread.md).
- **Inference — test it:** your median weight loss is 13.4 % (n=28). On Hoos's rule that fits
  coffee with ~11.5–12.5 % moisture roasted light, but your green moisture is unknown — ask the
  supplier or use a meter before reading anything into it [S0025](../sources/S0025-roast-rebels-hoos-cultivars-airflow-interview.md). For scale: 13.4 % is inside the band most home roasters log (11–15 %, mostly 13–14 %) [S0032](../sources/S0032-home-barista-green-coffee-weight-loss.md) and well above the ~10 % of the very lightest drops [S0030](../sources/S0030-royal-coffee-understanding-roast-yield-and-loss.md), although your development is only ~1:00. Your log has no green moisture (density is logged once), so moisture and roast degree cannot be separated yet.
- **Your loss against the new tables (P).** Your median 13.4 % (range 12.0–15.9 %, n=28) sits between Hoos's "light" (11–13 %) and "medium" (14–16 %) and at Sweet Maria's City+ (13.3 %) [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md) [S0062](../sources/S0062-sweet-marias-how-to-calculate-weight-loss.md), while your development is only ~1:08, so loss is running at medium-light weight with a short finish — green moisture is the missing piece. Within a coffee the Ethiopian Washed ranges 12.3–14.5 % (n=9; SD 0.7 points) and the Honduras Honey 13.3–13.8 % (n=5); the first is wider than Hoos's 0.5-point limit [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md), but your development time varies too and loss follows it (r = 0.72), so this is not a scale-error estimate. **Inference — test it:** weigh to 1 g and hold development fixed before using 0.5 points as your repeatability bar.
- **Your data (P):** Honduras Honey cracks ~6 °C higher than your washed coffees — see
  [findings](../skywalker/findings.md).
- **Inference — test it:** if a dense or wet coffee tastes sweet-less on the standard recipe, the gas-drum fixes translate to a lower early power % and a lower fan before dry end ([experiment 7](../skywalker/experiments.md)). On a lamp roaster where air mostly cools, restricting airflow may not cut loss the way it did on the drum [S0040](../sources/S0040-hacea-roast-approach-rwanda-muhororo-natural.md).

## Open questions
- Measured moisture/density vs first-crack temperature and time — no data yet.
- Does processing change the first-crack temperature, or only how it's measured/heard?
  Roasters still split: same ±2 °C for all beans vs a new origin cracking 5–5.6 °C higher [S0024](../sources/S0024-home-barista-first-crack-temperature-changed.md).
- What is the green moisture of the coffees you roast (needed to use the weight-loss rule)?
- How radiant heating interacts with density (surface vs core).
- Do Hoos's loss bands [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md) line up with colour or cup for light roasts on a lamp roaster? Readers asked him how loss relates to Agtron and he did not answer on the page [S0059](../sources/S0059-hoos-why-take-weight-loss-percentage.md).
- Is hexanal in washed green coffee [S0064](../sources/S0064-vezzulli-2023-volatile-compounds-origin-process-roast-level.md) why some washed lots taste grassy at short development? The study does not test it.
- Does restricting airflow reduce roast loss and fix a green, sweet-less cup on an IR roaster, as on one gas drum? [S0040](../sources/S0040-hacea-roast-approach-rwanda-muhororo-natural.md)
