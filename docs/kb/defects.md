# Roast defects

!!! abstract "In short"
    - Two kinds: **objective** defects you can *see* on the bean (scorching, facing, tipping, chipping) and **subjective** ones you *taste* (under-/over-development, "roasty", baked). `conf: low` [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)
    - **Under-developed** → malty/nutty, aggressive acidity, metallic or vegetal. **Over-developed** → muted acidity, heavy, little nuance. Don't confuse with under-/over-*roasted* (end temperature too low/high). `conf: medium`
    - **"Baked"** has no agreed cause or even definition — RoR crash, flick, stalled temperature, or long development with a flat RoR are all proposed. **Contested.**
    - **Words:** Hoos keeps *underdeveloped / overdeveloped* for the time from first crack to drop and *under- / over-roasted* for end temperature, colour or weight loss, and no longer uses "baked" because it has become close to meaningless; he says subjective defect words mostly mean the taster did not like the cup [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md). `conf: low`
    - **Astringency can come from the green coffee, not only the roast:** immature beans (not only visible quakers) are described as the main source of astringent flavours; a relayed study puts ~10.8 % quakers by weight at the point a natural drops below 80 points [S0061](../sources/S0061-hoos-decoding-defects-immature.md). `conf: low`
    - On the Skywalker, surface defects come mainly from direct radiant heat, not hot drum metal — **Inference — test it.**

## Objective defects (visible)

| Defect | What you see | Usual cause | Avoid by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scorching** | Dark/black specks on the bean surface, usually before browning | Bean contact with metal that's too hot: charging too hot, too little airflow, too much early heat, thin drums; may not show in the finished roast but still touches the taste [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md) | Lower early heat/charge; keep beans moving [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) |
| **Facing** | Scorch on the flat face, late in the roast | Beans pressed against hot drum walls as the batch swells (overfilled drum), a drum speed that is too slow, or overheated metal late [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md) | Don't overfill; avoid overheating the drum late [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) [S0013](../sources/S0013-melheim-2023-optimal-batch-size.md) |
| **Tipping** | Charred spot at the germ end, usually early | Roasting too fast; bean shape, density and altitude affect susceptibility; described as heat damage to the endosperm cap and possibly the germ, with a burnt aftertaste [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md) | Gentler early heat [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) |
| **Chipping** | Small crater blown out of the bean | Only in dark roasts around second crack; cause unconfirmed | Little flavour impact [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) |
| **Charring** | Blackened beans | Excess heat late in the roast, by conduction or by convection [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md) | Less late heat |

`conf: low` — single practitioner source for most rows; larger batches raise the risk of facing,
tipping and scorching [S0013](../sources/S0013-melheim-2023-optimal-batch-size.md).

## Subjective defects (taste)

- **Under-development** — too short from first crack to drop: malty/nutty, aggressive acidity,
  metallic or vegetal; also described as grainy (dropping right at the first pops, or a 45 s finish
  on an ordinary coffee) [S0019](../sources/S0019-sweet-marias-first-crack-faq.md) [S0022](../sources/S0022-home-barista-nordic-roasters-forum-exercise.md) and flat/vegetal or "green nasty acidic" on Skywalker auto
  roasts [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md) [S0016](../sources/S0016-homeroasters-skywalker-main-thread.md). Different from *under-roasted* (end temperature too low).
  `conf: medium` [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) Consistent with research: shorter development = more acidity at equal
  colour [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md) [S0012](../sources/S0012-munchow-2020-roasting-conditions-multi-study.md).
- **Underdeveloped, baked and grassy in one practitioner's Colombian washed trio (1 kg, commercial roasters).** A baked, over-acidic roast lost 14.8 % (excess acidity like underripe orange, low sweetness and complexity, dry mouthfeel); a grassy roast on a second machine lost 13.0 % (grass and straw, floral, malic acidity, weak body); an adjusted first-machine roast with +10 % heat and a 20 °C lower initial temperature lost 15.0 % and was balanced (citric and malic acidity, strong body, red apple). His rules: underdeveloped flavours "usually emerge from insufficient airflow or inadequate heat", baked flavours from prolonged low-temperature roasting, too little heat, or airflow out of balance with the heat. Three roasts on two machines in different years, one taster, no times or temperatures in the text. Baked (14.8 %) and balanced (15.0 %) lost almost the same weight, so loss alone does not separate them. `conf: low` [S0051](../sources/S0051-cho-2023-firescope-light-roast-colombian-washed-2.md)
- **Volatile fingerprints of "under" and "over" (gas drum, chemistry only).** Three 5 kg roasts of one Guatemalan coffee, defined only by starting temperature (210 / 220 / 240 °C; in-roaster probe 180 / 200 / 240 °C): the "underdeveloped" one had the highest azine (46 %) and aldehyde (16 %) shares, the "standard" medium-light one the most acids (17 %) and esters, the "overdeveloped" one the most alcohols (15 %) and furans (12 %). No roast times, colour, weight loss or sensory scores in the parts read, so these are markers, not a recipe. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum, 5 kg` [S0050](../sources/S0050-rusinek-2022-roast-defects-volatile-compound-profile.md)
- **Vegetal / "raw" note and temperature.** One roaster finds a raw, vegetal flavour that
  disappears abruptly about 5.6 °C above first-crack start regardless of time; another finds a chlorophyll-like note below ~204–205 °C (adj. ≈ 191 °C) with first crack at ~198 °C. `conf: low` [S0023](../sources/S0023-home-barista-vegetal-flavor-first-crack.md) Ties
  under-development to *temperature reached*, not only seconds — see [Development](development.md).
- **Astringency.** A tongue-drying finish. Reported causes: a charge that is too cool (inside left
  under-developed while the outside hits the drop temperature) [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md); tipping, which infrared
  heating makes easy [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md); large power swings [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md); a plateau before first crack followed by a
  crash [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md); a short browning phase (more astringent and vegetal with a shorter one on an
  IKAWA) [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md). `conf: low` `ctx: drum / IR + hot air / fluid bed`; no controlled test.
- **Astringency from a dense or decaf seed (gas drum).** A Swiss Water decaf (density 1.20 g/ml) was astringent and sweet-less on every standard profile and at three charge temperatures; three roasts with a lower-heat "soak" plus longer-restricted airflow removed it, the least-soak one best. Three changes at once, so the cause is not isolated. `conf: low` `ctx: gas-drum, 400 g` [S0041](../sources/S0041-hacea-roast-approach-swiss-water-rwanda-muhororo.md) The Kaleido fix (a higher charge with lower power percentages) and this one (a gentler start plus less air) do not obviously point the same way — see [Myths & contested](myths-and-contested.md).
- **Immature beans and quakers (green-coffee cause).** Hoos's round-up quotes the SCAA handbook that immature beans are "the main source of astringent flavors", and relays Rabelo et al. 2021: about 10.8 % quakers by weight pushes a natural below 80 points, lower levels still perceptible. Every quaker was an immature bean but not every immature bean becomes a quaker, so a roast can be hurt without showing quakers. His cues in green: beans curved inward toward the centre cut, tight clinging silverskin, tight or empty centre cut — not necessarily small or very green. The study itself was not read. `conf: low` [S0061](../sources/S0061-hoos-decoding-defects-immature.md)
- **Over-development** — too long after first crack: low/muted acidity, heaviness, lack of nuance.
  Different from *over-roasted* (end temperature too high). `conf: medium` [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)
- **"Roasty"** — vague; usually means the taster disliked it; most often from too high an end
  temperature. `conf: low` [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)

## Baked

**What it tastes like** — sources agree on the description: dull, flat, hollow, bready/malty, less
sweet; Rao adds that it loses sweetness and tartness as the cup cools (easy to blame on the green).
`conf: medium` [S0008](../sources/S0008-rao-what-is-baked-coffee.md) [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)

**Definitions differ.** Matt Perger's, as forum members quote an article that is now gone: a lack of sweetness or dark-roast flavours in a coffee that is not dark; on his grid, dark and under-developed is "ashy, bitter", light and developed is bright and sweet. A stall has no taste of its own but usually leaves an under-developed, flat coffee; the usual mechanism quoted (Rao) is a stall letting sugar chains cross-link, which one member doubts applies before development. Second-hand, grade C. `conf: low` [S0044](../sources/S0044-home-barista-matt-perger-on-roast-development.md)

**A third camp drops the word.** Hoos no longer uses "baked" because it has become virtually meaningless, and splits the vocabulary into objective defects (visible) and subjective ones (the taster did not like it) [S0060](../sources/S0060-hoos-roasting-defects-vocabulary.md). Others say what they mean by naming the cause (below). `conf: low`

**What causes it — contested.** Proposed causes [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md):

1. A sharp RoR **crash** after first crack — Rao's main explanation; a crash in the last ~30 s does
   less harm `ctx: gas-drum` [S0008](../sources/S0008-rao-what-is-baked-coffee.md)
2. A large RoR **dip followed by a rise** (flick) [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)
3. Bean temperature **stalling** [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md)
4. **Long development (4+ min) with a flat RoR** [S0010](../sources/S0010-hoos-loring-avoiding-roasting-defects.md) — close to the research "Baked" roast:
   390 s development at equal colour, measurably more bitter/roasted/astringent [S0006](../sources/S0006-alstrup-2020-development-time-sensory.md)
5. **Development out of proportion to total roast time** — Rao's 2014 view: changing development
   time without regard to total time "often create[s] baked flavors and destroy[s] sweetness" [S0018](../sources/S0018-rao-2014-development-time-ratio.md)
6. A **first crack that stalls or stops** — one of five Rwanda roasts stalled in crack and tasted baked; the author's rule is never to let first crack stop or fall below where it began `ctx: electric drum, sample roaster` [S0034](../sources/S0034-sweet-marias-stretchin-out-the-roast-pt2.md)
7. **Long low-temperature roasting, too little heat, or airflow out of balance with the heat** — one practitioner's list for baked flavours in a Colombian washed light roast (14.8 % loss, over-acidic, low sweetness) [S0051](../sources/S0051-cho-2023-firescope-light-roast-colombian-washed-2.md)

Against the flat-RoR explanations: on a Skywalker, ~30 s of flat (zero) RoR during a ~45 s
development produced no cardboard/baked taste in one owner's cup [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md) — one anecdote.

CoffeeMind argues the defect categories themselves are too blunt; ask "how do I want it to taste?"
and experiment at equal colour [S0005](../sources/S0005-coffeemind-rate-of-rise-bad-reference.md). See [Rate of rise § Contested](rate-of-rise.md#contested).

### How to learn to taste it
- Cup blind, write notes, *then* look at the curves [S0008](../sources/S0008-rao-what-is-baked-coffee.md).
- Isolate one variable and keep colour equal, or colour will decide the result [S0005](../sources/S0005-coffeemind-rate-of-rise-bad-reference.md) [S0008](../sources/S0008-rao-what-is-baked-coffee.md).

## On the Skywalker (IR)
- **Inference — test it:** before changing the profile for an astringent or green cup, sort a roasted batch for pale, flat, wrinkled or inward-curved beans [S0061](../sources/S0061-hoos-decoding-defects-immature.md). At 350 g, 10.8 % is about 38 g of quakers (derived), so a visible handful is worth separating and tasting without before you blame heat. The pale-bean cue is Hoos's, from green coffee, not from lamp roasts.
- **Inference — test it:** the visible defects in the table are explained by hot *metal*; on the
  Skywalker the lamp heats bean surfaces directly [S0001](../sources/S0001-skywiki-infrared-roaster.md), so scorching/tipping risk is more likely
  tied to high lamp power early, especially with small batches. Check beans from fast/hot roasts
  for specks and dark tips.
- **Inference — test it:** cause #4 (long development + flat RoR) is the one most relevant here,
  because a flat RoR is normal on a radiant roaster [S0002](../sources/S0002-skywiki-convection-vs-radiant.md). Keep development moderate when RoR is
  flat and taste for flatness.
- At 350 g you're well below a level where facing from an overfilled drum is likely. [S0009](../sources/S0009-skywiki-putrid-upstairs-response.md) [S0013](../sources/S0013-melheim-2023-optimal-batch-size.md)
- **Charred ("charcoal") beans:** a few completely black beans per roast are common on Skywalkers
  (3–4 per 250 g was normal for one owner). They come from beans thrown up to lamp level; a
  slightly faster drum motor raised his count to 8. Pick them out after each roast. `conf: low` [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md)
- **Astringent roasts on an IR machine** (Kaleido, not a Skywalker): same colour and weight loss
  as an excellent Kaffelogic roast, but astringent; fixed by a higher charge, lower power
  percentages and gradual stepwise cuts [S0026](../sources/S0026-home-barista-kaleido-m10-astringent-roasts.md). **Inference — test it:** if a Skywalker roast tastes
  astringent or "done", suspect fast hot early power (tipping) and cool charges before blaming
  development time. `conf: low`
- **Colour can mislead on lamp roasters.** A V2 owner says light roasts "don't come out looking
  light" and advises trusting development time, weight loss and drop temperature over colour
  [S0027](../sources/S0027-coffeesnobs-skywalker-roasters-thread.md); compare whole-bean vs ground colour as suggested below.
- **Dark outside, under-developed inside:** an experienced owner's fastest (~7 min) roasts looked
  darker than average but were under-developed inside, which he links to surface heating by the
  lamp [S0015](../sources/S0015-homeroasters-skywalker-roasts-thread.md) [S0017](../sources/S0017-homeroasters-skywalker-mods-thread.md). `conf: low` **Inference — test it:** compare whole-bean vs ground colour on
  your fastest roasts.
